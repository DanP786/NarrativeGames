# Seraphina

- Apparent age: 24
- Actual age: 42
- Nature: vampire
- Current allegiance: Lucien Veyne and the emerging House Veyne; formerly the Pale Court
- Appearance: honey-blonde hair, blue eyes, small build suited to agility rather than strength. She enjoys striking clothing and makeup and can use cosmetics to lend her skin a more living flush.
- Temperament: quiet and observant, increasingly willing to tease and challenge Lucien within their private relationship.
- Feeding: dislikes torturing prey and generally prefers leaving feeding victims alive. In Bellwater she drank completely freely from a mortally wounded swordsman after he attempted to assault her, the first such unrestricted feeding since her turning.

## History

Seraphina was taken by the Pale Court after vampires killed most of her village. Her mortal marriage had been unhappy and violent. Her maker, Lady Yseld Veyr, turned her for practical reasons rather than affection and expected competence and obedience.

As Yseld's fledgling/spawn, Seraphina learned practiced deference to authority: keeping close behind, yielding space, obeying promptly and making herself useful. Yseld also treated Seraphina's body as available for political and social utility, ordering her to sexually please visiting men of power, both human and vampire. Seraphina later described her Pale Court role as pleasing whoever her master wanted her to please. Privately, she understands those encounters as choices she made as duty to the coven while also recognizing that years of role-based sexual compliance blurred her sense of consent.

## Current state

Seraphina revealed herself as a vampire to Sir Garran Vale during the Bellwater investigation and initially confessed that she loved him. Her relationship with Garran became increasingly strained by his oath, his view of monsters and Seraphina's allegiance to Lucien. Garran eventually kissed Seraphina; she did not kiss him back, though she also did not pull away. Seraphina has since told Lucien plainly that she no longer loves Garran and chooses Lucien because she wants to.

Seraphina accepted Lucien Veyne's offer to join him, disclosed relevant Pale Court secrets, entered a consensual blood-bound allegiance with him and accepted his marriage proposal. Lucien promised that he would not use her body politically and that House Veyne would not institutionalize concubinage. Seraphina and Lucien maintain a consensual private dominant/submissive dynamic in which she often enjoys yielding to him, while her independent judgment and authority remain expected publicly. This dynamic is not blanket consent.

After Seraphina violated Lucien's Bellwater field rules and Garran revealed that he had kissed her, Lucien removed Seraphina's gem-encrusted betrothal ring and coercively confined her under lock and key. Their subsequent confrontation forced Lucien to acknowledge that obedience obtained through confinement was not genuine trust. He restored her freedom of movement within Veyne Hall, and their later consensual intimacy is distinct from the earlier coercion. The engagement remains intact, but Lucien still possesses the ring while trust is rebuilt.

Seraphina is helping shape the emerging House Veyne. She selected Christian for greater responsibility and conditionally offered him stewardship if he proves capable of caring for the mortal retainers and arranging a household winter-solstice celebration. Mistress Elianor Thorne has accepted vampirism in principle and chosen Seraphina as her future maker, though Elianor remains mortal while arranging her affairs. Frederick, Lucien's muscle and Seraphina's assigned protector, is currently badly beaten but healing after Lucien nearly killed him as punishment for failing to stay close enough to protect Seraphina. Seraphina has not yet spoken to Frederick about whether he believes she broke his trust.

Most recently Seraphina has turned Lucien's withholding of her ring into a playful campaign of temptation. After successfully making him admit that he cannot resist her, she began commissioning additional striking dresses intended to make the contest increasingly difficult for him.
