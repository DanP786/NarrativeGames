# Seraphina

## Identity
- Apparent age: 24
- Actual age: 42
- Nature: vampire
- Current allegiance: House Veyne and Lucien Veyne by renewed declared choice, formerly the Pale Court
- Current role: Lucien's betrothed and co-founder/partner in House Veyne with newly asserted governing authority; prospective maker of Elianor Thorne

## Appearance
Seraphina has honey-blonde hair, blue eyes and a small build suited to speed and agility rather than strength. She enjoys makeup, especially using it to imitate the warmth and colour of living skin, and has learned to use clothing as both self-expression and a social weapon. After returning to Veyne Hall she deliberately rejected clothes chosen for her by Mara Voss, selected her own white gown and used makeup to sharpen her presentation when she felt socially threatened.

## Temperament
Seraphina is quiet and observant by nature. She tends to watch before speaking and has spent much of her vampiric life surviving through usefulness, restraint and careful obedience rather than physical dominance. She is increasingly deliberate about distinguishing obedience she freely chooses from obedience imposed upon her.

She dislikes needless cruelty and torture. Her instinct is usually to leave feeding victims alive, and she has repeatedly shown concern for mortals who exist within vampire power structures. At House Veyne she pushed for willing sexual relationships rather than concubinage, fair treatment of retainers, reasonable material comfort for the humans of the household and a winter-solstice celebration specifically for them.

## History and the Pale Court
Seraphina was born mortal and married off to a lazy farmer who drank too much; the marriage was unhappy and violent. Her village was later attacked by vampires. She was taken by the Pale Court after most of the village was killed and was turned by Lady Yseld Veyr for practical rather than affectionate reasons.

As Yseld's fledgling, Seraphina learned practiced deference to authority: keeping close behind, yielding space, obeying promptly and making herself useful. Yseld also treated Seraphina's body as available for political and social utility, ordering her to sexually please visiting men of power, both human and vampire. Seraphina later described her Pale Court role as pleasing whoever her master wanted her to please. Privately, she understands those historical encounters as choices she made as duty to the coven while also recognizing that years of role-based sexual compliance blurred her sense of consent.

After Seraphina later left Veyne Hall alone, the Pale Court forcibly recovered her. Yseld's old expectations resumed immediately and included assigning Seraphina to wealthy Lord Othmar Rell at a Pale Court ball. Lucien entered the Court alone, publicly called Seraphina his betrothed, and Seraphina explicitly asked him to take her away. During their escape she told him, "My home is with you. I know that now." Sven allowed them an opening to flee after Seraphina pleaded that she wanted to go and invoked Yseld's treatment of her.

## Feeding and vampirism
Seraphina dislikes torturing prey and generally prefers feeding without killing. She commonly selects people she can rationalize as deserving some consequence, particularly drunks or men behaving badly toward women, but she has also fed tactically when survival required it.

During the Bellwater investigation she fed briefly from a bound worker to accelerate healing after severe injuries and deliberately left him alive. Later, after a mortally wounded swordsman attempted to sexually assault her, Seraphina drank completely freely from him. It was the first time since her turning that she had allowed herself that degree of unrestricted feeding; he died afterward.

Her skin is unnaturally cold. Direct sunlight is dangerous enough that she uses a hooded cloak to protect herself, but exact damage thresholds have not yet been established. Her proper hooded sunlight cloak remains with the unresolved Bellwater operation.

## Garran Vale
Seraphina followed Sir Garran Vale secretly for roughly a year before their first direct relationship, drawn to his authority, seriousness, strength, confidence and devotion to duty. During the Bellwater investigation she revealed that she was a vampire and told him, "Because I love you."

Their relationship became increasingly complicated by Garran's oath to kill monsters, Seraphina's feeding, his inability to reconcile her conduct with his doctrine and her eventual allegiance to Lucien. Garran later kissed Seraphina while knowing she was engaged to Lucien. Seraphina did not kiss him back, though she also did not pull away. She subsequently told Lucien plainly, "I do not love Garran anymore. I have chosen you because I want to." That remains Seraphina's declared position. Garran does not know she said this to Lucien.

During Seraphina's disappearance Lucien went to Garran for help searching. Garran was openly pleased to hear she appeared to have left Lucien but nevertheless helped. Garran later came to Veyne Hall to ensure Seraphina was safe after her return. Whether he remained after Mara ordered him out is unestablished.

## Lucien Veyne
Lucien first approached Seraphina as a rival vampire during the Bellwater investigation and offered her another allegiance. She eventually accepted, disclosed relevant Pale Court secrets and entered a consensual blood-bound allegiance with him. She accepted his marriage proposal. Lucien promised that he would never use her body politically as Yseld had done, would never ask her to give herself sexually to another and would not build House Veyne around institutional concubinage.

Seraphina and Lucien maintain a consensual private dominant/submissive relationship. Seraphina often enjoys deliberately yielding physical control and deference to him in private. This is not blanket consent and does not transform coercion into consensual play merely because the relationship contains chosen submission.

That distinction became central after Bellwater. Lucien removed Seraphina's ring and coercively confined her after she violated field rules and Garran revealed their kiss. Seraphina challenged the premise directly, and Lucien eventually acknowledged that locked-door obedience could not constitute genuine trust. A later attempt at repair also failed when Lucien demanded trust without giving Seraphina a clear path and made her chosen intimacy feel insufficient. Seraphina left Veyne Hall rather than continue proving herself on undefined terms.

After the Pale Court forcibly recovered her, Seraphina chose Lucien again when he came for her. Back at Veyne Hall she demanded, "My ring," and Lucien restored the gem-encrusted betrothal ring immediately without another test. Seraphina is wearing it again and the engagement is mutually reaffirmed.

Seraphina then challenged Lucien over the political structure of their relationship. She rejected the prospect of being merely his exclusive concubine or privately named queen while he retained all meaningful authority. Lucien agreed that this would make House Veyne a prettier cage and committed to giving Seraphina genuine authority in household governance and the emerging coven. Their consensual private dominance/submission is now explicitly separate from Seraphina's public judgment and office.

Their reconciliation has also become intensely physical by mutual choice. Seraphina willingly renews the private submission she promised Lucien; this remains something she gives, not evidence that Lucien owns her decisions. During daylight rest she jokingly observed that if children were possible Lucien would surely have made her pregnant by now. Lucien said that if such a thing were possible he would want the child because it would be theirs. This establishes only hypothetical feeling; no pregnancy is implied.

## House Veyne
Seraphina helped shape the emerging coven rather than merely joining Lucien's household. She selected Christian for closer consideration, tested him by placing provisioning in his hands and conditionally offered him stewardship if he proved capable of overseeing retainer welfare and arranging a household winter-solstice celebration. She explicitly told him that House Veyne would not have concubines and that sexual companionship must involve willing participants.

Mistress Elianor Thorne accepted vampirism in principle after Seraphina explained its costs and chose Seraphina as the vampire she wants to make her. Elianor remains mortal while arranging her affairs.

Frederick serves as Lucien's protector and was nearly beaten to death by Lucien after failing his Bellwater assignment. He is recovering. Seraphina has chosen him as her preferred third attendee at the upcoming Pale Court parley if he is genuinely healthy enough to fight.

Mara Voss, a mortal gentlewoman with household-management experience, was brought in temporarily while Lucien repeatedly left Veyne Hall searching for Seraphina. Mara's overfamiliarity and intrusion into Seraphina's domestic space caused immediate tension. Mara has never been Lucien's lover. Seraphina has now asserted that the lady-of-the-house authority Mara was exercising belongs to Seraphina if she chooses to wield it.

## Bellwater and unresolved threats
Seraphina helped expose an organized network collecting blood from living captives. Twelve captives were freed. The chain leads upward toward Master Hadrik, but Hadrik's exact role and the final recipient or use of the blood remain unknown. A riverside storehouse remains a lead and the substitution operation was left unresolved.

The Pale Court now knows Lucien Veyne's name and House Veyne and has formally demanded a truce meeting at an old north-road chapel at midnight three nights after delivery of its letter. Two Court representatives will attend; Lucien may bring Seraphina and one other. The author, the representatives and Yseld's exact plans remain unknown.

## Current state
Seraphina is at Veyne Hall, resting through daylight in the shared chambers with Lucien after their renewed reconciliation. She is clean and groomed; her gem-encrusted betrothal ring is restored to her hand. Her original daggers were confiscated by the Pale Court. Lucien's ivory-handled daggers and red armour remain available at Veyne Hall.

Seraphina has chosen Lucien and House Veyne again, but on clearer terms: she intends to possess genuine public authority while keeping her private submission voluntary and separate. House Veyne intends to attend the Pale Court truce meeting, with Frederick preferred as the third attendee if recovered enough to fight.
