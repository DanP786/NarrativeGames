# Session 01

Seraphina's secret fixation on Sir Garran Vale drew Lady Yseld Veyr's scrutiny and eventually carried Seraphina into Garran's Bellwater investigation. She revealed herself as a vampire, confessed that she loved him, fought beside him, suffered serious injury, and confronted both her own hunger and Garran's revulsion toward monsters. During the Bellwater violence she fed fully from a mortally wounded swordsman after he attempted to assault her, the first time since her turning that she allowed herself to drink without restraint.

Lucien Veyne entered Seraphina's life as a rival vampire lord and offered her a place beside him in founding a new coven. Seraphina ultimately abandoned the Pale Court for Lucien, disclosed Yseld's useful secrets, entered a consensual blood-bound allegiance with him, and accepted his proposal of marriage. She later told Lucien plainly that she no longer loved Garran and chose Lucien because she wanted him. Lucien promised that House Veyne would not use Seraphina's body as the Pale Court had done and that the new household would have no institutional concubines.

House Veyne began taking shape around them. Seraphina selected Christian for responsibility, testing him first on provisioning and later conditionally offering him stewardship if he can safeguard the welfare of the mortal retainers and arrange a household winter-solstice celebration for them. Frederick became Lucien's principal muscle and protector. Mistress Elianor Thorne accepted Seraphina's offer of vampirism in principle and chose Seraphina as her future maker, though she remains mortal while arranging her affairs.

The Bellwater investigation uncovered an organized blood-collection network connected upward to Master Hadrik. Living captives were being repeatedly bled; twelve were freed. A riverside storehouse with a broken waterwheel became the next lead, and a substitution plan was prepared using six original human-blood bottles and six animal-blood replacements, but Seraphina left before the handoff and the operation remains unresolved. Garran kissed Seraphina during the investigation; she did not kiss him back, but she also did not pull away. When Lucien learned of the kiss and of Seraphina's disobedience in entering danger alone, the confrontation ended with Lucien removing her betrothal ring and taking her home.

Lucien then confined Seraphina under lock and key while demanding that she earn back his trust. Their subsequent confrontation forced him to acknowledge that locked-door obedience was not genuine trust; he told her he loved her, the door was opened, and Seraphina later exercised her freedom to leave their private rooms without being stopped. Their consensual private intimacy resumed separately from that coercive confinement. Lucien later admitted that he had beaten Frederick nearly to death for failing his assigned duty to remain close enough to protect Seraphina, while also acknowledging Seraphina's own role in creating the separation. Frederick's own feelings about either of them remain unknown.

Most recently Seraphina turned Lucien's continued possession of her gem-encrusted betrothal ring into a playful contest. Wearing a fitted red silk halter dress, she successfully tempted him into admitting that he could not resist her, then declared that she intends to make his life difficult until the ring is returned. After claiming he had lasted only two hours in their first contest, she left him in his office and began commissioning still more devastating dresses from her own office. Lucien retains the ring and maintains that its return is tied to restored trust; their engagement remains intact.
