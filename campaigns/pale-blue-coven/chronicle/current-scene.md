# Current scene

Seraphina has escaped a forced return to the Pale Court with Lucien Veyne. At the Pale Court ball, Lucien publicly called her his betrothed; Seraphina asked him to take her away immediately. During the escape Seraphina told Lucien, "My home is with you. I know that now," explained Yseld's coercive assignment of her to Lord Othmar Rell, and explicitly asked Lucien to return her betrothal ring when they reached home. Lucien promised to do so without another test. They later sheltered through daylight at a roadside inn, reconciled physically by mutual choice, and resumed the road after sunset.

Seraphina and Lucien reach Veyne Hall. In the entrance hall they find Sir Garran Vale, who knowingly crossed Lucien's prohibition against entering Veyne property. Garran says he came to ensure Seraphina was safe. During the confrontation Seraphina meets Mara Voss for the first time: a beautiful, long-black-haired new household addition with a low sultry voice, confident authority, and enough familiarity to place a hand on Lucien's arm. Mara orders Garran to leave, then judgmentally tells travel-worn Seraphina that she has already arranged a bath, laid out clothes, and that Seraphina can fix her hair and be made presentable.

Seraphina withdraws her hand from Lucien quickly after Mara touches his other arm, gives Garran a small smile, and leaves for her rooms. In her chamber she bathes, tends her honey-blonde hair, rejects every garment Mara laid out, dresses in a white gown of her own choosing, and deliberately applies makeup after feeling threatened and competitive because of Mara's beauty and familiarity with Lucien.

Rather than return immediately to Lucien or confront Mara, Seraphina visits Christian first. Christian is visibly relieved to see her safely returned. He tells her the hall was unsettled during her absence, Lucien had people searching, Frederick is recovering, and Mara arrived during Seraphina's absence and quickly began organizing household routines and issuing instructions. Christian does not know Mara's private relationship with Lucien, exact rank, nature, origin or the terms under which she came to Veyne Hall.

Christian says nobody moved into Seraphina's rooms. Seraphina questions this. Christian clarifies that nobody has been sleeping there or treating the chamber as their own quarters to his knowledge, though Mara has plainly entered the rooms and taken practical authority over arranging servants, baths and clothing there.

Seraphina looks away and observes, "And Lucien has been preoccupied looking for me." Christian confirms from his own household perspective that Lucien's attention was indeed dominated by Seraphina's disappearance and search. He carefully avoids claiming knowledge of Lucien's private dealings with Mara. Christian notes that Lucien was frequently absent or occupied, household decisions still had to be made, and Mara was willing to make them. This offers a practical explanation for how Mara accumulated visible authority quickly without establishing whether Lucien formally granted all of it.

Seraphina's original daggers remain confiscated by the Pale Court. Her gifted ivory-handled daggers and fitted red leather armour remain at Veyne Hall and are available. Her betrothal ring remains with Lucien, who promised to return it now that they are home.
