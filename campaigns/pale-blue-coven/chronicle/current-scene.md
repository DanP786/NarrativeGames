# Current scene

After being forcibly recovered by the Pale Court near the hill-road priory, Seraphina is taken back to the Pale Court stronghold. Lady Yseld Veyr has removed her from the Hadrik investigation and assigned another Pale Court operative to continue it. Back at the Pale Court, the household absorbs Seraphina's return into routine, restores her black-and-pale-blue formal appearance, keeps her original daggers, and treats her as though she has resumed her old place despite her explicit rejection of its claim on her loyalty.

At the Pale Court ball, Yseld assigns Seraphina to Lord Othmar Rell, a wealthy mortal magnate whose river toll rights, grain warehouses, and wagon contracts make him commercially useful. Seraphina resumes the pleasant, gracious social persona expected of her, dances with Othmar, and gives him the familiar cultivated story she has historically used with politically useful men: sheltered, modest, flattered by worldly attention, shaping the interaction so that any later intimacy he seeks feels to him mutually anticipated rather than imposed duty. Othmar accepts the performance and grows increasingly comfortable and personally interested.

In a quieter gallery Othmar lightly challenges Seraphina's cultivated innocence. Before she can answer, Lucien Veyne appears behind her and says, "There you are, little bunny." Seraphina immediately goes stiff. When Othmar asks whether they are acquainted, Lucien answers publicly and without qualification, "She is my betrothed." The declaration exposes a major secret Seraphina had withheld from Yseld: not merely that Lucien is personally close to her, but that they are formally bound toward marriage. Seraphina feels panic and immediately looks past Lucien toward the ballroom to see who may have witnessed or overheard the declaration.

The gallery is quieter than the main hall but not sealed from it. Several guests and Pale Court attendants are within plausible sight or earshot of the exchange. Yseld herself is not established as having heard the declaration, but a passing servant has gone back into the ballroom and the risk of the information spreading is immediate.

Seraphina steps back from Othmar and tells him, "Apologies, Lord Othmar, it appears our evening has concluded." Othmar accepts the dismissal with controlled dignity rather than making a scene, but he is plainly displeased and confused that Yseld assigned an apparently betrothed woman to entertain him without disclosing it. He withdraws toward the ballroom, leaving Seraphina and Lucien in the gallery. This creates a direct political risk: Othmar can now confront Yseld or repeat Lucien's claim to others.

Lord Othmar now knows that Lucien directly claims Seraphina as his betrothed, though he still does not know the deeper allegiance, blood oath, House Veyne, Garran, or Hadrik history. Yseld still does not yet have established on-screen knowledge that Lucien is the unknown vampire Seraphina stayed with, that Seraphina swore allegiance to him, or that they became engaged; if Othmar or another witness reaches her, those questions may connect quickly. Lucien knows Seraphina's former maker used her body for political and social sexual service and has explicitly promised never to require her to give sexual access to another for political utility. He does not yet have established knowledge on-screen that Yseld assigned Seraphina to Othmar tonight.

The unresolved rupture remains: Seraphina has explicitly told Yseld that her loyalty ended when Yseld began giving her sexually to useful men, while Yseld continues to regard those assignments as legitimate coven duty. Her engagement with Lucien has not formally ended; Lucien still possesses the ring, and Seraphina does not know whether she wants it returned. The Bellwater blood-substitution operation, riverside-storehouse lead, and Hadrik network remain unresolved.
