# Current scene

Seraphina has escaped a forced return to the Pale Court with Lucien Veyne. At the Pale Court ball, Lucien publicly called her his betrothed; Seraphina asked him to take her away immediately. During the escape Seraphina told Lucien, "My home is with you. I know that now," explained Yseld's coercive assignment of her to Lord Othmar Rell, and explicitly asked Lucien to return her betrothal ring when they reached home. Lucien promised to do so without another test. They later sheltered through daylight at a roadside inn, reconciled physically by mutual choice, and resumed the road after sunset.

Seraphina and Lucien have returned to Veyne Hall. Garran Vale was waiting despite Lucien's prohibition, saying he came to ensure Seraphina was safe. Seraphina also met Mara Voss, a striking black-haired mortal household addition who displayed confidence, household authority and physical familiarity with Lucien. Mara ordered Garran out and presumptuously arranged Seraphina's bath and clothing. Seraphina rejected Mara's chosen garments, bathed, dressed in her own white gown, repaired her hair and deliberately applied makeup because Mara made her feel threatened and competitive.

Seraphina questioned Christian about what changed during her absence. He confirmed Lucien remained preoccupied with searching for her, Mara arrived during that absence and quickly began organizing household routines, and Mara has entered Seraphina's rooms without living in them.

Seraphina waits for Lucien in his office, blocks his path to his desk, and demands her ring. Lucien honors his promise without testing her and restores the gem-encrusted betrothal ring to her finger.

Seraphina interrogates Lucien about Mara. Lucien states Mara has not shared his bed, he has not taken her as a lover, and nobody warmed his bed while Seraphina was gone. Asked why Mara touched him, Lucien explains that Mara is confident, useful and presumptuous, and that he allowed more familiarity than usual while his attention was consumed by finding Seraphina. When Lucien calls Mara beautiful, Seraphina catches the adjective; Lucien distinguishes beauty from desire and reminds Seraphina that she is the woman he searched for, entered the Pale Court for and intends to marry.

Lucien explains Mara is a mortal gentlewoman he knew socially before Seraphina, never as a lover. During Seraphina's disappearance Lucien repeatedly left to search and Christian was not ready for full stewardship, so Lucien called Mara in temporarily to manage servants, stores, suppliers, correspondence, schedules and guests. Mara's intended authority never included Seraphina's private chambers, wardrobe or standing.

Seraphina objects that these are tasks for the lady of the house, then challenges whether House Veyne is truly different from the Pale Court if she is reduced to decoration and Lucien's exclusive concubine. Lucien accepts the force of the challenge and commits to Seraphina possessing real power in the house and emerging coven, with her chosen private submission explicitly separate from her public judgment and office.

The confrontation eases into teasing. Seraphina says she will still kneel before Lucien in private. When reminded she must decide what to do with Mara, she jealously threatens to cut off Mara's hair; Lucien refuses arbitrary humiliation but reassures Seraphina that her own hair is prettier. Seraphina softens and agrees.

Mara interrupts with a newly delivered letter for Lucien. Seraphina steps closer to Lucien before Mara enters. Mara notices Seraphina's white gown, restored ring and closeness to Lucien, reports the messenger refused hospitality, and offers the letter without touching Lucien. Seraphina dismisses Mara with a cool, "Thank you, Mara." Mara recognizes the dismissal, addresses her as "Lady Seraphina," and withdraws without challenging her authority.

Lucien offers Seraphina the correspondence as an immediate opportunity to exercise her voice in House Veyne affairs. Seraphina opens it. The Pale Court message demands a truce meeting at an old roadside chapel at midnight three nights hence. Two Pale Court representatives will attend; Lucien may bring Seraphina and one other companion. It says the meeting will address Seraphina's defection, Lucien's claim upon her and the injury between the houses, and warns refusal will be taken as confirmation that House Veyne chooses open hostility. The writer is unnamed, marked only with a pale-blue sign, and the letter pointedly calls Seraphina "the fledgling Seraphina." Whether Yseld will attend and whether the truce is trustworthy remain unknown.

Seraphina judges the meeting itself reasonable. Lucien agrees that accepting negotiations is preferable to choosing open war by silence, while insisting they prepare for possible treachery. Seraphina wants the permitted third attendee to be someone who can fight. Lucien names Frederick as the obvious House Veyne candidate if sufficiently recovered and reluctantly raises Garran as a formidable but politically dangerous alternative.

When Seraphina accuses Lucien of inconsistency because he had threatened to kill Garran if the knight entered Veyne Hall again, Lucien admits the threat was real when he made it but explains that circumstances changed while Seraphina was missing. Lucien went to Garran himself during his search for Seraphina, reasoning that Garran was one of the few people outside Veyne Hall who knew her habits, capabilities and some of the dangers around her. Garran helped Lucien search and shared what useful knowledge he had rather than exploiting Seraphina's disappearance. Garran was openly pleased to learn that Seraphina had apparently left Lucien, which Lucien found infuriating, but the knight nevertheless aided the search rather than using the rupture to pursue his own advantage. Lucien therefore no longer considers Garran's mere presence at Veyne Hall automatically punishable by death, though he has not forgiven the kiss, the prior boundary crossing or Garran's feelings for Seraphina. This also explains how Garran knew Seraphina was absent and why he later came to Veyne Hall to learn whether she was safe.

Whether Garran is still physically present at Veyne Hall after Mara ordered him out remains unestablished.

Lucien and Seraphina remain together in the office with the opened Pale Court letter. Mara remains a temporary household administrator pending Seraphina's larger decision about whether she stays, hands over authority, or leaves. Mara's private ambitions and feelings toward Lucien remain unestablished.

Seraphina's original daggers remain confiscated by the Pale Court. Her gifted ivory-handled daggers and fitted red leather armour remain available at Veyne Hall. Her betrothal ring is restored to her hand.

Immediate unresolved development: House Veyne is inclined to accept the Pale Court truce meeting at the old roadside chapel at midnight three nights hence; they must decide precautions and choose a capable third attendee. Frederick is an obvious loyal candidate if recovered; Garran has now materially helped Lucien during the search for Seraphina and is a formidable but politically fraught alternative. The Pale Court rupture, Bellwater blood-substitution operation, riverside-storehouse lead, Hadrik network, Christian's prospective stewardship and Elianor Thorne's planned turning remain unresolved.
