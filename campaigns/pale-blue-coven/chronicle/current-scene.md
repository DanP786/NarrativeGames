# Current scene

Several days later at Veyne Hall, Seraphina and Lucien share a private bath before the fire. In the privacy of their rooms, it has become usual for Seraphina to wear nothing but her betrothal ring. Lucien has revealed that before their Bellwater encounters his old master once took him to the Pale Court, where Lucien saw Seraphina and thought her beautiful. Lucien later killed that master after rejecting the belief that making Lucien meant owning him, and he chose not to inherit the old coven. During the Pale Court visit Lady Yseld offered Seraphina's company to Lucien's master as part of the Court's hospitality. Lucien says that when younger he did not fully understand the coercive structure behind what he saw, envied his master, and only later understood it differently. He admits that at first the privileges of mastery, including being offered beautiful people by other covens, appealed to him because he confused power with freedom. He says he still likes authority and willing obedience but does not want a court where a maker sends a fledgling to someone's bed because refusal would cost safety, standing, or favor, and says he will not build such a court. Seraphina traces her fingers up Lucien's forearm and asks, "No concubines?" Lucien confirms that their new coven will have no institutional concubine role and that neither Seraphina nor another member will be assigned sexual service as a duty of rank or political hospitality. This is consistent with Lucien's earlier promise never to ask Seraphina to give herself to another. It does not prohibit freely chosen relationships among consenting adults or change Seraphina and Lucien's mutually chosen private dynamic. The anonymous-message investigation and Bellwater followers remain unresolved. The betrothal ring's exact gemstone, metal, provenance, monetary value, and deeper symbolism remain unestablished.
