# Current scene

Seraphina has escaped a forced return to the Pale Court with Lucien Veyne. At the Pale Court ball, Lucien publicly called her his betrothed; Seraphina asked him to take her away immediately. During the escape Seraphina told Lucien, "My home is with you. I know that now," explained Yseld's coercive assignment of her to Lord Othmar Rell, and explicitly asked Lucien to return her betrothal ring when they reached home. Lucien promised to do so without another test. They later sheltered through daylight at a roadside inn, reconciled physically by mutual choice, and resumed the road after sunset.

Seraphina and Lucien have returned to Veyne Hall. Garran Vale was waiting despite Lucien's prohibition, saying he came to ensure Seraphina was safe. Seraphina also met Mara Voss, a striking black-haired mortal household addition who displayed confidence, household authority and physical familiarity with Lucien. Mara ordered Garran out and presumptuously arranged Seraphina's bath and clothing. Seraphina rejected Mara's chosen garments, bathed, dressed in her own white gown, repaired her hair and deliberately applied makeup because Mara made her feel threatened and competitive.

Seraphina questioned Christian about what changed during her absence. He confirmed Lucien remained preoccupied with searching for her, Mara arrived during that absence and quickly began organizing household routines, and Mara has entered Seraphina's rooms without living in them.

In Lucien's office, Seraphina demanded her ring and Lucien restored the gem-encrusted betrothal ring to her finger immediately, without another test. She then interrogated him about Mara. Lucien stated Mara has never shared his bed or been his lover and explained that Mara was temporarily called in to manage the household while Lucien repeatedly left to search for Seraphina. Seraphina challenged whether House Veyne was truly different from the Pale Court if she was reduced to decoration and Lucien's exclusive concubine. Lucien accepted the criticism and committed to Seraphina possessing real power in the house and emerging coven, with her chosen private submission explicitly separate from her public judgment and office.

Mara later interrupted with a newly delivered letter for Lucien. Seraphina dismissed Mara with a cool, "Thank you, Mara." Mara recognized the dismissal, addressed her as "Lady Seraphina," and withdrew without challenging her authority.

The Pale Court message demands a truce meeting at an old roadside chapel at midnight three nights hence. Two Pale Court representatives will attend; Lucien may bring Seraphina and one other companion. It says the meeting will address Seraphina's defection, Lucien's claim upon her and the injury between the houses, and warns refusal will be taken as confirmation that House Veyne chooses open hostility. The writer is unnamed, marked only with a pale-blue sign, and the letter pointedly calls Seraphina "the fledgling Seraphina." Whether Yseld will attend and whether the truce is trustworthy remain unknown.

Seraphina judges the meeting itself reasonable. Lucien agrees they should attend while preparing for possible treachery. Seraphina wants the permitted third attendee to be someone who can fight. Lucien names Frederick if sufficiently recovered and reluctantly raises Garran as a formidable but politically dangerous alternative.

When Seraphina accuses Lucien of inconsistency because he had threatened to kill Garran if the knight entered Veyne Hall again, Lucien explains that circumstances changed while she was missing. Lucien went to Garran himself during his search because Garran knew Seraphina's habits, capabilities and some dangers around her. Garran helped search and shared useful knowledge rather than exploiting her disappearance, although he was openly pleased to learn she had apparently left Lucien. Lucien therefore no longer treats Garran's mere presence at Veyne Hall as automatically punishable by death.

Seraphina asks Lucien, "You didn't think I had run off to him?" Lucien admits plainly that Garran was one of the first possibilities he considered. Part of why he went to Garran was to see with his own eyes whether Seraphina was there. Garran's genuine surprise at Lucien's arrival and obvious pleasure at hearing that Seraphina had left Lucien made it clear Garran had not already seen or sheltered her. Lucien admits the suspicion was ugly but unsurprising after the kiss, their recent rupture and Seraphina leaving without a destination. He also makes clear that he did not conclude she had betrayed him; he checked the possibility rather than acting on jealousy as certainty. This is another contrast with his earlier coercive response to Garran and the Bellwater rupture.

Whether Garran is still physically present at Veyne Hall after Mara ordered him out remains unestablished.

Lucien and Seraphina remain together in the office with the opened Pale Court letter. Mara remains a temporary household administrator pending Seraphina's larger decision about whether she stays, hands over authority, or leaves. Mara's private ambitions and feelings toward Lucien remain unestablished.

Seraphina's original daggers remain confiscated by the Pale Court. Her gifted ivory-handled daggers and fitted red leather armour remain available at Veyne Hall. Her betrothal ring is restored to her hand.

Immediate unresolved development: House Veyne is inclined to accept the Pale Court truce meeting at the old roadside chapel at midnight three nights hence; they must decide precautions and choose a capable third attendee. Frederick is an obvious loyal candidate if recovered; Garran has materially helped Lucien during the search for Seraphina and is a formidable but politically fraught alternative. The Pale Court rupture, Bellwater blood-substitution operation, riverside-storehouse lead, Hadrik network, Christian's prospective stewardship and Elianor Thorne's planned turning remain unresolved.
