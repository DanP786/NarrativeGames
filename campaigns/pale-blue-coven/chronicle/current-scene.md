# Current scene

The next day at Veyne Hall, Seraphina walks the halls in close-fitted trousers, boots, tunic and waistcoat with numerous leather straps, her honey-blonde hair in soft bouncing curls. Christian, still under consideration for the inner circle while handling household provisions, has been distracted by Seraphina's appearance. After Seraphina teasingly asks whether they need to find him a companion, Christian suggests that Frederick take him to a brothel and proposes recruiting concubines for the new coven from there. Seraphina stops immediately and lays down the household rule directly: "Any pleasure you seek out will be from a willing participant. You will either use your charm or pay good coin. We will not have concubines in this household." Christian is therefore explicitly informed that House Veyne will not maintain an institutional concubine role or assigned sexual service, and that any sexual or romantic companionship he seeks must be consensual, whether freely pursued through mutual attraction or paid for as a consensual service. No concubines are authorized or recruited. Whether Christian knows the personal history behind Seraphina and Lucien's rule remains unestablished. Frederick remains Lucien's muscle. Mistress Elianor Thorne has accepted Seraphina's offer in principle and chosen Seraphina to make her a vampire, but remains mortal while arranging her affairs before a carriage is sent for her. Seraphina remains Lucien's betrothed and intended queen and continues to wear his enormous gem-encrusted betrothal ring. The anonymous-message investigation and Bellwater followers remain unresolved.
