# Current scene

Seraphina has escaped a forced return to the Pale Court with Lucien Veyne. At the Pale Court ball, Lucien publicly called her his betrothed; Seraphina asked him to take her away immediately. During the escape Seraphina told Lucien, "My home is with you. I know that now," explained Yseld's coercive assignment of her to Lord Othmar Rell, and explicitly asked Lucien to return her betrothal ring when they reached home. Lucien promised to do so without another test. They later sheltered through daylight at a roadside inn, reconciled physically by mutual choice, and resumed the road after sunset.

Seraphina and Lucien have returned to Veyne Hall. Garran Vale was waiting despite Lucien's prohibition, saying he came to ensure Seraphina was safe. Seraphina also met Mara Voss, a striking black-haired mortal household addition who displayed confidence, household authority and physical familiarity with Lucien. Mara ordered Garran out and presumptuously arranged Seraphina's bath and clothing. Seraphina rejected Mara's chosen garments, bathed, dressed in her own white gown, repaired her hair and deliberately applied makeup because Mara made her feel threatened and competitive.

Seraphina questioned Christian about what changed during her absence. He confirmed Lucien remained preoccupied with searching for her, Mara arrived during that absence and quickly began organizing household routines, and Mara has entered Seraphina's rooms without living in them.

Seraphina waits for Lucien in his office, blocks his path to his desk, and demands her ring. Lucien honors his promise without testing her and restores the gem-encrusted betrothal ring to her finger.

Seraphina interrogates Lucien about Mara. Lucien states Mara has not shared his bed, he has not taken her as a lover, and nobody warmed his bed while Seraphina was gone. Asked why Mara touched him, Lucien explains that Mara is confident, useful and presumptuous, and that he allowed more familiarity than usual while his attention was consumed by finding Seraphina. When Lucien calls Mara beautiful, Seraphina catches the adjective; Lucien distinguishes beauty from desire and reminds Seraphina that she is the woman he searched for, entered the Pale Court for and intends to marry.

Seraphina demands Mara be gone. Lucien asks her first to hear why Mara is present. He explains Mara is a mortal gentlewoman from a minor but well-connected merchant-gentry family whom he knew socially before Seraphina, never as a lover. During Seraphina's disappearance, Lucien repeatedly left to search and Christian was not ready for full stewardship, so Lucien called Mara in temporarily to manage servants, stores, suppliers, correspondence, schedules and guests. Mara's intended authority never included Seraphina's private chambers, wardrobe or standing.

Seraphina objects that these are tasks for the lady of the house. Lucien agrees that Mara was filling an absence rather than replacing her, and says Seraphina can take actual authority over Veyne Hall rather than merely hold a title.

Seraphina then prods Lucien in the chest and directly challenges the structure of their relationship: "You told me you didn't want me as just decoration. Tell me how this is turning out to be anything different than Pale Court, other than being your exclusive concubine." The accusation lands seriously because it invokes the central harm of Seraphina's former coven: being valued for usefulness, appearance, obedience and sexual availability while meaningful authority remained elsewhere.

Lucien does not answer by claiming sexual exclusivity alone makes House Veyne different. He acknowledges that if he keeps every meaningful decision, delegates household authority to others, expects Seraphina merely to look beautiful beside him and then calls her his queen in private, the distinction would indeed be hollow. He states that he wants Seraphina to possess real authority: over the domestic governance she chooses to take, over the shaping and recruitment of their emerging coven, and a voice in decisions affecting House Veyne rather than being assigned duties solely at his pleasure. He also distinguishes their private consensual dominance from public political subordination: her chosen deference in intimacy is not a grant of ownership over her public judgment or office. Lucien accepts that his behavior must demonstrate this difference rather than requiring Seraphina to trust the promise.

Mara remains a temporary household administrator pending Seraphina's decision after this confrontation. Mara's private ambitions, feelings toward Lucien, and whether she intends her role to remain temporary remain unestablished. What occurred with Mara and Garran after Seraphina left the entrance hall remains unestablished. Garran's deeper reason for concern, how he knew Seraphina was absent, and whether he has now left Veyne Hall remain unestablished.

Seraphina's original daggers remain confiscated by the Pale Court. Her gifted ivory-handled daggers and fitted red leather armour remain available at Veyne Hall. Her betrothal ring is restored to her hand.

The Pale Court rupture, Bellwater blood-substitution operation, riverside-storehouse lead, Hadrik network, Christian's prospective stewardship and Elianor Thorne's planned turning remain unresolved.
