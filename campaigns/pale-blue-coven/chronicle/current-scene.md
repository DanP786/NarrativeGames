# Current scene

Seraphina has escaped a forced return to the Pale Court with Lucien Veyne. At the Pale Court ball, Lucien publicly called her his betrothed; Seraphina asked him to take her away immediately. Sven let them pass after Seraphina pleaded that he knew what Yseld made her do. During the escape Seraphina told Lucien, "My home is with you. I know that now," explained Yseld's coercive assignment of her to Lord Othmar Rell, and explicitly asked Lucien to return her betrothal ring when they reached home. Lucien promised to do so without another test. They later sheltered through daylight at a roadside inn, reconciled physically by mutual choice, and resumed the road after sunset.

Seraphina and Lucien reach Veyne Hall without another established Pale Court interception. In the entrance hall they find Sir Garran Vale, who knowingly crossed Lucien's prohibition against entering Veyne property. Garran says he came to ensure Seraphina was safe. During the confrontation Seraphina meets Mara Voss for the first time: a beautiful, long-black-haired new household addition with a low sultry voice, confident authority, and enough familiarity to place a hand on Lucien's arm. Mara orders Garran to leave, then judgmentally tells travel-worn Seraphina that she has already arranged a bath, laid out clothes, and that Seraphina can fix her hair and be made presentable.

Seraphina withdraws her hand from Lucien quickly after Mara touches his other arm, gives Garran a small smile, and leaves for her rooms. Her jaw tightens at Mara, but she does not argue. She had already intended to bathe and change before Mara's intervention.

In Seraphina's chamber, the bath is waiting and her belongings remain where Seraphina herself left them before her departure. She bathes thoroughly, washing away the road, river and remnants of the Pale Court journey, then tends to her honey-blonde hair using her own familiar routine and belongings. She deliberately refuses every garment Mara laid out for her. Instead she chooses her own clothing from her own things, an act of private spite and a quiet reassertion that Mara does not choose Seraphina's presentation or govern her personal rooms. Seraphina is actively angry and privately thinks, in substance, who does Mara think she is?
