# Current scene

After being forcibly recovered by the Pale Court near the hill-road priory, Seraphina is taken back to the Pale Court stronghold. Lady Yseld Veyr has removed her from the Hadrik investigation and assigned another Pale Court operative to continue it. Back at the Pale Court, the household absorbs Seraphina's return into routine, restores her black-and-pale-blue formal appearance, keeps her original daggers, and treats her as though she has resumed her old place despite her explicit rejection of its claim on her loyalty.

At the Pale Court ball, Yseld assigns Seraphina to Lord Othmar Rell, a wealthy mortal magnate whose river toll rights, grain warehouses, and wagon contracts make him commercially useful. Seraphina resumes the pleasant, gracious social persona expected of her and spends the opening of the evening accompanying him.

Lucien Veyne appears in a quieter gallery and calls Seraphina "little bunny." When Othmar asks whether they are acquainted, Lucien answers publicly, "She is my betrothed." Seraphina immediately realizes the danger of the disclosure, dismisses Othmar, and urgently tells Lucien, "Take me away from here. Now." Warning bells sound as they attempt to leave.

Seraphina and Lucien flee through Pale Court passages. Sven, a familiar Pale Court coven-mate, blocks them with another vampire. After Seraphina insists she wants to leave and pleads, "You know what she makes me do," Sven understands the reference to Yseld's treatment and gives them a narrow opening, restraining his companion rather than forcing Seraphina to remain. Yseld's voice is heard behind them calling Seraphina's name as the escape reaches the exterior.

Lucien lifts Seraphina onto one of two prepared horses and mounts the same animal behind her rather than lose time taking the second. They flee together. Seraphina directs Lucien onto a darker road through country she knows, then upstream from a broken bridge to a shallower river crossing. They successfully force the horse across and continue on the opposite bank while pursuit reaches the river behind them.

Mounted closely together after the crossing, Lucien admits that he had believed Seraphina left him and willingly returned to the Pale Court. Seraphina answers plainly, "My home is with you. I know that now." Seraphina leans fully back against him, says she missed him, and explains that after leaving Veyne Hall she became lost rather than deliberately choosing not to return.

Seraphina tells Lucien that Yseld assigned her to entertain Lord Othmar, make him feel special, allow whatever he wanted from her, and smile through it. Lucien now understands the Othmar assignment was a direct continuation of the sexual/political-use system he had previously promised Seraphina she would never endure in House Veyne. His anger is directed at Yseld rather than Seraphina or Othmar.

Lucien explains he came without a war-band because he did not initially know whether Seraphina wanted extraction; an armed House Veyne arrival would have announced war and risked having her moved or confined before he could speak to her. He prepared two horses and entered relying on social audacity and mortal guests to inhibit immediate violence, intending contact first and extraction only if Seraphina wanted it. When she said, "Take me away from here. Now," he acted.

Seraphina points out that the escape and Lucien's public claim mean Yseld can now learn both Lucien's identity/connection to Seraphina and their betrothal. Lucien agrees secrecy is effectively finished and says House Veyne must prepare for Pale Court scrutiny.

Seraphina seriously tells Lucien, "Then you better give it back when we return," explicitly asking for her betrothal ring back and renewing the engagement by her own choice. Lucien promises to return it without another test. Seraphina then teases, "You should probably marry that bride to stop her from running away again." Lucien accepts the marriage implication warmly but rejects the premise that marriage should function as a restraint: he wants to marry her because she chooses to stay and because he wants her as his wife, not because a wedding could prevent her leaving.

Still folded closely around Lucien's arm as they ride, Seraphina remarks that she had some rather nice dresses she did not get to wear. She is referring to the deliberately revealing dresses she had commissioned at Veyne Hall during her earlier campaign of temptation and reconciliation. Lucien recognizes the reference and responds with renewed teasing interest, making clear that he remembers those dresses and expects they can still be worn once she is safely home.

They have not yet safely broken pursuit, though distance and the river have bought time. Pale Court riders remain somewhere behind them. Seraphina is unarmed; her original daggers remain in Pale Court possession. One prepared horse was left behind and taken by the pursuers.

It is still not established that Yseld personally heard Lucien's betrothal declaration, received Othmar's report, or clearly identified Lucien as the unknown vampire Seraphina protected during interrogation; however, given Othmar's direct knowledge, witnesses to the escape, and Lucien's presence, Seraphina and Lucien reasonably assume the information can reach Yseld. Lord Othmar knows Lucien directly claims Seraphina as his betrothed. Lucien knows what Yseld expected of Seraphina with Othmar that night.

The unresolved rupture with the Pale Court remains. Seraphina has now explicitly chosen Veyne Hall as home, chosen Lucien again, asked for the betrothal ring back, and playfully raised the prospect of proceeding to marriage. Their prior trust injuries are not erased. The Bellwater blood-substitution operation, riverside-storehouse lead, and Hadrik network remain unresolved.
