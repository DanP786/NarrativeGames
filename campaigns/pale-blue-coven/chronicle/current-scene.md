# Current scene

Later that evening at Veyne Hall, after the dungeon discussion with Garran, Lucien privately goes over his conditions for Seraphina taking an active field role in the Bellwater investigation while beyond his immediate reach. He permits her to participate but requires Frederick to accompany the expedition; Seraphina is not to disappear alone with Garran. Lucien expects Seraphina to use her own judgment in the field rather than wait helplessly for permission when circumstances change, but requires her to withdraw rather than pursue alone if the investigation splits the group or an unknown supernatural threat tries to draw her away. If Garran gives an immediate tactical order in a fight that is plainly meant to keep someone alive, Lucien permits Seraphina to heed it; this does not grant Garran broader authority over her. Lucien requires Seraphina to return to Veyne Hall if the investigation reveals that the people watching her are deliberately trying to isolate or lure her, rather than accepting the bait without informing him. He also tells her that if circumstances make the original plan impossible, her priority is to come home alive with whatever information she can preserve rather than die merely to complete the task. These conditions are protective and possessive but do not erase Seraphina's independent field judgment or constitute blanket control over her choices. Garran's Bellwater evidence remains active: the operation is consolidating larger shipments, at least one included living captives, and someone has ordered Seraphina watched rather than taken. The exact purpose of the shipments, identity of the person who ordered Seraphina watched, Hadrik's direct involvement, and destination of the living captives remain unknown. Frederick is assigned to accompany the investigation. Christian remains at Veyne Hall handling household provisions. Mistress Elianor Thorne remains mortal while arranging her affairs before Seraphina turns her.
