# Current scene

In a cramped passage off Bellwater's crowded night market, Sir Garran Vale has privately warned Seraphina that someone supplied him with a description of her and falsely associated her with drained deaths, apparently trying to turn him into a weapon against her. Their discussion has shifted to Seraphina's former coven role and why she left. Seraphina has told Garran that her master assigned her to please whoever the master wanted, and that she originally followed and fell in love with Garran because she believed he could save her. Asked whether Lucien knows and whether he asks the same of her, Seraphina says, "He knows. It is why he offered me a way out. And no. I give myself to him willingly." Privately, Seraphina tells herself she is willing with Lucien, while recognizing that years of treating sexual compliance as coven duty have blurred her own sense of consent; she regards herself as having consented to each former encounter because it was her duty. Garran hears only her spoken distinction: Lucien knows her history, offered an escape from that role, does not assign her to others, and Seraphina says her intimacy with him is willing. Garran does not know Seraphina and Lucien are engaged, blood-bound, or planning a new coven with Seraphina as prospective queen, nor that Seraphina recently declared she no longer loves Garran. Lucien remains at the goldsmith's shop and has not been established as seeing Seraphina leave. The two suspected watchers remain unidentified.
