# Current scene

After being forcibly recovered by the Pale Court near the hill-road priory, Seraphina is taken back to the Pale Court stronghold. Lady Yseld Veyr has removed her from the Hadrik investigation and assigned another Pale Court operative to continue it. Back at the Pale Court, the household absorbs Seraphina's return into routine, restores her black-and-pale-blue formal appearance, keeps her original daggers, and treats her as though she has resumed her old place despite her explicit rejection of its claim on her loyalty.

At the Pale Court ball, Yseld assigns Seraphina to Lord Othmar Rell, a wealthy mortal magnate whose river toll rights, grain warehouses, and wagon contracts make him commercially useful. Seraphina resumes the pleasant, gracious social persona expected of her, dances with Othmar, and gives him the familiar cultivated story she has historically used with politically useful men: sheltered, modest, flattered by worldly attention, shaping the interaction so that any later intimacy he seeks feels to him mutually anticipated rather than imposed duty. Othmar accepts the performance and grows increasingly comfortable and personally interested.

In a quieter gallery, Lucien Veyne appears and calls Seraphina "little bunny." When Othmar asks whether they are acquainted, Lucien answers publicly, "She is my betrothed." The declaration exposes a major secret Seraphina had withheld from Yseld. Seraphina immediately checks who might have heard. The gallery is not sealed from the ballroom; a servant has already passed back inside, and the risk of the information spreading is immediate.

Seraphina dismisses Othmar with an apology, saying their evening has concluded. Othmar withdraws with controlled displeasure and heads across the ballroom toward Lady Yseld. Watching him approach her, Seraphina leans against the gallery wall in visible alarm and tells Lucien, "Take me away from here. Now." Lucien recognizes the urgency and accepts her request without demanding an explanation first. He takes her hand and begins moving her away from the gallery toward an exit route rather than toward the ballroom, intending to get her out of the Pale Court immediately if possible.

Othmar has not yet been established as reaching Yseld or speaking to her. Yseld has not yet been established as hearing Lucien's betrothal claim, seeing Lucien clearly, or knowing that he is the unknown vampire Seraphina protected during interrogation. The Pale Court is nevertheless an occupied hostile stronghold full of vampires and attendants; leaving openly with Seraphina may provoke interception once noticed. Lucien's route into the ball, preparations, companions, and escape arrangements remain unestablished and can emerge as the attempted departure unfolds.

Lord Othmar now knows that Lucien directly claims Seraphina as his betrothed, though he still does not know the deeper allegiance, blood oath, House Veyne, Garran, or Hadrik history. Lucien knows Seraphina's former maker used her body for political and social sexual service and has explicitly promised never to require her to give sexual access to another for political utility. He does not yet have established knowledge that Yseld assigned Seraphina to Othmar for the evening or what she expected from that assignment.

The unresolved rupture remains: Seraphina has explicitly told Yseld that her loyalty ended when Yseld began giving her sexually to useful men, while Yseld continues to regard those assignments as legitimate coven duty. Her engagement with Lucien has not formally ended; Lucien still possesses the ring, and Seraphina does not know whether she wants it returned. The Bellwater blood-substitution operation, riverside-storehouse lead, and Hadrik network remain unresolved.
