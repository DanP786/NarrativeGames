# Current scene

At night outside the underground Bellwater gladiator ring, Seraphina stands between the unresolved claims of Sir Garran Vale's oath and her new allegiance to Lucien Veyne. After Garran tells Seraphina she can find him if she wants continued contact and work on the unresolved Hadrik investigation, Lucien's restrained possessiveness finally breaks the surface. He deliberately makes Garran understand that Seraphina is not merely sheltering with him: she has chosen Lucien intimately and politically, and Lucien intends a future with her that he considers far larger than Garran's present hunt. Lucien uses their sexual intimacy as a taunt, telling Garran that Seraphina has cried his name in his bed, and then reveals the larger claim that matters more: he is building something with Seraphina at his side. The disclosure is possessive and meant to wound Garran as well as establish Lucien's claim, but Lucien does not speak for Seraphina's feelings toward Garran or deny her independent choices. Garran now knows Seraphina and Lucien are lovers and that Lucien intends to build some larger enterprise with her, but he still does not know Lucien originally abducted her, the blood-oath, the prospective-queen title, or the exact nature and scale of the new coven.
