# Current scene

In a cramped passage off Bellwater's crowded night market, Sir Garran Vale has warned Seraphina that someone supplied him with a description of her and falsely associated her with drained deaths, apparently trying to turn him into a weapon against her. Their private discussion became physically intimate before Lucien arrived: Seraphina stepped into Garran's space, placed her hands on his chest and leaned against him, while Garran admitted he cares for her and wants to touch and kiss her but insisted she tell him what she wants. Lucien arrived before she answered, revealed their engagement to Garran, and later watched Garran leave after Seraphina firmly confirmed, "I am," when asked whether she would marry Lucien. Once Garran was gone, Lucien confronted Seraphina over finding her physically close to Garran. He caught her by the hair in a recognized vampiric demand for deference, escalated the grip almost to pain, and pinned her against the wall when she initially said she could not explain. Seraphina yielded rather than fighting and admitted she did not know whether she wanted Garran to kiss her; she explained that she had wanted Garran to rescue her from her former coven, Garran rejected her, and Lucien offered what she wanted, so she chose Lucien. When Lucien pressed whether she would still choose him if Garran now offered everything she once wanted, Seraphina answered unequivocally that she would choose Lucien every time and called her old rescue-dream with Garran "a fantasy a desperate woman conjured." Seraphina handed Lucien the anonymous message. Lucien then warned her, "If I find you with him again I will kill him and make you watch," a deliberate threat against Garran and coercive warning to Seraphina. After considering the message and the unresolved watchers, Lucien returns his attention to Seraphina and says, "Ah. And so you remember who you belong to." He produces a small box and orders, "Open it." He steps back enough that Seraphina is no longer crushed against the wall. As she takes and opens the box, Lucien carefully rearranges the honey-blonde hair he had pulled out of place. Inside is an enormous gem-encrusted ring, presented in the context of their betrothal. The ring's exact gemstone, metal, provenance, monetary value, symbolism, and whether Seraphina puts it on are not yet established. The exact written wording of the anonymous message also remains unestablished beyond Garran's report that it supplied enough description to identify Seraphina and falsely linked her to three drained deaths. The two suspected watchers remain unidentified.
