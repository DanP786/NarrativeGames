# Current scene

After being forcibly recovered by the Pale Court near the hill-road priory, Seraphina is taken back to the Pale Court stronghold. Lady Yseld Veyr has removed her from the Hadrik investigation and assigned another Pale Court operative to continue it. Back at the Pale Court, the household absorbs Seraphina's return into routine, restores her black-and-pale-blue formal appearance, keeps her original daggers, and treats her as though she has resumed her old place despite her explicit rejection of its claim on her loyalty.

At the Pale Court ball, Yseld assigns Seraphina to Lord Othmar Rell, a wealthy mortal magnate whose river toll rights, grain warehouses, and wagon contracts make him commercially useful. Seraphina resumes the pleasant, gracious social persona expected of her, dances with Othmar, and gives him the familiar cultivated story she has historically used with politically useful men: sheltered, modest, flattered by worldly attention, shaping the interaction so that any later intimacy he seeks feels to him mutually anticipated rather than imposed duty. Othmar accepts the performance and grows increasingly comfortable and personally interested.

In a quieter gallery, Lucien Veyne appears and calls Seraphina "little bunny." When Othmar asks whether they are acquainted, Lucien answers publicly, "She is my betrothed." The declaration exposes a major secret Seraphina had withheld from Yseld. Seraphina immediately checks who might have heard. She dismisses Othmar; he withdraws toward Lady Yseld. Seraphina urgently tells Lucien, "Take me away from here. Now." Warning bells sound as they attempt to leave.

Seraphina and Lucien run through the Pale Court passages toward an exterior door. Lucien says he brought horses. Two Pale Court vampires block the route, one armed with a sword. The familiar vampire is Sven. After Seraphina begs him to let her go, Sven demands that she say whether leaving is truly her choice. Seraphina pleads, "I want to go! You know what she makes me do! Please."

The reference to Yseld's treatment lands personally with Sven. It establishes that Sven has enough prior awareness of the way Yseld has used Seraphina to understand what she means without further explanation. Sven chooses Seraphina's stated will over immediate obedience: he physically checks his armed companion and gives Seraphina and Lucien a narrow opportunity to pass toward the exterior door. He does not join their escape and does not openly declare allegiance against the Pale Court; his choice is limited to letting Seraphina make her own departure. Other Pale Court vampires are approaching from behind, so the opening is brief.

Othmar has not yet been established as speaking to Yseld. Yseld has not yet been established as hearing Lucien's betrothal claim or identifying him as the unknown vampire Seraphina protected during interrogation, but the warning bells mean Pale Court personnel are reacting to the attempted departure. Lucien's exact entry method, preparations, companions, and external escape arrangements remain unestablished. Seraphina is unarmed; her original daggers remain in Pale Court possession.

Lord Othmar knows that Lucien directly claims Seraphina as his betrothed, though he still does not know the deeper allegiance, blood oath, House Veyne, Garran, or Hadrik history. Lucien knows Seraphina's former maker used her body for political and social sexual service and has explicitly promised never to require her to give sexual access to another for political utility. He does not yet have established knowledge that Yseld assigned Seraphina to Othmar for the evening or what she expected from that assignment.

The unresolved rupture remains: Seraphina has explicitly told Yseld that her loyalty ended when Yseld began giving her sexually to useful men, while Yseld continues to regard those assignments as legitimate coven duty. Her engagement with Lucien has not formally ended; Lucien still possesses the ring, and Seraphina does not know whether she wants it returned.
