# Current scene

8 November 2142, morning. After a month of consistent compliance with Axiom Meridian's work and wellness programme, Mara Vey reports to the Employee Health clinic at Central Campus for her first-month physical. Her work and residential routines have become familiar, her exercise tolerance has improved, and she continues to receive folate under preventative reproductive-health support. In a private examination room, Mara changes into the provided gown and sits on the examination couch. Dr Iona Senn begins the consultation. Mara reports no unusual symptoms and says she is sleeping better than she has in some time. Senn notes the improvement. Mara agrees to blood-pressure measurement but asks why reproductive health is reviewed and how it can be tracked. Senn explains the general clinical rationale and sources of data: baseline blood chemistry, periodic hormone tests, menstrual history, nutrition and biometric trends; routine residential monitoring alone cannot directly establish fertility. She clarifies that Mara's record indicates preventive monitoring rather than a diagnosis and invites questions before proceeding.
