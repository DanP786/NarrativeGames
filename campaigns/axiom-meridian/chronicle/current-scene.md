# Current scene

8 November 2142, morning. After a month of consistent compliance with Axiom Meridian's work and wellness programme, Mara Vey reports to the Employee Health clinic at Central Campus for her first-month physical. Her work and residential routines have become familiar, her exercise tolerance has improved, and she continues to receive folate under preventative reproductive-health support. In a private examination room, Mara changes into the provided gown and sits on the examination couch. Dr Iona Senn begins the consultation. Mara reports no unusual symptoms and says she is sleeping better than she has in some time. Senn notes the improvement. Mara agrees to blood-pressure measurement but asks why reproductive health is reviewed and how it can be tracked. Senn explains the general clinical rationale and sources of data: baseline blood chemistry, periodic hormone tests, menstrual history, nutrition and biometric trends; routine residential monitoring alone cannot directly establish fertility. She clarifies that Mara's record indicates preventive monitoring rather than a diagnosis. When Mara asks whether folate is birth control, Senn explains clearly that folate is vitamin B9 and is not contraceptive; it supports normal cellular functions and early fetal development if pregnancy occurs, and the preventative allocation is not evidence of a diagnosis or a pregnancy. Mara says she uses no contraception, noting that work and wellness schedules have left little time to socialise or form relationships. Senn acknowledges the difference between scheduled health routines and a personal life, does not assume Mara's sexual history, and begins the blood-pressure check while asking whether Mara wants to discuss the lack of free time as a wellbeing concern. Mara becomes embarrassed and dismisses the question; Senn accepts this without pressing, records no requested social-wellbeing intervention, and moves on to heart and lung examination. Mara says she has never felt faint during a blood test. Senn collects a routine venous sample without incident. Mara steps onto the measurement platform, which records her weight, posture and body-composition trends. Senn notes modest improvements consistent with the month of nutrition and exercise. Senn then proposes a pelvic ultrasound, beginning abdominally and discussing any further exam before consent. Mara asks whether she should have a smear test, having never had one. Senn explains cervical screening differs from ultrasound; Mara reports no relevant symptoms and agrees to the ultrasound. Senn completes the consented external abdominal ultrasound, reporting no obvious structural abnormalities and no reason for an internal scan. Endocrine results are within expected ranges. Mara agrees to hear the final preventative recommendations. Senn recommends continuing the existing nutrition, sleep and exercise programme, explains the folate allocation remains preventive and not a contraceptive, and presents an optional employee social-wellbeing resource without enrolling Mara. Mara asks for the information; Senn sends a voluntary social programme overview to her personal portal, with no enrollment, and concludes the appointment.
