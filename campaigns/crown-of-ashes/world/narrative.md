# Narrative

King Edric II died and Ardelane's succession fractured at his funeral. Lady Elowen Vale entered the Cathedral of the Nine Bells as a politically undereducated noble daughter and emerged from its violence as House Vale's surviving child after her brother Rowan used her as cover, charged Lord Cassian Veyne, and was killed by him. Prince Alaric also died, leaving Cassian Veyne and Prince Oren as the principal rival claimants.

The old Vale-Veyne feud did not prevent necessity from drawing Elowen and Cassian together. Shared danger at Greyfen and the tollhouse forced operational trust; Elowen helped save Cassian's life, and Cassian protected her from damaging rumors about the expedition. Cassian then proposed a dynastic marriage through Garrick Vale. The process angered Elowen because her wishes were not centered, but the settlement proved politically strongest for House Vale. Elowen ultimately chose the Veyne settlement over Prince Adrian's alternative, and she and Cassian married on the 16th of Deepfrost. What began as a coercive political bargain has become a deeply affectionate marriage and a genuine political partnership, though both still confront the unequal and hostile origins of the match.

Elowen has grown into an active political actor without becoming a conventional court schemer. She cultivates noblewomen through hospitality, listening and arguments about stability and continuity. Lady Aramelle Sorne revealed the western houses' central fear: houses that opposed or hesitated during the succession may be punished once a king is crowned. Elowen carried that insight to Cassian without promising pardons, offices or immunity. At Prince Oren's ball, Cassian and Oren publicly agreed that supporting the losing claimant during an unsettled succession should not automatically become treason afterward. Cassian deliberately spread that reassurance among undecided nobles, giving the western houses a credible principle around which to move.

Months without a crowned king have pushed Ardelane toward institutional strain and popular rebellion. Elowen independently opened friendly diplomatic channels to eight foreign courts through correspondence, gifts and entertainers used as informal cultural envoys. Two courts have sent meaningful written support for Cassian's claim; the identities, exact language and positions of the remaining six are not established. Elowen made no binding concessions. Cassian sees the foreign support as useful but dangerous if Oren can portray him as foreign-imposed. Elowen also instructed her entertainers only to observe naturally visible signs of foreign mobilization or hostile sentiment; they are not to conduct invasive espionage.

At Oren's ball, Prince Adrian covertly passed Elowen a note warning that the council is closer to choosing a king than either claimant believes and that unnamed men are trying to force the matter before winter worsens. Elowen disclosed the warning to Cassian at the first safe opportunity and gave him the note once home. Adrian's source, motive, the identity of the men, the mechanism of pressure and which claimant the council favors remain unknown. Cassian intends to verify the warning rather than trust or dismiss it.

A separate security breach inside Veyne House was solved: Lady Sabine Orrell, Cassian's former lover, bribed a junior laundress with legitimate linen access to place a dagger at Elowen's pillow as an intimidation. Sabine is secured and House Orrell denies prior knowledge. Final punishment is unresolved; a negotiated removal from Veyrhold, potentially through a distant marriage arranged with full disclosure, remains possible. The broader Greyfell/courier/priest conspiracy remains unresolved. A historical payment to a man later linked to the attacks was made through a House Dacre household account, but this is not proof House Dacre ordered the violence.

Elowen and Cassian now confront the central problem together: how to win a crown without teaching half the realm that defeat means destruction. Their private relationship mirrors the same question of power. Cassian has admitted that fear can turn protection into control; Elowen insists on being heard and retaining agency. After Oren's ball, Cassian explicitly tells Elowen that his pre-marriage distrust has changed: he trusts her to tell him difficult truths even when he may dislike them.
