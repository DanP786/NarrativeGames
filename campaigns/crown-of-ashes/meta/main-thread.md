# Main thread

- Status: active
- Central question: What does loyalty cost when family, survival, power, and forbidden attachment demand incompatible things?
- PC want: Elowen wants to remain useful to and loved by House Vale by fulfilling the place her family taught her to occupy, even as that place changes from ornamental daughter to political heir.
- Antagonist / obstacle: The royal succession has fractured after Alaric's death. Cassian Veyne and Prince Oren are rival claimants. House Vale's military and political weight makes its allegiance highly valuable. Rowan's death has made Elowen Garrick's acknowledged heir, turning her person, judgment, and eventual marriage into dynastic prizes.
- Active pressure: Garrick is using the thirty-day Vale-Veyne restraint to train Elowen, secure her succession, and assess both claimants without committing. Vale bannermen have sworn to uphold Elowen, but some openly doubt her sex, temperament, and inexperience. Oren and Cassian both have reasons to test whether Elowen can influence Garrick or be influenced herself.
- Romance vector: Elowen and Cassian remain genuine enemies divided by inherited feud, Rowan's killing, and Cassian's earlier use of Elowen as leverage. Political necessity is forcing repeated proximity and mutual observation before trust or affection is plausible; neither the killing nor the coercion will be erased. No marriage between them has been proposed.
