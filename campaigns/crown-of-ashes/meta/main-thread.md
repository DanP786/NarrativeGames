# Main thread

- Status: active
- Central question: What does loyalty cost when family, survival, power, and forbidden attachment demand incompatible things?
- PC want: Elowen wants to remain useful to and loved by House Vale by fulfilling the place her family taught her to occupy, even as that place changes from ornamental daughter to political heir.
- Antagonist / obstacle: The royal succession has fractured after Alaric's death. Cassian Veyne and Prince Oren are rival claimants. House Vale's military and political weight makes its allegiance highly valuable. Rowan's death made Elowen Garrick's acknowledged heir, turning her person, judgment, and eventual marriage into dynastic prizes. The tollhouse evidence shows an organized coercion/intelligence network actively mapping and manipulating neutral houses.
- Active pressure: Neutrality has failed as protection. Cassian has formally proposed marrying Elowen as the core of a Vale-Veyne settlement: Vale would support his claim; he would permanently recognize Elowen's legitimacy/present succession, preserve Vale's lands, offices, internal command, and fortresses, and negotiate succession protections keeping Vale institutionally distinct. Garrick has accepted the marriage in principle but not yet finalized the betrothal. He intends to demand binding articles protecting Elowen's property, Vale autonomy, his own right to remarry and produce lawful heirs, and the rights of any future Vale children. Adrian/Oren remains the principal alternative, meaning rejection risks pushing Vale into the opposing camp and war with Cassian.
- Romance vector: Elowen and Cassian remain emotionally unresolved enemies. Their journey and priory confinement created reluctant trust, attraction, familiarity, and dry humor, but Cassian has now used his superior political position to propose marriage directly to Garrick as statecraft. Garrick's acceptance in principle converts their emerging private softness into coercive dynastic pressure. The marriage is intended to occur before love or reconciliation, creating prolonged forced proximity and a likely emotional setback: Cassian can value/protect Elowen while still being the man whose political leverage leaves her little practical room to refuse.
