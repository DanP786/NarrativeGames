# Main thread

- Status: active
- Central question: What does loyalty cost when family, survival, power, and forbidden attachment demand incompatible things?
- PC want: Elowen wants to remain useful to and loved by House Vale by fulfilling the place her family taught her to occupy, even as that place changes from ornamental daughter to political heir.
- Antagonist / obstacle: The royal succession has fractured after Alaric's death. Cassian Veyne and Prince Oren are rival claimants. House Vale's military and political weight makes its allegiance highly valuable. Rowan's death made Elowen Garrick's acknowledged heir, turning her person, judgment, and eventual marriage into dynastic prizes. The tollhouse evidence now shows an organized coercion/intelligence network actively mapping and manipulating neutral houses.
- Active pressure: The thirty-day Vale-Veyne restraint expired while Elowen and Cassian were isolated at the priory. Their return to Veyrhold does not restore neutrality automatically. Rival alignments are hardening and Vale's continued neutrality increasingly invites coercion rather than buying safety. Garrick is considering two dynastic tools simultaneously: remarriage to create additional future Vale heirs as a long-term hedge, and Elowen's marriage as the only immediate way to lock Vale into a survivable political settlement. A hypothetical future child cannot command Vale now and could itself create a succession faction against Elowen.
- Romance vector: Elowen and Cassian remain genuine political enemies divided by inherited feud, Rowan's killing, and Cassian's willingness to use dynastic leverage. Their isolated journey and several days at the priory created reluctant trust, familiarity, and the first unguarded humor, but not love or reconciliation. The next phase will convert forced physical proximity into dynastic pressure: Cassian has strong strategic reason to prevent Elowen marrying into Oren's coalition and to secure Vale before Garrick can restructure his dynasty. Any Cassian-Elowen marriage should arise from unequal political necessity before either is in love, preserving resentment and coercion after the wedding rather than resolving the romance.
