- Time: evening, 24th of Harvestwane, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged
- Situation: Five days have passed since Oren's marriage proposal. Rowan has been buried. Elowen has spent the intervening days accompanying Garrick to meetings and beginning an exhausting political education while House Vale remains publicly neutral. ## Recent beats
- Rowan Vale died in the cathedral melee after charging Cassian Veyne, who killed him in front of Elowen. Prince Alaric also died, leaving the royal succession open.
- Garrick told Elowen that Rowan's death leaves her his provisional heir and began educating her in politics and governance.
- Garrick and Cassian agreed to seven days of mutual restraint in which neither house attacks the other and House Vale declares for no claimant.
- Prince Oren asserted a rival claim and offered a marriage between Elowen and his younger brother Adrian in exchange for an eventual Vale alliance and guarantees concerning Elowen's inheritance.
- Cassian later offered to extend the Vale-Veyne restraint for thirty days. If Garrick publicly names Elowen his lawful heir, Cassian promises recognition of her succession and lawful issue. In exchange, Vale must remain neutral between Cassian and Oren for thirty days and give prior notice before a marriage compact binds Elowen to either claimant's immediate family.
- Garrick intends to accept the core thirty-day bargain but counters with a reciprocal marriage-notice obligation if Cassian seeks a major marriage alliance of his own.
- Elowen asks about the marriage market for Adrian and Cassian and recognizes that she herself is now one of the strategically significant heiresses. Garrick explains that Cassian may or may not personally desire marriage, but a claimant seeking the Crown must eventually consider legitimate succession.
- Elowen asks directly who she is to marry. Garrick answers that he has not chosen a husband and will not commit her while the succession remains unresolved. He explains his criteria: her husband must strengthen House Vale without swallowing it, accept that Elowen is the Vale heir rather than merely transferring Vale lands into his own house, provide useful protection or alliance, and not place House Vale irretrievably on the losing side of the civil struggle. Garrick states plainly that Elowen will not have unrestricted choice, but because she must live with the political consequences and potentially rule beside the man, he now intends to hear her judgment before making a final decision. Adrian remains a possibility rather than a commitment; no other candidate is yet established.
