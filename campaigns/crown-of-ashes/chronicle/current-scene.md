## Recent beats
- Cassian proposes marriage directly to Garrick rather than courting Elowen, emphasizing the real dynastic power imbalance. He says Elowen would inherit Vale in her own right and that marriage would not automatically make him Lord Vale.
- Cassian proposes that if he wins the Crown, a first surviving son of the marriage would inherit the Crown and a second child would be intended for Vale, with succession protections negotiated in advance if there is only one child. His stated rationale is preventing the Crown from simply absorbing Vale by inheritance.
- Garrick asks what happens if he himself remarries and later produces additional legitimate heirs, forcing Cassian to address the fact that Elowen's present succession is not necessarily permanent under Vale's own dynastic structure.
- Cassian answers that he will not demand Garrick surrender his lawful right to remarry or produce children. He distinguishes recognition of Elowen's current status from an attempt to freeze Vale's internal succession forever.
- Cassian's proposed safeguard: the marriage settlement must define Elowen's position at the time of marriage and prevent a later Garrick marriage from being manipulated by outside factions to dispossess her arbitrarily. If Vale law gives a later legitimate son superior succession rights, Cassian will require substantial irrevocable lands/income/status for Elowen and their children rather than pretending the issue does not exist.
- Cassian also points out that a future infant Vale son would create a vulnerable regency and another target for the coercion network; Garrick's remarriage is therefore a long-term dynastic hedge, not an immediate substitute for political settlement.
- Garrick recognizes that Cassian has no incentive to forbid his remarriage outright: additional Vale heirs could preserve the house as a distinct line, but they could also become future rivals to Elowen/Cassian's children. This issue becomes a central marriage-contract negotiation rather than a plot loophole.
- Elowen remains present and outwardly composed while her future inheritance, marriage, and hypothetical children are negotiated. No betrothal has yet been agreed and her private reaction remains unspoken.
