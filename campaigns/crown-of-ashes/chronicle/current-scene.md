# Current scene

- Location: Vale residence in Veyrhold — Lord Garrick's solar
- Time: morning, 7th of Emberfall, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged
- Situation: Garrick is teaching Elowen to turn gathered intelligence into decisions. He has asked which item Vale should act on first and how to do so without revealing what Vale knows.

## Recent beats
- Garrick classifies Elowen's report: Cassian is a direct but deliberately messaging source; Corren and Dacre are leads requiring corroboration; Merrow's supposed desire for office is currently inference. He praises Elowen for preserving how she knows each claim.
- Garrick warns that Adrian's informal courtship and repeated public dances can generate political expectations even without promises, but does not object to Elowen permitting the courtship.
- Asked which intelligence Vale should act upon first, Elowen tentatively chooses Corren because the claim came through multiple contacts and reasons that Corren may itself be speaking openly enough for the inclination to spread.
- Elowen then challenges the premise of Garrick's question: she is unsure why Vale should act on any of the intelligence beyond verifying it.
- Garrick approves the distinction. He explains that verification is itself an action and that weak intelligence often should not trigger policy. Acting too quickly on rumor can expose Vale's sources, create commitments, or allow another house to manipulate Vale with planted information.
- Garrick says Corren is therefore a sensible first verification target: Vale can seek ordinary contact on border-security or trade matters, observe whether Corren frames Veyne as the preferred guarantor, and compare that with independent reports without directly asking 'Have you chosen Veyne?'
- He contrasts this with Merrow: testing Merrow's supposed appetite for office too directly could reveal Vale is shopping for his price or invite Merrow to inflate it. Dacre can be watched and corroborated without immediate approach.
- Garrick's lesson is that 'act' does not mean intervene politically; sometimes the correct action is to verify, watch, or deliberately do nothing until information crosses a threshold.
