# Current scene

- Location: House Vale's Veyrhold residence — Garrick's solar
- Time: evening, 19th of Harvestwane, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged
- Situation: After Prince Oren's afternoon presentation to the great houses, Garrick and Elowen return to the Vale residence. Oren's faction has proposed marriage between Elowen and Oren's younger brother, Lord Adrian. Garrick is using the offer as Elowen's first direct lesson in political analysis.

## Recent beats
- Rowan Vale died in the cathedral melee after charging Cassian Veyne, who killed him in front of Elowen. Prince Alaric was also killed, leaving the royal succession open.
- Garrick told Elowen that Rowan's death leaves her his provisional heir and that she must begin learning politics and governance.
- At a parley, Garrick and Cassian agreed to seven days of mutual restraint in which neither house attacks the other and House Vale declares for no claimant.
- Prince Oren entered Veyrhold and asserted a rival claim. He deliberately acknowledged Elowen as Garrick's surviving child, signaling awareness of House Vale's succession problem.
- That evening Oren proposed Elowen marry his younger brother, Lord Adrian, with Crown recognition of Elowen's inheritance in exchange for an eventual Vale alliance.
- Garrick asks Elowen what Oren is truly buying rather than what the proposal explicitly promises. Elowen identifies Vale resources and the legitimizing effect of Garrick's public support. Garrick explains that Oren is also trying to secure Elowen before another claimant can use her marriage to gain House Vale.
- Elowen independently notices that accepting Adrian during the seven-day restraint would conflict with Garrick's agreement with Cassian. Garrick confirms this and explains that even a secret acceptance would make Vale appear unreliable if discovered.
- Elowen then reasons through the larger risk herself: marriage to Adrian is advantageous if Oren becomes king, but if Oren loses to Cassian, the marriage would bind Elowen and House Vale directly to the defeated royal faction and could expose them to Cassian's retaliation. Garrick confirms that this is precisely why he will not gamble Elowen and House Vale on Oren while the succession remains unsettled. He explains that Cassian might not automatically destroy every relative of Oren, because a victorious ruler must distinguish useful submission from active rebellion, but Adrian as Oren's brother would be especially dangerous to be married to if Oren loses. Garrick's respect for Elowen's developing political reasoning increases.
