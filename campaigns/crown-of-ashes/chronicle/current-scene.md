# Current scene

- Location: House Vale's Veyrhold residence — music room
- Time: evening, 24th of Harvestwane, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged
- Situation: Five days have passed since Oren's marriage proposal. Rowan has been buried. Elowen has spent the intervening days accompanying Garrick to meetings and beginning an exhausting political education while House Vale remains publicly neutral. The seven-day restraint with Veyne expires in two days. Cassian Veyne has sent a formal proposal concerning renewal of the restraint and Elowen's succession.

## Recent beats
- Rowan Vale died in the cathedral melee after charging Cassian Veyne, who killed him in front of Elowen. Prince Alaric also died, leaving the royal succession open.
- Garrick told Elowen that Rowan's death leaves her his provisional heir and began educating her in politics and governance.
- Garrick and Cassian agreed to seven days of mutual restraint in which neither house attacks the other and House Vale declares for no claimant.
- Prince Oren asserted a rival claim and offered a marriage between Elowen and his younger brother Adrian in exchange for an eventual Vale alliance and guarantees concerning Elowen's inheritance.
- Elowen recognized both that accepting during the restraint would violate the substance of Garrick's agreement with Cassian and that marriage to Adrian could endanger House Vale if Oren ultimately lost. Garrick declined to commit to Oren during the unsettled succession.
- Over the following five days Rowan is formally buried. Elowen attends in mourning and continues to suffer intrusive memories of his death, though she performs the expected public rituals without incident.
- Garrick begins bringing Elowen to selected meetings. She remains inexperienced and quiet but is gradually learning to connect bargains, alliances, and dynastic consequences rather than treating each conversation in isolation.
- Cassian offers to extend the Vale-Veyne mutual restraint for thirty days. If Garrick publicly names Elowen his lawful heir, Cassian promises Veyne and prospective Crown recognition of her succession and lawful issue. In exchange, Vale must remain neutral between Cassian and Oren for thirty days and enter no marriage compact binding Elowen to either claimant's immediate family without first notifying Veyne.
- Elowen asks why her marriage concerns Cassian. Garrick explains that as Vale's heir her husband could gain influence over Vale lands, descendants, bannermen, and armies; Cassian wants warning before marriage turns Vale neutrality into another faction's strength.
- Elowen then asks why Garrick would agree to continued neutrality when Cassian killed Rowan. Garrick answers that he has not forgiven Cassian and may never do so, but Rowan charged Cassian against Garrick's explicit order and died in armed combat. Garrick refuses to sacrifice Elowen, House Vale's soldiers, and the house itself merely to make his grief feel like vengeance. He distinguishes forgiveness from strategy: neutrality preserves Vale while Cassian and Oren expose their strengths, and Cassian's offer also secures recognition of Elowen's succession without binding her immediately in marriage. Garrick admits that if a safe opportunity for justice or vengeance later presents itself he may take it, but he will not pursue revenge at the price of House Vale's survival.
