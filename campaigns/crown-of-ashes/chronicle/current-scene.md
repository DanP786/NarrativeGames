# Current scene

- Location: House Vale's Veyrhold residence — Garrick's solar
- Time: late morning, 4th of Emberfall, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged
- Situation: The ambush evidence review has broadened into Garrick teaching Elowen how Vale's neutrality functions as leverage between Oren and Cassian.

## Recent beats
- Garrick explains that Vale is waiting to learn each claimant's coalition, governing capacity, behavior under pressure, and likely treatment of Vale after victory, while also hardening Elowen's succession and Vale's exposed position.
- He says choosing early spends Vale's leverage: the chosen claimant has less reason to concede once Vale is committed, while the rejected claimant becomes an enemy. Waiting too long is also dangerous because Vale may become unnecessary or resented.
- Elowen asks what Oren and Cassian actually want from Vale beyond soldiers and grain, and how Vale can remain important while delaying its choice.
- Garrick explains that both claimants want legitimacy as much as military strength. Vale's endorsement could influence undecided lords, creditors, clergy, city officials, and lesser houses because Garrick is regarded as powerful enough that his choice would be interpreted as evidence about who is likely to prevail. They also want Vale's roads, fortifications, revenues, bannermen, and—through Elowen—dynastic alignment and future inheritance.
- Garrick identifies Elowen's marriage as one of Vale's largest unspent political assets now that she is heir: marrying her into a faction could bind Vale's future to it even without an immediate military declaration.
- To remain valuable while neutral, Garrick says Vale must be useful to the realm without becoming useful exclusively to either claimant: protect trade and food movement, mediate practical disputes, keep Vale forces intact, maintain communication with both camps, make limited bargains with defined boundaries, and demonstrate that Vale can solve problems neither claimant can solve alone.
- Garrick points to Elowen's grain-escort proposal as exactly the kind of neutral usefulness that increases Vale's importance—provided Vale survives the costs and does not let temporary service become permanent obligation.
- He warns that neutrality must look like strength and usefulness, not fear. If Vale merely refuses everything, the claimants will eventually work around it; if Vale becomes indispensable to civic stability while retaining its army and dynastic freedom, both sides must continue courting it.
