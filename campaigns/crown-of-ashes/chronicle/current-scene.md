# Current scene

- Location: House Vale's Veyrhold residence — great hall
- Time: evening, 26th of Harvestwane, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged
- Situation: Garrick has publicly proclaimed Elowen his lawful heir. After several Vale lords challenge her sex, demeanor, and suitability to rule, Garrick forcefully rejects their arguments and demands any man who prefers a distant male cousin state the rival claim openly before witnesses.

## Recent beats
- Garrick names Elowen his lawful heir before Vale lords gathered in Veyrhold. Elowen stands beside him rather than playing piano for the gathering as she ordinarily would have done.
- Lord Edric Harrow raises the existence of male Vale cousins. Another lord argues Elowen lacks the demeanor to govern; a third suggests she should marry a capable man who could effectively manage House Vale through her.
- Elowen averts her gaze and clasps her hands, reverting visibly to the deferential demeanor in which she was raised.
- Garrick states that Elowen's present demeanor is the result of his own choice to educate Rowan for rule and Elowen for ornament. He refuses to treat that upbringing as evidence she cannot learn.
- Garrick declares that Elowen's future husband will marry the heir of Vale, not replace her, and crudely rebukes his bannermen for preferring male authority merely because it is male.
- Elowen's cheeks redden visibly at her father's brash wording, though she remains silent and composed beside him.
- No lord accepts Garrick's invitation to openly name a distant male cousin with a superior claim. Lord Harrow eventually kneels and gives the required oath to recognize Elowen's succession. The other hesitant lords begin following, though their compliance does not establish genuine personal loyalty to Elowen.
