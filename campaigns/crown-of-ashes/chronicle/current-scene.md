# Current scene

- Location: Veyrhold, royal capital — Vale residence, Garrick's private solar
- Time: late morning, 12th of Frostwane, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged
- Situation: Cassian has formally proposed marrying Elowen as the basis of a durable Vale-Veyne settlement. Garrick has not accepted. The negotiation is now focused on whether the marriage protects Vale's independence or merely creates a path for Veyne/the Crown to absorb it.

## Recent beats
- Cassian proposes marriage to Elowen directly to Garrick as head of House Vale, not as romantic courtship. He argues that marrying Vale's acknowledged heir would make Vale part of the dynasty he is fighting to establish and give him direct reason to preserve Elowen's inheritance.
- Cassian proposes that Elowen remain heir in her own right, Vale lands/revenues/officers remain institutionally distinct, and he does not automatically become Lord Vale by marriage.
- On children, Cassian initially suggests a first surviving son could inherit the Crown if he wins it while a second child could inherit Vale, with protections negotiated if there is only one child. This is a proposed settlement term, not established law or final agreement.
- Garrick asks the reciprocal succession question: what happens if Cassian later has heirs outside Elowen—whether from a later marriage after her death, an earlier/other lawful dynastic arrangement, or children whose claims could compete with hers.
- Cassian answers that Garrick would be foolish to sign anything dependent on Cassian's future goodwill. He is prepared to bind the settlement in the marriage contract: Elowen's Vale inheritance remains hers and cannot be displaced by any child of Cassian who is not also Elowen's child; no future Veyne/Crown heir from another union gains a Vale claim through Cassian alone.
- Cassian refuses to promise the impossible that he can never have another lawful marriage under any circumstance (e.g. Elowen's death without surviving issue) or that Crown succession can ignore future political/legal realities. Instead he offers enforceable separation between Vale inheritance and his independent dynastic heirs.
- Garrick also raises the risk that if Elowen bears only daughters while Cassian later fathers a son elsewhere, that son could become Crown heir and later pressure Vale. Cassian accepts this as a genuine future risk and offers contractual/baronial guarantees for Vale's separate succession rather than pretending biology or politics can be controlled.
- Garrick remains deeply hostile and unconvinced; the point of this questioning is to make the proposed marriage structurally durable rather than rely on Cassian's affection or promises. No betrothal has yet been agreed.
- Elowen remains present and outwardly dutiful while the terms governing her future marriage and children are negotiated around her. She may speak if she chooses.
