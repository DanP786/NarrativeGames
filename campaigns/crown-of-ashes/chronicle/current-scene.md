# Current scene

- Location: House Merrow's Veyrhold townhouse — refreshments at the ballroom edge
- Time: evening, 6th of Emberfall, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged
- Situation: After visible dances with Julian Merrow and Adrian, Elowen is speaking directly with Cassian Veyne. Their conversation has shifted from politics into music, creating unexpectedly personal ground between members of hostile houses.

## Recent beats
- Cassian explains that he attends balls to observe alliances people reveal through social choices. He then asks Elowen whether she has always enjoyed balls.
- Elowen says she loves the music. By ingrained courtly habit she lightly teases Cassian when he asks what she plays, then visibly tenses at having used the familiar flirtatious manner with him and concedes that she plays piano.
- Cassian does not embarrass her over the tease. He asks whether she plays because noblewomen are expected to or because she genuinely enjoys it.
- Elowen's breath catches at the apparently nonpolitical question. She answers candidly that she loves to play because soldiers do not lose their lives if she plays poorly, implicitly connecting music to the recent consequences of her political decisions.
- Cassian cannot know Elowen's private suspicion about what he hopes to gain from the questions, but he can hear the guilt and burden contained in her explicit comparison.
- Cassian responds without absolving her or exploiting the admission. He says that command has consequences but distinguishes causing an ambush from making the decision that placed soldiers on the road. He notes that rulers who cannot bear the cost of decisions become useless, while rulers who cease to feel the cost become dangerous.
- The conversation gives Cassian a small amount of genuine personal insight into Elowen while giving Elowen an unsolicited glimpse of his philosophy of command rather than a direct factional talking point.
