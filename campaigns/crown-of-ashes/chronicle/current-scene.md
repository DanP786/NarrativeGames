# Current scene

- Location: House Merrow's Veyrhold townhouse — ballroom and adjoining reception rooms
- Time: evening, 6th of Emberfall, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged
- Situation: Elowen attends a major court ball under Garrick's instruction to remain visibly neutral while observing who seeks her attention and what they reveal.

## Recent beats
- Lord Julian Merrow asks Elowen for the first dance. Elowen accepts but asks him to show her around first, deliberately using the softer alluring social manner her mother taught her.
- Julian responds by becoming more attentive and guides her through the public reception rooms, presenting House Merrow as neutral and civic-minded. He remarks that many supposedly neutral houses are waiting for Lord Vale to choose first.
- Elowen feigns political ignorance and asks Julian to explain where the houses stand and whether the succession is leaning toward one claimant. Her performance is socially plausible because her prior public role and genuine political inexperience support it, though her untrained persuasion means she does not completely control how Julian interprets the question.
- Pleased to be treated as knowledgeable, Julian gives her a broad court reading rather than confidential intelligence. He says Oren has the advantage among traditional royalists, several court offices, and houses that prize direct royal blood; Cassian has stronger backing among harder military lords and those who believe Oren lacks the force to secure the realm. Several substantial houses remain undeclared, with Vale the most consequential among them and Merrow publicly neutral.
- Julian says neither claimant has a decisive advantage yet. Oren looks more naturally legitimate to some observers, while Cassian increasingly looks more capable of forcing a settlement if the contest turns violent. Julian carefully avoids naming which claimant his father privately prefers, despite Elowen's invitation to generalize about neutral houses.
- Elowen can observe that Julian enjoys explaining the contest and wants to appear informed, but becomes more careful whenever the conversation approaches House Merrow's own intended choice.
