# Current scene

- Location: Veyrhold, royal capital — Vale residence, Garrick's private solar
- Time: afternoon, 10th of Frostwane, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged
- Situation: Elowen and Cassian are back in Veyrhold after the priory. The thirty-day Vale-Veyne restraint has expired. Garrick has privately told Elowen that neutrality is no longer protecting Vale, that he is considering remarriage as a long-term dynastic hedge, and that Elowen's marriage must now be considered as an immediate political instrument.

## Recent beats
- Garrick explains that Elowen's attempted abduction demonstrated that hostile factions already treat control of her as control of Vale. Her status as Garrick's acknowledged adult heir makes her marriage immediately consequential in a way a hypothetical future child cannot match.
- Garrick says Oren's circle is formally pressing Adrian as a marriage candidate. He has received other approaches as well but has not yet named all of them.
- Elowen responds simply and deferentially, 'Yes, father.'
- Garrick does not mistake deference for informed consent. Consistent with his earlier instruction that Elowen must learn to understand decisions privately, he tells her he needs her judgment, not merely obedience, because she will have to live inside whatever alliance is made and may eventually rule Vale through it.
- Garrick identifies the immediate strategic problem with Adrian: marrying Adrian would be read as Vale joining Oren, ending any plausible neutrality and making Vale an enemy of Cassian if the succession turns violent.
- Garrick also recognizes that a marriage to a lesser neutral house may fail to provide enough protection against either claimant or the coercion network now targeting neutral houses.
- Garrick has not yet proposed Cassian as a groom in this beat. His extreme resentment over Rowan's death remains active, so accepting Cassian would require overwhelming strategic pressure rather than casual preference.
- Garrick's remarriage remains a genuine parallel option for future heirs, but it cannot replace the need for an immediate settlement because conception, birth, sex, survival, and eventual political capacity are uncertain and slow.
- The conversation is moving toward evaluating what kind of marriage can protect Vale now, setting up the political conditions under which an otherwise abhorrent Cassian marriage could become thinkable.
