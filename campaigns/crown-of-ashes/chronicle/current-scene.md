# Current scene

- Location: Veyrhold — House Veyne residence, wedding-planning room
- Time: afternoon, 8th of Deepfrost, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged
- Situation: Elowen and Cassian use the wedding seating plan as a political exercise. After confronting the dynastic consequences of Garrick remarrying to produce another Vale child, Elowen deliberately shifts perspective from Vale heir to Cassian's hypothetical wife and asks which house Garrick should marry into to best strengthen Cassian's succession.

## Recent beats
- Cassian suggested seating one of Elowen's young unmarried friends beside Garrick because she could be a plausible remarriage candidate. Elowen was appalled by the age difference. Cassian explained that Garrick's stated purpose in remarrying includes producing another lawful child, making a younger wife dynastically practical, while acknowledging that a woman's suitability and willingness are separate from succession utility.
- Cassian reminded Elowen that her wish to be displaced as Vale heir has consequences: another child requires Garrick to remarry, and that marriage affects another woman's life. Elowen sits silently with that consequence rather than dismissing it.
- Elowen then says: 'let’s pretend I’m your wife. And my duty is to you, not Vale. Which house would benefit Veyne aligning with Vale? For your succession'. This marks a deliberate analytical shift: she asks to reason from Veyne/Cassian's dynastic interests rather than her natal house's interests.
- Canon currently establishes Vale as a major independent house, Veyne as Cassian's claimant house, and Oren/Adrian as the principal rival coalition, but does not establish a complete roster of other great houses or their exact alignments. Cassian therefore should teach the selection criteria and may identify only houses already established in canon unless new houses are deliberately introduced as new canon.
- From Cassian's strategic perspective, the ideal Garrick remarriage would bind Vale to a house that adds something Veyne lacks without empowering Oren: military/geographic reach, grain/finance, capital access, or wavering legitimacy; it should preferably be neutral or weakly committed rather than a core Veyne ally (wasted gain) or hard Oren partisan (dangerous infiltration/conflict).
- The match must also avoid creating a future Vale succession faction hostile to Cassian/Elowen: any child of Garrick's new wife could become the Vale heir Elowen wants, so the mother's natal house would gain a long-term stake in Vale. Cassian will therefore value a house whose interests remain compatible with Veyne rule after that child inherits.
- Elowen's phrasing 'my duty is to you, not Vale' is hypothetical political role-play for the lesson, not an established renunciation of all loyalty to her birth house. Cassian should not mistake it for unconditional personal obedience.
