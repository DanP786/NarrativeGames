- Time: evening, 24th of Harvestwane, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged
- Situation: Five days have passed since Oren's marriage proposal. Rowan has been buried. Elowen has spent the intervening days accompanying Garrick to meetings and beginning an exhausting political education while House Vale remains publicly neutral. ## Recent beats
- Rowan Vale died in the cathedral melee after charging Cassian Veyne, who killed him in front of Elowen. Prince Alaric also died, leaving the royal succession open.
- Garrick told Elowen that Rowan's death leaves her his provisional heir and began educating her in politics and governance.
- Garrick and Cassian agreed to seven days of mutual restraint in which neither house attacks the other and House Vale declares for no claimant.
- Prince Oren asserted a rival claim and offered a marriage between Elowen and his younger brother Adrian in exchange for an eventual Vale alliance and guarantees concerning Elowen's inheritance.
- Elowen recognized both that accepting during the restraint would violate the substance of Garrick's agreement with Cassian and that marriage to Adrian could endanger House Vale if Oren ultimately lost. Garrick declined to commit to Oren during the unsettled succession.
- Cassian later offered to extend the Vale-Veyne restraint for thirty days. If Garrick publicly names Elowen his lawful heir, Cassian promises recognition of her succession and lawful issue. In exchange, Vale must remain neutral and give prior notice before a marriage compact binds Elowen to either claimant's immediate family.
- Garrick explained that Cassian's concern with Elowen's marriage is strategic: as Vale's heir, her husband could gain influence over Vale lands, descendants, bannermen, and armies.
- Elowen asked why Garrick would continue dealing with Cassian after Rowan's death. Garrick admitted his hatred and desire for vengeance but distinguished Rowan's death in armed combat from murder. He refuses to risk Elowen and House Vale in a blood feud merely to satisfy grief, though he may pursue vengeance later if it can be done without destroying the house.
- Elowen thanks Garrick and apologizes for questioning his judgment, reverting to the deference in which she was raised. Garrick explicitly tells her not to apologize for asking him questions: if she is to be his heir, he requires her to understand his decisions rather than merely obey them. He makes clear that this permission is not permission to undermine him publicly; disagreement belongs in private, while House Vale must present one will before outsiders.
