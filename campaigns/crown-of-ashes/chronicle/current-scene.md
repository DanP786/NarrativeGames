# Current scene

- Location: Veyrhold — Veyne House, dining room
- Time: evening, 13th of Deepfrost, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged, major relationship/power conflict
- Situation: Elowen and Cassian are at strained supper confronting the contradiction between his willingness to command her in matters he deems security-critical and his dislike of her ingrained, generalized obedience.

## Recent beats
- Elowen gives Cassian a strained 'Thank you' after he explains he believes he is presently the better judge of threats to her life. Internally, her gratitude is shaped by training that she should thank a man trying to keep her alive even while feeling controlled.
- Cassian hears the strain and initially tells her not to thank him 'like that.' After Elowen objects that he should not decide whether she means her own gratitude, he concedes the point.
- Cassian says Elowen does not owe him gratitude for the emergency order: he took power over her, believes the danger justified it, and would make the same decision again with current information, but she does not require his permission to be angry about it.
- Cassian observes that her strained thanks resembled the perfectly mannered, obedient manner she used when Garrick first told her she would marry Cassian. He says he does not want obedience merely because she was trained into it, though when Elowen asks whether he does want obedience when he decides he is right, he admits: 'In some matters, yes.'
- Elowen now confronts the contradiction directly: 'Do you want me to hate you or just simply confused? You make a decision for me expecting obedience then when I give you obedience you chastise me'
- Cassian should recognize this as a fair criticism. The distinction he is trying to draw is between compliance with a specific lawful/security command and habitual emotional/deferential submission in everything, but from Elowen's position that distinction is unstable because he holds the power to decide which matters qualify. He should not pretend the contradiction disappears merely by explaining it.
- Strong direction: Cassian can admit that he wants an unusually difficult combination—Elowen to resist/challenge him generally, yet obey in the narrow moments he believes immediate danger requires it—and that this places the burden on her to know when his command is 'the exception.' He may recognize that if he wants trust rather than conditioning, he must earn credibility and narrow/define the circumstances in which he can override her, even if he is not ready to surrender the emergency authority itself.
