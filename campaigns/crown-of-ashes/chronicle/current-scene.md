# Current scene

- Location: Palace of the White Hart, Veyrhold — west gallery
- Time: afternoon, 29th of Harvestwane, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged
- Situation: During a palace council recess, Prince Oren is testing Elowen's political judgment and whether she can be cultivated independently of Garrick.

## Recent beats
- Garrick and Cassian sealed a thirty-day restraint including Veyne recognition of Elowen's succession, Vale neutrality, and reciprocal advance notice of major marriage alliances.
- Garrick publicly named Elowen his lawful heir. Vale bannermen swore to uphold her succession despite some openly questioning her sex, demeanor, and inexperience.
- Oren approaches Elowen during a council recess and asks what House Vale requires from the eventual king. Elowen answers peace, then cautiously says Vale might lean toward whoever can secure peace without further bloodshed.
- Oren asks whether she would apply that standard equally to him and Cassian, despite Cassian having killed Rowan. Elowen says Garrick would not risk her for justice for Rowan.
- Oren then asks whether Elowen herself could live with Cassian crowned king if that were the price of peace.
- Elowen visibly struggles and answers only that Veyne and Vale have been rivals for generations. Oren recognizes that she has not answered yes or no. He interprets the response publicly only as evidence that the feud predates Rowan and cannot be reduced to one killing; he cannot know her private feelings.
- Oren presses no further on Rowan in that moment. Instead, he tells Elowen that inherited enmity is precisely why a claimant must know whether Vale neutrality can ever become support, and suggests that old rivalries can either be ended by settlement or become excuses for another generation of bloodshed.
