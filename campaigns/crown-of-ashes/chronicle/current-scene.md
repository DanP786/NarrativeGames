- Location: small roadside priory south-east of the old tollhouse — infirmary
- Time: around midday, 30th of Emberfall, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged
- Situation: Cassian survived severe blood loss and rests under the physician's care. Elowen sits beside him in borrowed priory clothing with the recovered tollhouse documents. Lord Tomas Halvern remains nearby in the priory.

## Recent beats
- The recovered documents show a broader intelligence/coercion operation tracking politically uncommitted houses. One fragment orders the 'Vale article' preserved for transfer and the 'Veyne obstruction' removed; another shows payments routed through intermediaries with a Blackwater reference; a list tracks Vale, Halvern, and other houses with annotations about alignment, marriage, pressure, and correspondence.
- A repeated cipher/mark appears across several documents, but its identity remains unknown. The papers do not conclusively identify the mastermind or clear all claimant factions.
- Cassian wakes enough for brief conversation. Elowen jokes that the papers are a marriage proposal from Lady Fiona, whom Cassian danced with at Merrow's ball; Cassian answers the teasing in kind. When pain catches him trying to shift, Elowen orders him not to move and he complies.
- Elowen turns serious and says the saved papers do not give them much beyond showing that the conspirators wanted to pressure her into a marriage.
- Cassian challenges the narrow reading. He points out that 'marriage pressure viable' appears as an intelligence annotation, not necessarily a fixed order to marry Elowen to one identified man. Whoever built the list is cataloguing pressure points: marriage is one lever over Vale, just as stolen correspondence is a lever over Halvern.
- Cassian emphasizes that Elowen's abduction would let the conspirators manufacture marriage pressure in several ways: compel her consent, threaten Garrick until he agrees, place her with a politically useful husband, or merely create rumors/compromise that narrow her acceptable matches. The documents do not establish which method or groom was intended.
- Cassian notes that this makes the absence of a groom's name significant: the operation may value control over Vale's marriage choice more than any single marriage. He advises treating any sudden proposal or faction claiming it can 'protect' Elowen after the attack as potentially informative rather than automatically complicit.
- Cassian remains weak and confined to bed; his analysis is lucid but physically taxing. Elowen retains control of the documents.
