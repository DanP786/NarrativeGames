# Current scene

- Location: House Merrow's Veyrhold townhouse — refreshments at the ballroom edge
- Time: evening, 6th of Emberfall, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: charged
- Situation: Elowen and Cassian are having their first sustained conversation without negotiators or armed men. Their exchange has become a direct political assessment of what Cassian wants from Vale and why Elowen's marriage matters to him.

## Recent beats
- Cassian clarifies that the thirty-day Vale neutrality is his minimum acceptable outcome, not his preferred one. He ultimately wants Vale's soldiers, legitimacy, and active support, but judged that demanding allegiance immediately after Rowan's death would be strategically impossible and counterproductive.
- Cassian says he would prefer Garrick and eventually Elowen to choose him because they judge him the safer or better king for Vale, producing a more durable settlement than coerced allegiance.
- Elowen asks why Cassian wanted notice of her marriage prospects rather than demanding that she accept no proposal during the thirty-day period.
- Cassian explains that Elowen's marriage can alter Vale's political alignment without Garrick ever formally declaring for a claimant. A match to Adrian, for example, would bind Vale's future inheritance by blood to Oren's dynasty and make continued neutrality increasingly artificial even if no immediate military oath were sworn.
- Cassian says he sought warning because warning preserves his opportunity to respond: he could improve his political terms, challenge the implications of a proposed match, secure counter-alliances, or decide that Vale neutrality had effectively ended. A flat prohibition on Elowen's marriage would have demanded far more control over Vale than Garrick had any reason to concede and would likely have destroyed the restraint negotiation.
- Cassian also points out that a prohibition would have reduced Vale's incentive to accept the bargain by freezing one of its largest dynastic assets solely for Veyne's benefit. He wanted information and reaction time, not an obviously unenforceable right to govern Garrick's daughter.
- Cassian states plainly that he does not need Elowen personally unmarried as an end in itself; he needs to know before her marriage is used to shift the balance of the succession. He does not imply a Cassian-Elowen match, consistent with the fact that none has been proposed.
