# Current scene

- Location: Veyrhold, royal capital — Vale residence, Garrick's private solar
- Time: late morning, 12th of Frostwane, Year 312 of the Crown
- Scene significance: climactic
- Situation: Cassian has formally proposed marrying Elowen as the core of a durable Vale-Veyne settlement. After testing the proposal against Vale autonomy, Elowen's succession, hypothetical children, and Garrick's own future remarriage/heirs, Garrick reaches a decision.

## Recent beats
- Cassian proposes that Vale end neutrality and support his claim. In exchange, if he prevails, Vale retains its lands, offices, internal command, and fortresses without a Veyne governor; Elowen's legitimacy and present succession are permanently recognized.
- Cassian states that a military alliance is insufficient and formally proposes marriage to Elowen. He addresses Garrick as head of House Vale rather than framing the proposal as courtship or asking Elowen romantically.
- Cassian says marriage would make preserving Vale part of his own dynasty rather than merely an allied promise. He proposes keeping Vale legally distinct and preventing himself from becoming Lord Vale merely through marriage.
- Garrick tests the issue of his own remarriage. Cassian does not demand Garrick remain unmarried and accepts that a future lawful Vale son may supersede Elowen if Vale law provides it, provided the marriage contract guarantees Elowen irrevocable lands, income, status, and rights for her children rather than allowing arbitrary dispossession.
- Cassian also agrees in principle that future lawful children of Garrick cannot be attainted or stripped of Vale succession merely because they inconvenience Cassian's dynasty.
- Garrick decides that continued neutrality is no longer viable and that Adrian would bind Vale to Oren without protecting Vale if Oren loses. Despite his extreme hatred of Cassian for Rowan's death, he judges Cassian's marriage proposal the strongest immediate survival settlement available.
- Garrick accepts the marriage proposal in principle, conditional on negotiated articles. His acceptance is not forgiveness and not yet a completed betrothal: lawyers/stewards must reduce terms to writing, including Vale autonomy, Elowen's property, succession contingencies, Garrick's remarriage rights, future Vale heirs, children of the Cassian-Elowen marriage, and limits on Veyne authority over Vale.
- Elowen is present when Garrick makes the decision. He does not ask her permission before accepting in principle, consistent with the dynastic norms and power imbalance established for the arc. Her private feelings remain hers; outward response is not yet established.
- Cassian's proposal and Garrick's acceptance create the intended emotional rupture: whatever reluctant trust grew at the priory now exists alongside the fact that Cassian has used political necessity to make himself her prospective husband.
