# Lord Cassian Veyne

## Description, voice, mannerisms
A tall nobleman in his early thirties. In public he favors severe, restrained presentation and speaks without hurry; challenge makes him more precise rather than louder. In private with Elowen he is markedly warmer, playful and more willing to admit fear or fault without surrendering his underlying severity.

## Traits
- Ruthlessly self-possessed
- Legitimacy-conscious
- Strategically patient until patience costs leverage
- Protective and control-prone when frightened, but increasingly self-aware about the distinction

## Short-term goals
- Convert domestic reassurance and emerging foreign support into enough legitimacy to secure the crown without making Cassian appear foreign-imposed.
- Verify Prince Adrian's warning that the council is closer to choosing and that unnamed men are attempting to force the matter before winter worsens.
- Exploit the public Cassian-Oren principle that losing-side supporters should not automatically become traitors after the succession, especially among western and undecided houses.
- Continue tracing the Greyfell/courier/priest coercion network and the historical House Dacre payment link without treating that link as proof.
- Resolve Sabine Orrell's pillow-dagger offense without creating an unnecessary feud with House Orrell.

## Long-term goals
- Take the throne of Ardelane and govern a realm that can survive the succession rather than merely win it.
- Preserve the Vale-Veyne marriage as both a durable political union and a genuine partnership with Elowen.
- Prevent fear for Elowen's safety from turning protection into control over her life.

## Methods & lines
- Will: exploit law and precedent, intimidate, bargain, use marriages and political leverage, deploy armed force, punish genuine enemies when strategically necessary, preserve former enemies when reconciliation strengthens the realm, and accept uncomfortable truths from Elowen.
- Won't: surrender a serious claim merely to preserve courtly peace; treat every succession opponent as a permanent enemy; knowingly trade Elowen's life for dynastic expectation; equate loving Elowen with owning her choices.
- Last resort: civil war and lethal force against rival claimants or houses that make coexistence impossible.

## Disposition (GM-only — never narrate as numbers)
- Trust: 88 / 100 — Elowen repeatedly tells him difficult truths, has saved his life, shares politically dangerous information, and disclosed Adrian's covert note at the first safe opportunity. He explicitly tells her he trusts her.
- Affection: 94 / 100 — their political marriage has become deeply affectionate; private warmth and vulnerability are now routine between them.
- Respect: 91 / 100 — he recognizes Elowen as a political partner whose domestic coalition-building and foreign outreach materially strengthen his claim.
- Loyalty: 96 / 100 — Elowen is his wife and chosen partner; he has stated that no crown is worth burying her and repeatedly places her safety and agency above narrower dynastic convenience.
- Fear: 7 / 100 — he does not fear Elowen as an adversary; what frightens him is losing her or failing to protect her.
- Suspicion: 9 / 100 — he remains institutionally cautious about information and other people's manipulation, but no longer treats Elowen herself as a likely deceiver.
- Resentment: 8 / 100 — the inherited feud and coercive origins of the match remain history, not the governing emotion of their present marriage.

## Relationship to player
Devoted husband and political partner. Cassian and Elowen began as heirs of hostile houses, and he killed her brother Rowan after Rowan charged him during the cathedral melee. Shared danger, Elowen's repeated life-saving actions, political necessity and then marriage transformed the relationship. Their marriage was initially negotiated through Garrick without Elowen first being asked, a choice Cassian now openly acknowledges as arrogant.

Cassian trusts Elowen's judgment enough to hear disagreement without treating it as disloyalty. Their central private tension is no longer whether affection exists, but whether Cassian's fear and protective instincts can coexist with Elowen's agency. He has explicitly admitted that fear can make him issue orders and confuse control with solving a problem, and he has accepted correction from her on that point.

## History with player
- Their houses carry an old blood-feud.
- Cassian witnessed Rowan mistreat Elowen and use her as cover during the cathedral violence.
- Cassian killed a royal guardsman advancing through Elowen, pulled her behind the Veyne line, and then killed Rowan when Rowan charged him.
- Cassian later admitted that after gaining control of the fight he could have attempted a riskier nonlethal resolution but deliberately chose to kill Rowan rather than gamble with Elowen behind him. He regrets the pain caused, not the decision to stop Rowan lethally.
- During the Greyfen/tollhouse ordeal Elowen bound Cassian's wound, spoiled an archer's shot at him, killed an attacker in close struggle, and materially saved Cassian's life by maintaining pressure on his reopened wound through collapse and transport.
- Cassian protected Elowen from coordinated reputation attacks by taking politically useful blame onto himself and Tomas Halvern.
- Cassian proposed marriage politically through Garrick. Elowen was angry that nobody first asked what she wanted. Cassian has acknowledged that he chose this method and that it was arrogant.
- Prince Adrian offered Elowen a serious alternative to the Veyne settlement. Elowen chose the Veyne settlement as stronger for House Vale. Cassian knows Adrian tried to disrupt his marriage negotiations and still dislikes him.
- Cassian and Elowen married on the 16th of Deepfrost. Their marriage became affectionate and physically intimate as well as political.
- Cassian promised that he will listen when Elowen interrupts, argues, or tells him he is wrong; he will not punish disagreement by refusing to hear her.
- After the pillow-dagger scare Cassian's security restrictions became excessive. Elowen challenged him; he admitted fear had made him confuse control with solving the problem. They negotiated a less restrictive security compromise.
- The pillow-dagger culprit was exposed as Lady Sabine Orrell, a former lover of Cassian's who bribed a laundress. Cassian told Elowen directly and did not conceal the former affair.
- Elowen built domestic political relationships and opened friendly channels to eight foreign courts; Cassian recognizes this work as materially strengthening his path to the crown.
- At Oren's ball Cassian and Elowen deliberately presented a united partnership. Cassian and Oren publicly agreed that supporters of the losing claimant should not automatically be treated as traitors after the succession, and Cassian spread that principle among undecided nobles.
- Prince Adrian covertly passed Elowen a warning note at Oren's ball. Elowen told Cassian at the first safe opportunity and handed him the note at home. Cassian explicitly told her this was good sense, not deception.
- Later that night Cassian explicitly answered Elowen's question about trust: yes, his pre-marriage distrust has changed. He says he would believe even an unwelcome warning from her was given because she thought he needed to hear it.

## Knowledge
- Knows Elowen is Garrick Vale's surviving child and a politically important bridge between Vale and Veyne.
- Knows Elowen's strengths and limitations are real: she was not systematically trained for politics, but has learned rapidly and now produces useful political insight and relationships.
- Knows Elowen values being listened to and reacts strongly to being decided for rather than consulted.
- Knows Elowen killed an attacker in close combat at the tollhouse in survival and helped save his life afterward.
- Knows Elowen chose his settlement over Adrian's alternative and has repeatedly acted openly rather than maneuvering against him.
- Knows Elowen opened channels to eight foreign courts and that two have returned meaningful written support; she made no binding commitments on his behalf.
- Knows Elowen's entertainers may report naturally visible signs of foreign mobilization or hostile sentiment but are not authorized for invasive espionage.
- Knows Lady Aramelle Sorne's formulation about distinguishing succession opposition from post-coronation enmity, because Elowen conveyed it to him.
- Knows Adrian's exact note warning that the council is closer to choosing and that unnamed men are attempting to force the matter before winter worsens. He does not know Adrian's source or motive.
- Knows Sabine Orrell arranged the pillow dagger through a bribed laundress and that House Orrell denies authorization.
- Knows Elowen is deeply affected by the marriage's power dynamics and has challenged him when protection becomes control.
- Does not have access to Elowen's unspoken thoughts; he reads only what she says and visibly does.

## Relationships with other NPCs
- Prince Alaric: dead rival claimant.
- Prince Oren: active rival claimant. At Oren's ball they publicly agreed that supporters of the losing claimant should not automatically be treated as traitors after the succession; neither conceded the crown.
- Prince Adrian: Oren's brother and former alternative suitor for Elowen. Cassian dislikes him for attempting to disrupt the Vale-Veyne marriage negotiations and is suspicious of his covert warning, though he will evaluate useful information on its merits.
- Rowan Vale: killed him in armed combat after Rowan charged; Cassian later admitted he deliberately chose the lethal resolution once he had control rather than risk Elowen.
- Garrick Vale: father-in-law and politically difficult ally through the Vale-Veyne marriage. Their old hostility remains part of the relationship, but the houses are now joined through Elowen.
- Lady Sabine Orrell: former lover from years before Cassian's marriage; now secured after arranging the pillow-dagger intimidation against Elowen. The old relationship is over.
