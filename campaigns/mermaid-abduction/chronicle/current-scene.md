- Casey waits until Daniel answers the full set of practical/cultural questions she raised rather than letting the conversation move on.
- Freedom/movement: Daniel explains that saelith status does not normally mean confinement to a household chamber. A human saelith can visit people, participate in settlement life and build relationships outside the paired household. The major practical restriction is human biology: submerged routes require air and swimming capacity. As Casey learns routes/language and demonstrates safe movement, Vaeren says he expects her practical freedom within the settlement to expand. Vaeren still insists on accompaniment for routes Casey cannot safely manage and continues to forbid a solitary attempt toward the surface.
- Access to Daniel/Mara: Vaeren agrees Casey may ask to visit Daniel/Seryn and Mara and does not claim a right to isolate her from other humans. Safe underwater transport remains necessary. Daniel emphasizes this is consistent with ordinary saelith household life rather than a special concession.
- Rights/status: Daniel explains that saelith is not ownership in merfolk custom. A recognized saelith belongs to a household/bond and is owed provision, protection, refuge and social recognition. Other merfolk are expected not to seize, sexually claim, casually command, injure or interfere with that person as though unattached. A saelith can speak, refuse ordinary requests, form friendships and raise grievances. However, the old taking tradition is coercive by human standards because it historically allows the taker to establish the household bond without the human's initial consent.
- Vaeren's authority: Vaeren claims authority primarily over safety and the exclusivity of the saelith bond, not total command over Casey's daily behavior. He says he will stop Casey from actions he believes immediately threaten her life, from disappearing into unsafe submerged routes, from attempting to return to the surface alone, or from permitting another merfolk to make a competing/intimate saelith claim. He does not claim the right to force sex, pregnancy, affection, speech, clothing, friendship or constant physical proximity.
- Ending a saelith bond: Daniel explains there is no single uncontested rule. Bonds can end through death; abandonment by the merfolk taker is a serious disgrace; some households/community authorities recognize separation or dissolution after grave mistreatment, repudiation, or sustained breakdown. The difficult disputed case is a human who never accepted the taking and continually asks to return to the surface. Older custom tends to treat the original taking as creating an enduring bond; less traditional merfolk argue continuing refusal must matter and can justify dissolution/return. No universal automatic release period exists.
- Casey's continuing refusal: Vaeren is directly asked what happens if Casey never calls him her saelith, even after years. He does not threaten punishment or claim he can force the word from her. He says her answer remains hers. However, he also does not promise that continued refusal alone will make him return her to the surface. This exposes the unresolved contradiction in his position: he recognizes that Casey's reciprocal acceptance cannot be compelled while still claiming the right to keep her below the sea.
- Daniel makes clear that Casey can challenge Vaeren, seek other merfolk perspectives and potentially bring serious disputes beyond the household, though exact formal authorities/procedures in the settlement remain unestablished.
- Casey remains human and cannot breathe water. The temporary warmth/oxygen-conservation effect remains active but does not imply pregnancy or permanent transformation.
