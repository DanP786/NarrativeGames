# Current scene

- Scene significance: climactic
- Location: Casey and Vaeren's air chamber
- Time: later in Casey's gradual physical recovery after the drowning attempt; exact surface date/time unverifiable
- Casey survived a serious drowning/aspiration event after prolonged food refusal and an active suicidal crisis. She has resumed eating and sleeping, and her physical strength is gradually returning, but her mood remains fragile and the larger crisis unresolved.
- Earlier in this scene Seryn comforted Casey, heard her confess the false-pregnancy cover and admit genuine care for Vaeren, made her laugh for the first time in weeks, and arranged her hair with the ornaments Casey had previously removed. Seryn then left Casey and Vaeren alone.
- Casey privately recognizes that she loves Vaeren, but has NOT said this aloud; neither Vaeren nor Seryn knows it.
- Casey and Vaeren acknowledged the central difficulty between them: she wants the shore, he took her from it, she lied because she expected him to stop her, and attraction/care remain despite the rupture. Vaeren distinguished what he wants from what he has the right to keep.
- Vaeren explicitly promised: "When you are strong enough to survive the journey, I will take you back to the shore." This is a major irreversible change: return is now something Vaeren has agreed to enable rather than prevent. Exact timing/route/preparations and what follows remain open.
- Casey was stunned because immediately before the promise she had privately been prepared to concede and tell Vaeren she would stay if only he forgave her. She never said this aloud, so Vaeren does not know she was about to offer it.
- Casey now asks, "What about what you want? What you just said...?" She is referring to Vaeren's immediately preceding admission that he wants her alive, honest, laughing/arguing/living in their household again and that personally he still wants her, while questioning how that desire fits with his promise to return her.
- Vaeren answers that his wants have not vanished: he still wants Casey to stay, wants the household/life/relationship they were building and wants her personally. But he now distinguishes wanting Casey to stay from making her stay. He recognizes that if she remains only because he withholds the shore, he can never know whether she chose him; returning her is therefore both an acknowledgment of her autonomy and the only way any future choice of him could be genuinely hers.
- Vaeren does not condition the promised return on Casey promising to come back, forgive him, remain his saelith, maintain contact or reciprocate his love/attraction. He may hope she chooses him once free, but he does not demand that hope be satisfied.
- Casey's private willingness to concede before the promise remains unspoken and is not a commitment. Her love for Vaeren, guilt, attraction, desire for the surface and the reality of his abduction can all coexist.
- The promise of return does not erase the original captivity or automatically resolve whether Casey will ultimately leave, stay, forgive him, maintain contact if possible, or understand herself as his saelith.
- Casey's recent laugh/smile and present emotional shock do NOT establish that her depression, guilt or suicidal vulnerability is fully resolved.
- Casey ordinarily speaks merfolk now; dialogue is rendered in English for readability.
