- DEEPBREATH CANON ESTABLISHED: Deepbreath is an old merfolk biological/ritual adaptation performed on a willing air-breathing saelith by a merfolk partner who knows the method. It temporarily changes how the human body uses and exchanges stored oxygen, allowing the human to remain submerged for roughly one to several hours depending on the individual, exertion and conditions. It does NOT create gills, does NOT allow breathing water in the ordinary sense, and does NOT make drowning impossible. The human still eventually needs ordinary air.
- The method requires intimate mouth-to-mouth transfer of a specialized merfolk secretion/breath compound combined with a deliberate pressure/respiratory sequence. The exact physiological mechanism is not scientifically understood by merfolk. It is more involved and sustained than ordinary rescue breaths or kissing, but is NOT inherently sexual and does not require intercourse. It must be actively accepted/cooperated with; attempting it on a resisting or panicking human is dangerous.
- Deepbreath takes several minutes to establish. Immediate sensations commonly include chest pressure, cold/tingling through the lungs/throat, slowed urge to breathe and temporary lightheadedness. It fades naturally. Known ordinary aftereffects can include fatigue, thirst, headache or chest soreness. Serious complications are uncommon when correctly performed on a healthy willing adult, but misuse, excessive exertion, attempting to extend beyond its fading window, or performing it on someone medically compromised can be dangerous. It is not treated as trivial.
- Deepbreath is distinct from the previously established post-sex merfolk-human effect that temporarily improves warmth and oxygen conservation. The two effects can overlap, but neither creates gills or permanent aquatic transformation.
