# Current scene

- Scene significance: climactic
- Location: Casey and Vaeren's air chamber
- Time: later in Casey's gradual physical recovery after the drowning attempt; exact surface date/time unverifiable
- Casey survived a serious drowning/aspiration event after prolonged food refusal and an active suicidal crisis. She has resumed eating and sleeping, and her physical strength is gradually returning, but her mood remains fragile and the larger crisis unresolved.
- Earlier in this scene Seryn comforted Casey, heard her confess the false-pregnancy cover and admit genuine care for Vaeren, made her laugh for the first time in weeks, and arranged her hair with the ornaments Casey had previously removed. Seryn then left Casey and Vaeren alone.
- Casey privately recognizes that she loves Vaeren, but has NOT said this aloud; neither Vaeren nor Seryn knows it.
- Casey and Vaeren acknowledged the central difficulty between them: she wants the shore, he took her from it, she lied because she expected him to stop her, and attraction/care remain despite the rupture. Vaeren distinguished what he wants from what he has the right to keep.
- Casey sat at the pool edge with her legs in the water directly in front of Vaeren and asked, "You will not take me back?" meaning the human shore/home.
- Vaeren answered directly. He said he had spent the days since she asked trying to find a reason to refuse that was not simply another form of "because I cannot bear it," and had found none. He then explicitly promised: "When you are strong enough to survive the journey, I will take you back to the shore."
- This is a major irreversible change in the main conflict: Vaeren has now agreed to return Casey to the human shore once she is physically strong enough for the journey. The exact departure date, route, preparations, conditions of the journey and what happens afterward remain unestablished. He has not made the promise conditional on Casey forgiving him, staying emotionally attached, renouncing others, or returning to him.
- Casey is stunned silent by the promise. Immediately before hearing it, she had been on the verge of conceding: she was prepared to tell Vaeren she would stay if only he would forgive her. She did NOT say this aloud, so Vaeren does not know she was about to offer it.
- Vaeren's promise radically changes what staying/leaving can mean. Casey experiences it as being given her life back: for the first time since the abduction, return to the human world is no longer merely something she must escape toward against Vaeren's will but something he has agreed to enable.
- Casey's private willingness to concede moments before the promise does not become a commitment because it was never spoken, and Vaeren's decision is not made in exchange for it.
- Casey's love for Vaeren, her guilt, her attraction to him, her desire for the surface and the reality of his abduction can all coexist. The promise of return does not erase the original captivity or automatically resolve whether Casey will ultimately leave, stay, forgive him, maintain contact if possible, or understand herself as his saelith.
- Casey's recent laugh/smile and present emotional shock do NOT establish that her depression, guilt or suicidal vulnerability is fully resolved.
- Casey ordinarily speaks merfolk now; dialogue is rendered in English for readability.
