- Casey now asks the broader question: why merfolk take humans at all.
- Daniel, with Vaeren and Seryn contributing/correcting his merfolk explanation, describes the taking as an old merfolk tradition rooted in the belief that sea and shore should sometimes be joined through a deliberately chosen human-merfolk saelith household. The merfolk participant chooses a particular human and personally brings that human alive across the boundary into merfolk life.
- In the older cultural logic, the taking itself is not understood merely as kidnapping for labor, sex, reproduction or ownership. It creates/announces a lasting saelith bond and imposes serious duties on the merfolk taker: air, food, warmth, refuge, protection, safe passage and public responsibility for the human life brought below.
- The old stories/custom also expect the human to become part of the merfolk household and, in conservative interpretations, eventually share enduring companionship, loyalty, physical partnership and possibly children. Reproduction can be part of such bonds but is not their sole purpose or definition.
- Daniel emphasizes the fundamental human objection: the oldest custom assumes the merfolk chooser may initiate this bond by taking the human without prior human consent. From a human perspective this remains abduction/captivity regardless of the duties or sacred/traditional meaning merfolk attach to it.
- Merfolk do not all interpret the tradition identically. Some retain the older view that a successful taking establishes an enduring bond; others give greater weight to continuing human refusal and may accept separation, dissolution or return in disputed cases. Exact origins, oldest myths, formal procedures and the prevalence of the practice remain unestablished.
- Vaeren's own taking of Casey belongs to the old tradition: he chose her deliberately, brought her alive below and considers himself bound to provide/protect her as saelith, while still refusing her demand to return to the shore. He does not claim the tradition made Casey's initial resistance into consent.
- Casey remains dependent on Daniel for detailed translation of fluent merfolk discussion, though she continues learning basic vocabulary.
- The housewarming framing and Casey's practical control of the chamber do not establish that Casey has accepted permanent residence or answer whether Casey regards Vaeren as her saelith in return.
- Casey's private strategy of learning the language/settlement and gaining trust/influence in hopes of eventually persuading Vaeren to return her remains unknown to Vaeren and the others.
