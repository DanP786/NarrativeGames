# Current scene

- Scene significance: charged
- Location: Casey and Vaeren's air chamber
- Time: the day after Casey freely said she wants to stay; exact surface date/time unverifiable
- Casey survived a serious drowning/aspiration event after prolonged food refusal and an active suicidal crisis. She has resumed eating and sleeping and is gradually recovering physically; her recent laughter, intimacy and decision to stay do NOT establish that the preceding emotional crisis is fully resolved.
- The previous day Vaeren explicitly promised: "When you are strong enough to survive the journey, I will take you back to the shore." Return became something he agreed to enable rather than prevent, restoring a genuine choice Casey had not previously possessed.
- With the shore genuinely available, Casey said, "I want to stay," then reinforced the sincerity of that choice by telling Vaeren, "I'm not lying about this, Vaeren." Vaeren accepted her statement without demanding vows and preserved her right to ask for the shore later.
- Casey privately recognizes that she loves Vaeren but still has NOT said this aloud; Vaeren does not know it.
- The next day has a quieter domestic texture. Casey relaxes on a mattress-like resting surface newly gifted to her by Neris; exact materials/construction and Neris's stated reason for the gift are unestablished. Vaeren is nearby repairing a fishing net.
- Casey asks what changed Vaeren's mind after she admitted lying. Vaeren explains that the lie initially made things worse, but eventually forced him to ask why Casey believed she had to lie: honesty about an escape attempt would simply have caused him to stop her. Her genuine affection, household attachment and desire to escape could all coexist, contrary to his former assumption.
- Vaeren further acknowledges that Casey's deterioration and drowning crisis forced him to confront the distinction between keeping her alive and having the right to decide where her life must be. He explicitly refuses to frame her near-death as a price she had to pay for freedom and says he should have understood from her original refusal at the shore that his reasons did not make her "no" irrelevant.
- Vaeren offers his hand rather than imposing touch. Casey takes it and says, "I'm glad you said you would take me home. Because now when I say I want to stay, you can believe me." This explicitly articulates Casey's understanding of why restored freedom matters to their relationship: the real possibility of leaving makes her present choice to remain credible rather than coerced or strategically appeasing.
- Vaeren can recognize this as the central distinction he has only recently learned. He may be deeply moved, but should not convert Casey's statement into irrevocable ownership. Believing her means accepting both halves of her agency: believing her when she says she wants to stay now, and believing/listening if she ever says she wants to leave later.
- Casey's statement strengthens the authenticity of her chosen relationship with Vaeren, but does not retroactively justify the abduction, erase past harms, settle every meaning of saelith, or reveal her private realization that she loves him.
- Vaeren's shore promise remains operative if Casey later changes her mind. The ethical distinction between her current chosen life and her former captivity depends on that continuing freedom.
- Casey ordinarily speaks merfolk now; dialogue is rendered in English for readability.
