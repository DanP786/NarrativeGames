# Current scene

- Scene significance: charged
- Location: occupied air chamber within the unnamed deep merfolk settlement
- Time: after Casey has slept for several hours; exact surface time unknown
- Casey remains captive after being forcibly taken from the shore by Vaeren, who refuses to return her human home and calls her "saelith."
- Casey asks Mara why merfolk take humans from the shore.
- Mara explains her understanding after fourteen years: there is no single practical necessity. The taking is an ancient cultural tradition among some merfolk, tied to old beliefs that sea and shore are joined through chosen human-merfolk household bonds. A merfolk who invokes the tradition chooses a particular human rather than merely acquiring any available person, and the act is treated by traditionalists as establishing obligations of provision, protection and enduring belonging toward the human taken.
- Mara emphasizes that merfolk disagree about the custom: some regard it as sacred or honorable, some as archaic, and some reject it. Human consent is the central moral fracture from Casey's perspective, and Mara does not excuse the abduction by describing its cultural meaning.
- Mara says the tradition is not needed because merfolk lack mates of their own species, and reproduction is not its sole purpose. Its oldest origin and exact ritual logic remain uncertain even to Mara and can be discovered further through merfolk sources.
- Vaeren remains in the pool at the ledge, observing without understanding the full English exchange.
