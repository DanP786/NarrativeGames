# Current scene

- Scene significance: climactic
- Location: Casey and Vaeren's air chamber
- Time: later in Casey's gradual physical recovery after the drowning attempt; exact surface date/time unverifiable
- Casey survived a serious drowning/aspiration event after prolonged food refusal and an active suicidal crisis. She has resumed eating and sleeping, and her physical strength is gradually returning, but her mood remains fragile and the larger crisis unresolved.
- Earlier in this scene Seryn comforted Casey, heard her confess the false-pregnancy cover and admit genuine care for Vaeren, made her laugh for the first time in weeks, and arranged her hair with the ornaments Casey had previously removed. Seryn then left Casey and Vaeren alone.
- Casey privately recognizes that she loves Vaeren, but has NOT said this aloud; neither Vaeren nor Seryn knows it.

- Vaeren has made clear that he still wants Casey, their household and the relationship/life they were building, but distinguishes wanting her to stay from making her stay. He recognizes that if he withholds the shore, neither of them can know whether Casey ever truly chooses him.
- Casey fears that returning to the human world may mean never seeing Vaeren again. Vaeren does not guarantee future contact but says he could potentially return to the coast only if Casey wanted him to. Casey then shows genuine hurt/jealousy at the idea he might take another saelith. Vaeren answers that he does not want a replacement or "a" saelith; he wants Casey, while reiterating that wanting her does not grant him the right to keep her.
- Casey now struggles to speak and asks, "Why do you want me?" The question is not merely reassurance-seeking; internally, the restoration of her genuine choice changes her posture. Casey is no longer preparing to surrender simply to secure Vaeren's forgiveness. She has a growing certainty that if she ultimately stays, Vaeren will have to earn that freely chosen decision rather than receive it through captivity, guilt or capitulation.
- Casey has NOT yet told Vaeren that she is considering staying, has NOT withdrawn her right to return, and has NOT said she loves him. Vaeren cannot know the full significance of her new certainty unless she reveals it. He can, however, recognize that she is asking him to account for why he wants her as a particular person rather than merely as a saelith role.
- Vaeren's answer should draw on established reasons he chose/grew attached to Casey: before the taking he watched her liveliness, social warmth, laughter, concern for others, attention to the sea and her resistance when a human man grabbed her; afterward he came to know her humor, arguments, stubbornness, care for his family, curiosity, household-making, willingness to challenge him and the genuine affection/intimacy they shared. He must not use these qualities to justify the original abduction; indeed, admiring her resistance while violating her autonomy remains part of the irony/wrong he now recognizes.
- Casey's new internal stance is significant: with return genuinely available, staying would have to become an active choice on terms she can test and negotiate. This does not predetermine that she will stay; Vaeren can still fail to earn it, and Casey may still choose the surface.
- The promise of return does not erase the original captivity or automatically resolve whether Casey will ultimately leave, stay, forgive him, maintain contact if possible, or understand herself as his saelith.
- Casey's recent laugh/smile and present emotional shift do NOT establish that her depression, guilt or suicidal vulnerability is fully resolved.
