# Main thread

- Status: established and hardened
- Central question: Can anything genuine grow between Casey and Vaeren when their relationship begins in abduction, captivity and profound unequal power—and what happens if Casey's growing attachment never becomes consent to the life he chose for her?
- PC want: Casey wants safety, autonomy, understanding, meaningful freedom of movement, and ultimately a way home or out of captivity. Her private strategy is to learn the language and settlement, gain trust and influence, and use that leverage in hopes of eventually persuading Vaeren to return her; Vaeren does not know this strategy.
- Antagonist / obstacle: Vaeren himself, the ancient merfolk assumptions that make his taking culturally meaningful, Casey's human biological vulnerability in an underwater world, and the disputed status of a human's continuing refusal under saelith custom.
- Established direction: Vaeren deliberately chose Casey after watching her from the sea and forcibly brought her alive into the merfolk settlement under the old taking tradition. "Saelith" is an enduring paired household/bond status rather than simple ownership or a clean equivalent of human spouse/mate. It imposes duties of air, food, warmth, refuge, protection and social recognition on the merfolk partner, while older interpretations treat the initial taking as sufficient to create an enduring bond even without human consent. Less traditional interpretations give greater weight to continuing refusal and may recognize dissolution/return in disputed cases.
- Relationship state: Casey and Vaeren have developed real trust, attraction and voluntary physical intimacy, including a first consensual sexual encounter, without erasing the coercive foundation of the relationship. Vaeren refuses forced sex or pregnancy and will not answer for Casey whether she considers him her saelith. He nevertheless continues to refuse her return to the human shore and expects shared life below the sea to deepen over time.
- Current pressure: Casey is actively interrogating the rules around her captivity rather than merely surviving it. She has learned that practical freedom inside the settlement can expand, that she may maintain relationships with other humans/merfolk, that saelith gives social protections as well as constraints, and that dissolution is culturally disputed rather than impossible. The sharp unresolved issue is whether Casey can turn growing knowledge, status and relationships into genuine autonomy—and whether Vaeren's claimed respect for her choices can survive a choice that ultimately rejects the life he has imposed.
- Outcome remains open: romance, escape, negotiated autonomy, dissolution, return to the surface, reconciliation, tragedy or some other resolution are not predetermined.
