# Main thread

- Status: established and hardened
- Central question: Can anything genuine grow between Casey and Vaeren when their relationship begins in abduction, captivity and profound unequal power—and what happens if Casey's growing attachment never becomes consent to the life he chose for her?
- PC want: Casey wants safety, autonomy, understanding, meaningful freedom of movement, and ultimately a way home or out of captivity. Her private strategy is to learn the language and settlement, gain trust and influence, and use that leverage in hopes of eventually persuading Vaeren to return her or creating an escape opportunity; Vaeren does not know this strategy.
- Antagonist / obstacle: Vaeren himself, the ancient merfolk assumptions that make his taking culturally meaningful, Casey's human biological vulnerability in an underwater world, and the disputed status of a human's continuing refusal under saelith custom.
- Established direction: Vaeren deliberately chose Casey after watching her from the sea and forcibly brought her alive into the merfolk settlement under the old taking tradition. "Saelith" is an enduring paired household/bond status rather than simple ownership or a clean equivalent of human spouse/mate. It imposes duties of air, food, warmth, refuge, protection and social recognition on the merfolk partner, while older interpretations treat the initial taking as sufficient to create an enduring bond even without human consent. Less traditional interpretations give greater weight to continuing refusal and may recognize dissolution/return in disputed cases.
- Relationship state: Casey and Vaeren have developed real trust, attraction and voluntary physical intimacy without erasing the coercive foundation. Casey has now explicitly called Vaeren her saelith in return, explaining that if she is his then he is hers. Vaeren receives this as profound reciprocal recognition and over the following days becomes much more openly affectionate and confident in initiating consensual intimacy. Casey willingly reciprocates and enjoys that intimacy, but reciprocal saelith status is not blanket sexual consent and she retains the right to refuse any particular encounter.
- Current pressure: Casey underestimated how much reciprocal saelith recognition would mean to Vaeren. Her words and ongoing intimacy increasingly convince him that the shared life he imposed has become mutually chosen, while Casey privately continues to want eventual return or escape and deliberately hopes his growing trust/attachment will yield greater freedom, influence or opportunity. Her affection and desire are genuine, so the strategy is not a wholly false romance; the danger is that the same actions can carry radically different implications for each of them.
- Sharp unresolved issue: whether Casey can turn growing knowledge, status, reciprocal attachment and expanded practical freedom into genuine autonomy—and whether Vaeren's claimed respect for her choices can survive a choice that ultimately rejects life below the sea despite Casey having called him her saelith.
- Outcome remains open: romance, escape, negotiated autonomy, dissolution, return to the surface, reconciliation, tragedy or some other resolution are not predetermined.
