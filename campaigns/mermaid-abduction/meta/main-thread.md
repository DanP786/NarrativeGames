# Main thread

- Status: established and hardened
- Central question: Can anything genuine grow between Casey and Vaeren when their relationship begins in abduction, captivity and profound unequal power—and what happens if Casey's growing attachment never becomes consent to the life he chose for her?
- PC want: Casey wants safety, autonomy, understanding, meaningful freedom of movement, and ultimately a way home or out of captivity. Her private strategy is to learn the language and settlement, gain trust and influence, and use that leverage in hopes of eventually persuading Vaeren to return her or creating an escape opportunity; Vaeren does not know this strategy.
- Antagonist / obstacle: Vaeren himself, the ancient merfolk assumptions that make his taking culturally meaningful, Casey's human biological vulnerability in an underwater world, and the disputed status of a human's continuing refusal under saelith custom.
- Established direction: Vaeren deliberately chose Casey after watching her from the sea and forcibly brought her alive into the merfolk settlement under the old taking tradition. "Saelith" is an enduring paired household/bond status rather than simple ownership or a clean equivalent of human spouse/mate. It imposes duties of air, food, warmth, refuge, protection and social recognition on the merfolk partner, while older interpretations treat the initial taking as sufficient to create an enduring bond even without human consent. Less traditional interpretations give greater weight to continuing refusal and may recognize dissolution/return in disputed cases.
- Relationship state: Casey and Vaeren have developed real trust, attraction and voluntary physical intimacy without erasing the coercive foundation. Casey has explicitly called Vaeren her saelith in return, explaining that if she is his then he is hers. Vaeren received this as profound reciprocal recognition and became much more openly affectionate and confident in initiating consensual intimacy. Casey willingly reciprocates and enjoys that intimacy, but reciprocal saelith status is not blanket sexual consent and she retains the right to refuse any particular encounter.
- Current pressure: Casey underestimated how much reciprocal saelith recognition would mean to Vaeren. Her words and ongoing intimacy increasingly convinced him that the shared life he imposed had become mutually chosen, while Casey privately continued to want eventual return or escape and hoped his growing trust/attachment would yield greater freedom, influence or opportunity. After a clandestine solo trip to Mara in which Casey unsuccessfully proposed escaping together, Casey covered the trip with a false pregnancy scare. The guilt and practical impossibility of sustaining her strategy then overwhelmed her, and when Vaeren told her she was not alone she sobbed directly, "I am. I want to go home." This is genuine and means her human surface home/life.
- Sharp unresolved issue: Casey has now put her continuing desire for the surface directly against Vaeren's belief that reciprocal saelith, genuine affection, intimacy and their shared household mean she has chosen life with him. Vaeren still does not know her private escape strategy or the truth of the Mara visit. The immediate question is whether he can hear "I want to go home" as a real autonomous choice rather than treating her attachment to him as overriding it—and what Casey herself is willing to risk or surrender to make that choice real.
- Outcome remains open: romance, escape, negotiated autonomy, dissolution, return to the surface, reconciliation, tragedy or some other resolution are not predetermined.
