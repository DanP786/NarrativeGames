# Tide Hall

## Overview
The Tide Hall is the settlement's principal crown-used ceremonial gathering place. It is not a dry ballroom transplanted underwater but an immense vertical marine chamber: terraces of dark stone rise around a broad central water column so merfolk can gather at different depths, cross the open space freely and watch ceremonies from above, below and around one another.

## Appearance and atmosphere
For the annual saelith celebration the chamber is transformed with dense constellations of cultivated living lights, pale shell and polished stone ornament, and long luminous marine streamers that bend in the currents. Music is carried partly as audible sound and partly as deep rhythmic vibration through water and stone. Formal saelith households mingle throughout the chamber rather than standing on a single floor.

## Human accommodation
Along the chamber's sides are several maintained air alcoves/galleries where human saelith can breathe, rest, eat and converse without remaining submerged continuously. Their existence reflects the long historical presence of humans in merfolk households. Reaching and moving between them still ordinarily requires submerged travel unless a human has deepbreath or is assisted.

## Ceremonial use
The crown uses the Tide Hall for major realm gatherings including the annual saelith celebration and its voluntary Tide-Witness acknowledgements. The exact royal dais/position and the present king's arrival details remain unestablished until encountered.
