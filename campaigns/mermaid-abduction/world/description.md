# World description

The human world is contemporary and mundane. Merfolk are folklore to humanity: stories, art, tourist kitsch, and fantasy. No recognised human institution knows they are real.

Beyond the human shore lies a hidden mythical marine world. Merfolk possess their own communities, customs, taboos, stories and ways of understanding the sea. Their biology and culture are not merely human society transplanted underwater. Other mythical marine beings, places and dangers may exist and will be established through exploration rather than catalogued in advance.

Among at least part of merfolk culture, stories of mermen taking human women from the shore are ancient and culturally significant. The practice is not assumed morally benign merely because it is traditional. Old custom historically treated a successful taking as creating a saelith household claim with strong duties of provision/protection and weak recognition of the taken human's refusal. Less traditional interpretation increasingly distinguishes a bond imposed by taking from a saelith bond consciously affirmed by both people. Casey and Vaeren now occupy an unusual position: Vaeren restored Casey's genuine option to return to shore, and Casey subsequently chose to stay. This gives her continued presence a voluntary basis that the original taking lacked, without retroactively legitimising the abduction.

## Merfolk realm and crown

Casey's settlement belongs to one merfolk kingdom among multiple peoples/realms of the wider ocean. The crown is hereditary within a royal lineage but succession is not simple human-style primogeniture: an eligible heir must also be accepted by senior settlement/kin representatives, allowing a manifestly unsuitable heir to be passed over. The king arbitrates disputes crossing settlements or kin groups, protects shared routes and waters, oversees realm-wide law/custom, receives foreign envoys, and serves as ceremonial guardian of old traditions. Households and settlements retain substantial autonomy, so the king is powerful without governing ordinary domestic life minute by minute.

Below the royal lineage is no rigid human-style peerage of dukes/counts. Political standing grows from old kin-lines, service, reputation, stewardship of important routes/resources and recognised offices. Some families are therefore considerably more influential than ordinary households without being titled nobility.

Vaeren, Seryn and Neris come from one such respected **old service-family**. Their parents' household has longstanding standing in the settlement and access to senior civic circles, but they are not royal, not heirs to the throne and do not possess blanket authority over other households. Their family is well known enough that Vaeren arriving publicly with a newly taken human saelith attracts more notice than the same act by an obscure household would.

Vaeren serves as a **route-warden and outer-water scout**. His work involves maintaining knowledge of safe passages, currents, hazards and boundary waters; inspecting/repairing route markers and nets/barriers; carrying warnings; and ranging farther toward the kingdom's margins and occasionally toward human-adjacent waters than most settlement residents. The role is skilled and respected, gives him unusual independence of movement and partly explains why he had repeated opportunities to observe Casey's shore before taking her. It does NOT grant him royal rank, policing power over ordinary households, or a special legal right to take humans.

Seryn is a **cultivator-healer**, specialising in living marine materials used for medicine, household cultivation, ornament and human adaptation. She is not the realm's chief healer, but she is skilled, socially connected and works with specialist healers/cultivators. Her familiarity with human physiology has deepened during seven years with Daniel. This helps explain her knowledge of the post-intimacy respiratory effect and of deepbreath research/treatment. She cannot simply administer deepbreath herself without the required matched culture, preparation and specialist oversight.

Daniel has meaningful household/social standing as Seryn's long-established saelith but holds no independent merfolk political office currently established. Neris's occupation remains unestablished.

The present king's personal name, appearance and personality remain to be encountered in play. The crown recognises the old legality/custom of human taking but does not require it, and interpretations within the realm are contested. A taken human is legally a person within a saelith household rather than chattel property, but old law historically gives the taker disturbing power over where that human lives. Voluntary reaffirmation, refusal, dissolution and return are areas of cultural/legal tension rather than perfectly settled doctrine.

## Annual saelith celebration

Each year the crown hosts a major celebration of saelith households. It includes merfolk-merfolk and human-merfolk bonds. The gathering combines feasting, music, formal display, storytelling, gifts and public recognition. Its central ceremonial tradition is the Tide-Witness: new or newly reaffirmed saelith pairs may voluntarily stand before witnesses, touch open hands and speak a short mutual acknowledgement. Existing pairs may repeat it, but are not required to. Participation in the celebration or Tide-Witness is culturally significant, not legally mandatory.

For an old-fashioned taking, appearing together can traditionally be read as public household recognition even if the human's consent was historically treated ambiguously. Under newer interpretations, the Tide-Witness is meaningful only if both speak for themselves. Casey cannot be compelled to perform it, and her attendance alone does not constitute an irrevocable vow. If she and Vaeren ever choose to participate, her ability to leave must remain compatible with whatever they freely promise.

## Merfolk biology and human compatibility

Merfolk are long-lived relative to humans but not immortal; adulthood and aging are recognisable life stages, with exact maximum lifespan varying and not yet numerically fixed. They are air-and-water adapted marine people able to remain submerged indefinitely through their own respiratory biology, but prolonged dryness is uncomfortable and eventually dangerous. They sleep, eat, heal and reproduce biologically rather than as purely magical beings. Their medicine combines empirical marine pharmacology, cultivated organisms and traditions humans would sometimes describe as magical.

Humans and merfolk are reproductively compatible. Human-merfolk conception is possible but less frequent than same-species conception, and viable hybrid children exist. Hybrid inheritance is variable rather than producing one uniform half-human form: aquatic/respiratory traits, legs versus tail, scales/skin, eyes and pressure tolerance can differ among children. Hybrids are recognised people with full household standing, though uncommon enough to attract attention and sometimes become romanticised as symbols of sea-and-shore union. Exact gestational details remain to be established if pregnancy becomes relevant.

Merfolk possess reliable traditional fertility-control preparations derived from cultivated marine organisms. Use is a choice rather than automatic. Daniel and Seryn's exact reproductive choices remain private/unestablished unless revealed in play. Casey and Vaeren are canonically NOT using contraception or another fertility-prevention method; pregnancy is therefore genuinely possible but is not currently established.

## Deepbreath: human underwater breathing

A recently developed merfolk medical craft called **deepbreath** can temporarily allow an ordinary human to breathe underwater. It is neither a wearable device nor a simple drinkable potion. The reliable human-safe form has existed only for a few years.

Deepbreath was developed from study of the same merfolk-human biochemical interaction that produces the already-established temporary warmth and improved oxygen conservation in a human after sexual intimacy with a merfolk partner. Researchers discovered that compounds/proteins carried in that partner-specific exchange can temporarily alter a human's oxygen use. Deepbreath amplifies and redirects those properties through a cultivated symbiotic marine organism until true underwater gas exchange becomes possible.

Because the underlying response depends on the individual human AND the merfolk partner whose biology first produces a compatible response, **every deepbreath treatment is bespoke**. A healer must obtain a small nonsexual biological sample from the merfolk saelith/partner (such as shed scale material, mucus/saliva or blood according to the preparation) and use it to cultivate a matched living culture for that specific human. The process does NOT require sex to manufacture or renew deepbreath, and no human is required to engage in sexual activity to qualify. For humans without a merfolk sexual partner, healers can sometimes identify a compatible donor through testing, but this is slower and less predictable.

The matched culture is then introduced by trained healers as a living medicinal infusion. For a limited period it changes how the human lungs/blood exchange gases, allowing underwater respiration while leaving the person biologically human. The effect eventually fades and must be renewed. It does not create permanent gills, transform legs into a tail, grant merfolk pressure tolerance, swimming ability, cold resistance or immunity to drowning from injury/exhaustion. A deepbreath human remains physically disadvantaged underwater compared with merfolk.

The ordinary post-intimacy effect and deepbreath are therefore related but NOT identical. Sex alone never produces true underwater breathing. Casey's previous warmth/oxygen-conservation episodes were evidence that her physiology responds strongly enough to Vaeren's biology that a matched deepbreath culture might plausibly be attempted; they were not themselves treatment.

Deepbreath cultures are difficult to grow, short-lived and potentially dangerous if mismatched or poorly prepared. Specialist healers administer them; they are not ordinary household possessions. Seryn's cultivator-healer work gives her meaningful knowledge of the process, but she cannot casually create a safe dose by herself.

Access is neither automatic nor something won through combat. Under current custom, a human must knowingly request/accept the treatment, and a sponsoring saelith household petitions for specialist access. Because matched cultures consume scarce cultivation capacity, priority historically goes to humans whose household role, work or demonstrated integration requires regular submerged independence. This system is politically contentious: traditionalists argue that a human saelith should travel under merfolk protection and therefore does not need independent underwater breathing, while reform-minded merfolk argue that withholding mobility keeps humans structurally dependent on their takers.

Daniel does NOT have deepbreath and has never received the treatment. Mara's status is not yet established. Daniel, Seryn and Vaeren know deepbreath exists. Casey has NOT previously been told about it. Vaeren did not conceal it as a calculated secret to prevent escape; during Casey's early captivity he did not regard her as eligible/ready for a scarce treatment granting much greater independent mobility, and after their rupture/recovery the subject had not yet been revisited. Seryn's professional knowledge means she knew substantially more about deepbreath than Casey realised. Daniel knew of its existence through Seryn but has no personal experience of receiving it. Nonetheless, from Casey's perspective the fact that all three knew a method existed that could reduce her dependence while nobody told her is a legitimate potential grievance. Their reasons do not erase the omission.

Humans believe Casey drowned after her disappearance. Her body is never recovered. No human rescue or investigation thread intrudes into the campaign; the narrative follows Casey into the hidden world.
