# Session 02

## Pregnancy loss and marital breach
Aemilia's first pregnancy, approximately four months advanced, ends after a severe marital quarrel with Lucius and a night of escalating pain and bleeding. The quarrel begins after Eros reports a private conversation with Aemilia to Lucius and describes Aemilia as sheltered and likely to yield if Lucius disagrees. Aemilia feels wounded that Lucius repeatedly explains and defends Eros's position when Aemilia is trying to tell him that Eros's words hurt her. When Aemilia tries to leave, Lucius physically stops her twice. The second time he shuts the door and forces her shoulders against it after explicitly promising he would not stop her again. He catches her high and avoids her pregnant belly, but recognizes the broken promise and tells her, **“I was wrong.”** No causal link between the argument, restraint and later miscarriage is established.

During the night Aemilia develops significant abdominal pain and vaginal bleeding. Demetrios and a trusted midwife attend her. Once examined, the midwife concludes that the pregnancy is being lost and has progressed too far to stop; neither attendant establishes a cause. The loss completes over hours with substantial bleeding. Aemilia remains unconscious for roughly a day from severe weakness and blood loss before waking. The exact sex, appearance and handling of the lost child remain unestablished. No later fever, renewed hemorrhage or other new complication is established, but Aemilia remains markedly weak.

Pia, Aemilia's longtime personal attendant, had been given her first genuine day away from ordinary duties and fails to return. More than a week later she remains missing, with no message or confirmed word reaching the household. Her whereabouts, reason for absence and fate remain unknown.

## Grief and family counsel
Aemilia spends several days withdrawn in bed from grief rather than confinement or medical order. Cornelia stays with her for several days. Aemilia asks whether she is weak and eventually tells her mother about Eros's judgment, the quarrel with Lucius and the physical restraint. Cornelia initially explains some of Eros's conduct through the realities of enslavement, which Aemilia experiences as taking Lucius's side; Cornelia later acknowledges that understanding Eros's position does not place Eros beyond reproach. Aemilia sends her mother home and withdraws again.

Aemilia eventually returns to household work while still physically weak. Her manner has changed: she remains soft-spoken and controlled but no longer offers the easy smiles, reassurance and warmth with which she previously cushioned commands. The reserve applies broadly rather than only to Eros. She struggles to dress and arrange her hair without Pia and works beyond her endurance. Lucius does not force reconciliation and the core dispute remains unspoken between them after the loss.

Terentia visits on Aemilia's second day back at work. Aemilia tells her that she feels she failed her duty to the Valerian family and fears the old pressure over the childless marriage. Terentia acknowledges the dynastic duty to bear legitimate children but rejects treating an uncontrollable pregnancy loss as a moral failure. Aemilia then discloses the marital breach in substance, including Lucius physically stopping her, the broken promise and the dispute over Eros. Terentia takes the restraint and broken word seriously without claiming they caused the miscarriage. She tells Aemilia that mercy toward Eros and the children does not require confidence or warmth, and that Lucius remains responsible for the choices he made. Aemilia says she does not want anyone punished but feels punished herself by the situation. Terentia advises that Aemilia has already told Lucius what hurt her and need not repeatedly chase him for acknowledgment; if the marriage is to recover, Lucius must eventually address the breach himself.

The visit turns to Valeria Tertia. Terentia says Valeria is well and still unaware that her parents are considering Gaius Vibius Rufus as a possible husband. Marcus is investigating Rufus's family standing, property, debts, reputation and usefulness while Terentia is more concerned with temperament and motive. Terentia is provisionally favorable, particularly because Rufus treated Valeria's Greek-poem challenge seriously rather than patronizing her, but she remains alert to whether he wants Valeria herself or merely access to the Valerii. No engagement or promise exists. Terentia invites Aemilia to visit the elder Messalla household when stronger; Aemilia answers, **“I would like that.”**

## Pia and the closing beat
After Terentia leaves, Aemilia catches herself beginning the old habit of stroking her pregnant belly and returns to work. Later she goes personally into the working part of the domus to ask the steward whether there is any word of Pia. There is none. Aemilia does not order punishment or further action and says only, **“No I… just hope she is safe.”**

On the way back Aemilia reaches a flight of steps and discovers she is too weak to climb them. She initially insists she is merely resting, then pushes herself upward until her legs shake and one knee nearly folds. She finally tells a servant, **“Get Lucius.”** Lucius comes, asks permission before lifting her, and carries her upstairs. Aemilia voluntarily rests her head against his chest and closes her eyes. Because he remembers her distress around the bed where the child was lost, Lucius takes her to a nearby private sitting room rather than their shared bedchamber. When he asks whether she wants him to stay and begins to withdraw his hand, Aemilia grabs it. Lucius remains beside the couch, holding her hand without treating the closeness as forgiveness or resolution.

## Background canon established OOC
During the weeks of Aemilia's withdrawal after the miscarriage, Eros privately sought Lucius's company in a manner recalling their former sexual relationship. Lucius understood the invitation and was genuinely tempted, but did not resume sexual access or have sex with Eros because his fidelity promise to Aemilia remains a hard line. Eros's motive remains unresolved and mixed rather than established as malicious. Aemilia and Terentia do not know the encounter occurred unless later informed through a plausible in-world channel.
