# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, several weeks after S01. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant. The pregnancy is more visibly apparent but no complication is established.
- Morning at Lucius and Aemilia's Valerian domus in Rome. Lucius is away from the domus. The discreet Rufus-Valeria gathering is now several weeks past. Terentia remains cautiously favorable; Valeria and Rufus remain unaware of the matchmaking purpose; Marcus Valerius Messalla has not been told. Rufus's Atalanta-poem search remains unresolved.
- Aemilia receives Tullia in the tablinum with Pia attending. Tullia is an adult married Roman woman in her thirties from a respectable senatorial-connected family, known to Aemilia by sight from elite social and religious occasions but not as an intimate. Tullia's own attendant remains near the doorway.
- Tullia explains that one of her husband's freedmen, Publius, has been pressed for payment on an obligation by a Valerian household agent. The debt itself is not denied. The agent has threatened to seize pledged workshop equipment and stock if payment is not produced promptly. Publius and his wife say this would prevent the workshop from continuing to earn.
- Aemilia promises to take the matter to Lucius when he returns, saying the household would not want a man's livelihood stripped without consideration. She does not forgive the debt, declare the agent wrong, order collection stopped herself, or accept Publius's account as proven.
- Aemilia asks for more detail. Tullia says Publius told her the payment presently demanded is 18,000 sesterces, an amount currently due on a larger obligation advanced for workshop stock and operating expenses and secured in part against equipment and stock. According to Publius, delayed customer payments and poor-quality purchased metal have caused the cash shortage. Tullia can vouch for the workshop's existence and Publius's long reliable service to her husband's household but not for his books or every explanation.
- The Valerian agent is Gnaeus Fabius Priscus, a freeborn professional agent used by Lucius's household for some urban debts and commercial obligations. Tullia knows the name from the notice delivered to Publius and has not personally dealt with him.
- Aemilia asks whether Publius owes more than one creditor. Tullia says yes: Publius has ordinary trade obligations to suppliers in addition to the Valerian debt, and she understands that at least one supplier is also waiting beyond the customary payment date. She does not know of another large secured loan comparable to the Valerian obligation and cannot guarantee none exists. Publius did not present himself to her as debt-free apart from Lucius; rather, his present difficulty appears to be a broader cash-flow squeeze in which several obligations are competing for limited ready money.
- Tullia adds that this is one reason she came seeking review rather than simply asking Aemilia to trust Publius: if other creditors begin taking assets, delaying the Valerian collection could merely leave Lucius last in line. She considers that possibility real enough to disclose rather than conceal it.
- The full original Valerian amount and terms, remaining balance, composition of the 18,000 due, total outside debts, priority/security of other creditors, whether Publius's business account is accurate, and whether Priscus acted within Lucius's standing instructions remain unverified.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia has established that Publius is not dealing with only one creditor. Tullia admits other trade debts and at least one overdue supplier, while not knowing of another major secured lender. The case is therefore more financially risky than a simple isolated missed installment.
