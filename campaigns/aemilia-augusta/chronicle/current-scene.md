# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, morning. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant, at the Valerian domus in Rome.
- Publius workshop crisis active. Figures: Aufidius 12,800; other pressing outside debts 6,200 and 3,600; Valerian due 18,000; pressing total ~40,600. Receivables ~21,000/~12,000 promptly collectible. Forced liquidation likely destroys value/may not cover Valerian claim. Lucius leans controlled rescue but has not executed it; Aufidius standstill on two seized enslaved craftsmen expires today.
- Pia's first genuine weekly day free ordinary labor is today. Another enslaved household woman dressed Aemilia/did hair; usual clothing, visibly different hair. Pia genuinely off ordinary duties.
- Marcus Aemilius Lepidus's unannounced visit has concluded. He withdrew prior pressure to end Aemilia's marriage, accepted her happiness and judgment, and agreed the marriage should continue. Aemilia and Marcus partially reconciled. Cornelia wants to be present during Aemilia's confinement/birth; Aemilia wants her there. Marcus has been told about the trusted unnamed midwife and Demetrios.
- After Marcus leaves, Aemilia and Lucius share an affectionate, impulsive adult marital interlude, non-explicitly narrated. Lucius then returns toward the urgent Publius/Aufidius matter before today's standstill expires.
- Aemilia conducts a private household-discipline inquiry with senior steward Gaius Philargyrus. She learns physical punishment occurs several times in an ordinary month and that lesser delegated supervisors sometimes strike enslaved subordinates without reporting each incident.
- Aemilia orders a one-month trial in the urban domus under Philargyrus's immediate administration: no punitive beating or striking by anyone. Necessary immediate force to stop assault or protect someone from bodily danger remains permitted, including protection of Aemilia and her pregnancy, but may not become punishment afterward.
- Aemilia orders immediate modest rewards for competent ordinary work rather than vague future promises or rewarding exhaustion. Philargyrus will track offenses, responses and rewards so the trial can be assessed. He will inform Lucius because Lucius retains ultimate household authority; unless countermanded, implementation begins immediately.
- Pia's weekly day free ordinary duties still needs formal standing instruction; Pia remains off duty today.
- Aemilia summons Eros, adult enslaved woman and mother of Fortunata (~4) and Felix (~2) by Lucius. Aemilia has her close the door and invites her to sit. Their first substantive private conversation is cautious because of the stark power imbalance.
- Aemilia tells Eros Pia suggested speaking with a servant about things Aemilia does not see and admits she has accepted ignorance until now. Eros explains that servants conceal quarrels, injuries, misconduct and complaints from masters, and that ordering truth does not make candor safe.
- Aemilia acknowledges Eros has particular reason to tell her what she thinks Aemilia wants to hear. Eros says she loves Fortunata and Felix without hesitation and explains that her lack of legal control over their futures makes her especially careful around Aemilia and Lucius.
- Asked whether she loves Lucius, Eros distinguishes caring for him and gratitude for his acknowledgment/care of their children from free choice. She says she was never in a position to decide whether Lucius came to her and cannot describe their history as equivalent to Aemilia's chosen adult marriage.
- Aemilia asks directly: 'Did you want to say no?'
- Eros answers with painful specificity rather than converting the entire past into a single yes/no category. She says there were times she did not want Lucius to come to her: when exhausted, ill, frightened of another pregnancy, or simply when she wanted to be left alone. She did not say no because she did not understand refusal as an option available to her as an enslaved woman summoned by her master.
- Eros also says there were other occasions when she did not dread his coming and could feel affection, pleasure in being favored, relief at kinder treatment, or hope that proximity would protect her and her children. She insists these feelings do not erase the fact that the choice was structurally Lucius's, not hers.
- No new canon is established that Lucius used physical force, threatened Eros, punished refusal, or was uniquely cruel; Eros's account concerns the coercion inherent in ownership and inability to give meaningful refusal. Likewise, no consensual-romance retcon is made.
- This answer creates a difficult truth for Aemilia: the husband who showed extraordinary restraint toward her childhood nevertheless used an enslaved woman's body within ordinary Roman master-slave power, including on occasions when Eros would have preferred to refuse.
- Eros's exact age beyond adulthood, appearance, origin and ordinary duties remain unestablished unless they emerge naturally in play.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia and Eros sit alone behind a closed door. Eros has admitted that there were occasions she wanted Lucius not to come to her but did not regard refusal as available, while also acknowledging her feelings toward him were not uniformly hatred or fear. Lucius remains occupied with Publius/Aufidius; Pia remains off duty.
