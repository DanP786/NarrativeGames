- After Marcus leaves, Aemilia and Lucius share an affectionate, impulsive adult marital interlude, non-explicitly narrated. Lucius then returns toward the urgent Publius/Aufidius matter before today's standstill expires.
- Aemilia conducts a private household-discipline inquiry with senior steward Gaius Philargyrus. She learns physical punishment occurs several times in an ordinary month and that lesser delegated supervisors sometimes strike enslaved subordinates without reporting each incident.
- Aemilia orders a one-month trial in the urban domus under Philargyrus's immediate administration: no punitive beating or striking by anyone. Necessary immediate force to stop assault or protect someone from bodily danger remains permitted, including protection of Aemilia and her pregnancy, but may not become punishment afterward.
- Aemilia orders immediate modest rewards for competent ordinary work rather than vague future promises or rewarding exhaustion. Philargyrus will track offenses, responses and rewards so the trial can be assessed. He will inform Lucius because Lucius retains ultimate household authority; unless countermanded, implementation begins immediately.
- Pia's weekly day free ordinary duties still needs formal standing instruction; Pia remains off duty today.
- Aemilia's private conversation with Eros has ended. Eros disclosed that Lucius's past sexual access was not something she always wanted and that refusal was not meaningfully available; she prefers remaining with her children to sale/transfer but would choose freedom if she could remain near/protect them. Aemilia promised no retaliation for candor and said she and Lucius do not wish to separate Eros from Fortunata/Felix; no manumission promise was made.
- Eros identified **Menophilus**, an enslaved senior cellar/store servant, as abusing delegated authority through arbitrary striking and intimidation. He oversees workers handling wine, oil, lamps, serving stores and related supplies and answers upward through the administrative chain to Philargyrus.
- Eros identified **Chryseros**, an enslaved stores clerk/assistant already handling part of Menophilus's work, as a plausible replacement. Chryseros is exacting and not especially warm but is not known by Eros to strike subordinates from temper/humiliation. Philargyrus would need to assess his full competence.
- Eros identified **Thais**, an enslaved senior woman involved in supervising laundry/linen work, as a lesser concern: no known beatings, but favoritism and punitive manipulation of work assignments, sleep and small comforts. Eros cautioned that accusations require corroboration and household cruelty is not reducible to a few villains.
- Aemilia disclosed to Eros that Lucius refused adult marital intimacy with Aemilia until eighteen and that Aemilia had once been jealous of Eros bearing Lucius's children. Their conversation sharpened the distinction between Aemilia's constrained freeborn status and Eros's enslavement.
- After dismissing Eros with thanks, Aemilia deliberately goes into the service areas used by the household's enslaved workers rather than summoning another person to an elite room.
- The service spaces are more cramped, functional and worn than the reception/family rooms: storage, work surfaces, vessels, baskets, linens and the traffic of domestic labor. Aemilia's presence interrupts ordinary rhythms because the pregnant domina does not routinely enter these working spaces simply to observe.
- Aemilia smiles at the recognition and rests one hand on her visibly pregnant belly while looking around. She realizes this is literally a portion of the domus she has never seen despite living in the household for roughly six years.
- Aemilia then walks slowly through the service rooms without speaking, observing rather than questioning anyone. The silence itself keeps workers alert at first; they do not know what she is inspecting or whether some fault has drawn her below stairs.
- As she continues without reprimand or summons, ordinary work cautiously resumes around her. She sees the physical rhythms usually hidden behind finished household service: lifting and shifting amphorae, measuring stores, sorting and folding cloth, cleaning vessels, carrying water and supplies, repairing worn household objects, and people moving around one another in constrained working space.
- She also sees evidence of hierarchy among the enslaved workers without yet knowing every relationship: some people give terse instructions while others immediately obey; desirable and unpleasant tasks are not distributed evenly; workers watch supervisors as well as Aemilia. None of this independently corroborates Eros's specific allegations against Menophilus or Thais yet.
- Aemilia's silent presence makes clandestine abuse unlikely to occur openly in front of her. Observation can show conditions, workload and hierarchy, but not easily the conduct people deliberately hide from the domina.
- Aemilia has not yet announced why she is there, asked for Menophilus/Chryseros/Thais, or revealed Eros as a source. Menophilus, Chryseros and Thais are not yet identified by sight to Aemilia.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia is walking silently through the service/work areas, observing ordinary labor as it gradually resumes around her. Nearby enslaved workers remain conscious of her presence but are beginning to return to their tasks. Lucius remains occupied with Publius/Aufidius; Pia remains off duty.
