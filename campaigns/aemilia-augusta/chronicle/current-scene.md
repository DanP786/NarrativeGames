# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, several weeks after S01. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant. Evening at Lucius and Aemilia's Valerian domus in Rome.
- Earlier Aemilia received Tullia concerning Publius's debt and independently checked Lucius's household records. The records substantially support Tullia's account: Publius originally received 60,000 sesterces for workshop capital; 18,000 is presently due as an overdue scheduled payment plus interest/arrears; earlier payments were made; workshop assets are pledged; Gnaeus Fabius Priscus appears to be enforcing within ordinary delegated authority without a fresh personal review by Lucius.
- While replacing those records, Aemilia found and deliberately read private tablets belonging to Lucius. They showed that during her later adolescence her father Marcus Aemilius discreetly explored the financial consequences of ending her still-childless marriage and the possibility of another future match, while Lucius secretly monitored those efforts and withheld the matter from Aemilia. The inquiries never advanced to an established replacement match.
- Aemilia also discovered premarital due-diligence reports compiled about her when she was about twelve, covering family, dowry, health, education, temperament and perceived dynastic suitability. Some informants had noticed her childhood habit of listening closely to political/estate conversation and remembering names and disputes. Lucius's notes rejected confident predictions about a child's future fertility or adult character and stated that he would judge Aemilia himself once he knew her.
- Aemilia restored all records exactly where she found them. Pia did not read the material. Aemilia then ordered Pia, 'We never entered the study.' Pia accepted the command and knows only that Aemilia entered Lucius's study, spent time alone there, and now wants that fact concealed.
- Lucius returns after Senate business in his ordinary manner, unaware of the study incident. Aemilia greets him warmly and enters his arms; he responds affectionately.
- Aemilia tells him Tullia came concerning Publius, a freedman of Tullia's husband whose metalworking workshop owes the Valerian household. Tullia says delayed customer payments and poor-quality metal have caused a cash shortage; she wants delay before pledged workshop property is taken. Tullia cannot verify Publius's books or total outside debts and admits other trade obligations and at least one overdue account. Aemilia promised only to put the matter before Lucius.
- Aemilia proposes investigating Publius's other debts and potentially consolidating claims to preserve the productive workshop and gain control over recovery. Lucius agrees this can make commercial sense if the figures support it, pauses Priscus pending fuller accounting, and invites Aemilia to review the figures with him; she accepts.
- Aemilia says she wants what is best for the house. Lucius affirms that her identification with the household's interests is why he seeks her judgment.
- When Aemilia asks whether the house can afford intervention, Lucius says yes: the Valerian household is not in financial distress, though wealth is not the same as limitless liquid cash. He says he would tell her if the household faced material strain affecting her or the domus.
- Aemilia then leans in, tilts her head, and asks, 'And if someone wanted my dowry back?' The question directly touches the secret material she read earlier about her father having explored dowry return and possible dissolution during the years of childlessness.
- Lucius immediately becomes more attentive. He answers the legal/practical question without assuming she knows the hidden history: if the marriage were ended and a lawful claim for return of the dowry arose, it would be a serious obligation but not one that would ruin the Valerian house. He emphasizes that a father cannot simply demand the dowry back while the marriage continues as though recalling a loan; return is tied to the legal position of the marriage and the dowry terms. He then asks Aemilia why she is asking about her dowry specifically, because the question is pointed enough to attract his notice.
- Lucius still does NOT know Aemilia inspected his household accounts or read the private reports. Aemilia has not revealed what she knows about her father's inquiries, Lucius's surveillance of them, or the premarital reports.
- CURRENT MOMENT: supper has shifted from light commercial discussion to a potentially charged marital subject. Aemilia has deliberately introduced the possibility of someone seeking her dowry back. Lucius has answered cautiously and is now watching her, asking why she chose that question.
