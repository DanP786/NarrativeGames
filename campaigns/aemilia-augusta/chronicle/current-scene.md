- Aemilia remains physically weakened after major blood loss, prolonged inactivity and limited eating. She can dress, walk and work, but the previous day's overexertion has not been erased by one night's sleep. No fever, renewed hemorrhage or new medical complication is established.
- At midday Terentia, 45, Lucius's stepmother and current matron of the elder Messalla household, visits. Aemilia receives her without changing her plain working presentation.
- Terentia observes Aemilia's pallor, weakness, unusually simple hair, subdued manner and household work. When asked how she is, Aemilia says **“I failed.”** Terentia asks what she believes she failed to do.
- Aemilia answers, **“My duty to your family.”** This establishes that Aemilia is interpreting the pregnancy loss through her dynastic duty as Lucius's wife to bear legitimate children for the Valerian family.
- Terentia does not deny that producing legitimate children is one of the duties expected of an elite Roman wife; she refuses instead the conclusion that a lost pregnancy is a moral or willed failure. She distinguishes an obligation to enter marriage, share the household and attempt to bear children from the uncontrollable survival of any particular pregnancy.
- Terentia also treats Aemilia as part of the Valerian family rather than merely an instrument for producing it: she tells Aemilia that the family lost the child, but that the family did not lose Aemilia, and that nearly bleeding to death is not a duty she owed them.
