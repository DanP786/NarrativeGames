# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, several weeks after S01. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant. Evening at Lucius and Aemilia's Valerian domus in Rome.
- Earlier Aemilia received Tullia concerning Publius's debt and independently checked Lucius's household records. The records substantially support Tullia's account: Publius originally received 60,000 sesterces for workshop capital; 18,000 is presently due as an overdue scheduled payment plus interest/arrears; earlier payments were made; workshop assets are pledged; Gnaeus Fabius Priscus appears to be enforcing within ordinary delegated authority without a fresh personal review by Lucius.
- While replacing those records, Aemilia found and deliberately read private tablets belonging to Lucius. They showed that during her later adolescence her father Marcus Aemilius discreetly explored the financial consequences of ending her still-childless marriage and the possibility of another future match, while Lucius secretly monitored those efforts and withheld the matter from Aemilia. The inquiries never advanced to an established replacement match.
- Aemilia also discovered premarital due-diligence reports compiled about her when she was about twelve, covering family, dowry, health, education, temperament and perceived dynastic suitability. Some informants had noticed her childhood habit of listening closely to political/estate conversation and remembering names and disputes. Lucius's notes rejected confident predictions about a child's future fertility or adult character and stated that he would judge Aemilia himself once he knew her.
- Aemilia restored all records exactly where she found them. Pia did not read the material. Aemilia then ordered Pia, 'We never entered the study.' Pia accepted the command and knows only that Aemilia entered Lucius's study, spent time alone there, and now wants that fact concealed.
- At supper Aemilia and Lucius discuss Tullia's petition and Publius's debt. Aemilia proposes investigating other creditors and potentially consolidating claims to preserve the productive workshop and gain control over recovery. Lucius agrees this may make commercial sense if the figures support it, pauses Priscus pending fuller accounting, and invites Aemilia to review the figures with him; she accepts.
- Aemilia says she wants what is best for the house. Lucius affirms that her identification with the household's interests is why he seeks her judgment. He reassures her that the Valerian house is not in financial distress and could afford a sensible intervention in Publius's case.
- Aemilia asks what would happen if someone wanted her dowry back. Lucius says her father cannot simply recall it while the marriage continues; lawful return would arise in connection with the marriage ending and would be substantial but not ruinous. He notices the specificity and asks why she is asking.
- Aemilia confesses that she searched for Publius's debt record in Lucius's study, stumbled upon his report concerning her father, and deliberately read the private material, including the older premarital reports about herself. Lucius says she crossed a private boundary, asks her not to read his private papers again, but refuses to use that breach to avoid his own concealment. Aemilia apologizes; Lucius accepts the apology and says he will neither punish nor humiliate her for admitting it. He apologizes in turn for withholding her father's inquiries once she was old enough to understand them.
- Aemilia explains that she had already deduced her father would investigate dowry recovery and remarriage if he was asking her to end the marriage. Lucius accepts the logic and ruefully recognizes that his own education of Aemilia made shielding her through ignorance unrealistic.
- Asked what actually troubles her, Aemilia says she is concerned whether her father is STILL seeking the end of the marriage. Lucius says he has no current evidence Marcus is still pursuing dissolution, but does not claim certainty; he promises to tell Aemilia if he learns the efforts have resumed.
- The conversation softens into affectionate teasing about Aemilia's former shyness, private insolence, punishment, and her precise use of grammar. Their hands remain joined.
- Aemilia asks what Lucius thought of her before and after they met. Lucius says the premarital reports gave him a dynastic outline but he distrusted predictions about a child's future fertility or adult character. After meeting her he found her shy, dutiful and intensely observant, and felt the age difference starkly: whatever the legal marriage, she was a twelve-year-old child placed in his household. His curiosity became what she might become if given room and education.
- Asked why he saw her as a child when many Roman men would not have been troubled by the distinction, Lucius says no single philosophy caused it. He had seen young brides before and distinguished legal marriageability from being grown; meeting Aemilia made the distinction concrete. He also weighed the unequal physical risks of pregnancy and saw no necessity to hurry merely because custom permitted it. He acknowledges many respectable Roman men, including his father, would regard his delay as unnecessary.
- Aemilia asks whether she herself convinced him because she was immature or inexperienced. Lucius says she was inexperienced and dependent rather than foolish; her intelligence was already apparent and made him more interested in allowing her time and education.
- While thoughtful after this exchange, Aemilia suddenly feels an unfamiliar painless movement in her belly and wonders if it is the child. A second sensation follows. Aemilia exclaims, 'Again!' draws Lucius's hand to the place on her belly and describes it as 'a flutter.' Repeated movement makes her interpretation more persuasive to them, though they still cannot establish with certainty from sensation alone exactly what it is. Lucius becomes visibly moved and intensely attentive. Because the movement is still subtle and early, his hand may not detect what Aemilia can feel internally; he does not falsely claim to feel it if nothing is perceptible. He keeps his palm where she placed it and waits with her, hopeful and quietly awed.
- Aemilia nods, smiles, and says, 'our child.' Lucius answers with unusually unguarded tenderness, 'Our child,' accepting the moment without qualification even though he still cannot necessarily feel the flutter himself. He keeps his hand on her belly where she placed it, looking between her face and the place beneath his palm, and tells her quietly that the child has made itself known to its mother first. He does not turn the moment into a dynastic claim about a son or heir.
- Overcome by the shared moment, Lucius carefully eases Aemilia back along the dining bench so she is supported and kisses her. The affection deepens into mutually welcomed marital intimacy between the adult spouses. The sexual encounter is not described; the scene fades over it. Lucius remains mindful of Aemilia's pregnancy and comfort. No pain, bleeding, or other complication is established.
- Afterward Lucius looks down at Aemilia with a possessive intensity consistent with his hierarchical Roman understanding of marriage and household authority. What catches him is the contrast between the formidable young woman who challenges his reasoning and the private trust with which she has yielded herself to him. His possessiveness is expressed non-explicitly: a steady gaze, keeping her close, and language of wife, household, and belonging rather than graphic attention to the sexual aftermath. He does not mistake her submission in this intimate moment for lack of judgment or erase the agency she has repeatedly shown.
- Aemilia sits up afterward, clasping the loosened top of her clothing closed while her skirt remains disordered around her legs, and says, 'I try to always obey.' Lucius takes the claim seriously rather than simply praising submission. He distinguishes obedience from silence or thoughtlessness: she generally honors his final household authority, but he expects her to think, advise, disagree before a decision, and tell him when she believes he is wrong. The private-paper breach remains an exception already addressed rather than proof she is habitually disobedient.
- Lucius still does not know that Aemilia ordered Pia to conceal their presence in the study unless she tells him.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia is sitting beside Lucius after their off-page intimacy, holding her loosened clothing closed and telling him she tries always to obey. Lucius remains possessive and affectionate but is answering within the established distinction between Roman marital authority and the independent judgment he deliberately cultivated in her.
