- At midday Terentia, Lucius's stepmother and matron of the elder Messalla household, visits. Aemilia receives her in her plain working presentation, with much of her hair loose down her back.
- Aemilia tells Terentia **“I failed,”** explaining **“My duty to your family.”** She voices fear that the miscarriage may revive earlier pressure from both families to end the marriage. Terentia establishes that no one has presently threatened the marriage and Lucius has not said he wishes to put Aemilia aside.
- Aemilia explains the marital breach: Eros repeated a private conversation to Lucius, including her judgment that Aemilia was sheltered/weak and likely to yield if Lucius disagreed. Lucius's response felt to Aemilia like defending Eros against his wife. During the argument Aemilia tried to leave. Lucius physically stopped her once, promised not to stop her again, then later shut the door and forced her shoulders/back against it when she tried to leave again. He recognized the broken promise and said **“I was wrong.”** Pain began during/after the argument; bleeding and miscarriage followed later that night. No medical cause was established, and neither Aemilia nor Terentia can establish that Lucius caused the loss.
- Aemilia tells Terentia that Lucius says he does not love Eros but cares about her because she is the mother of his children. Aemilia breaks on **“mother of his children”** because she has just lost her own first child and has no surviving child, then says **“She has a power over him.”** Terentia acknowledges Eros has influence through long familiarity and motherhood of Lucius's surviving children, but stresses that Lucius chooses what weight to give Eros's words and is responsible for his own judgment.
- Aemilia asks what she should do, saying she is not spiteful and does not want anyone punished but feels she is being punished instead. Terentia says mercy need not mean having no boundaries: Eros need not be punished, her children should not be drawn into the quarrel, but Aemilia need not treat Eros as a confidante and Lucius must answer for his own choices. Aemilia need not restore warmth or intimacy merely to prove she is kind.
- Aemilia clarifies, **“I tried to tell Lucius what she said was not alright. But he kept defending her. That is why I tried to leave.”** Terentia understands that Aemilia attempted to address the grievance directly before withdrawing and that the attempt to leave followed repeated failure to feel heard.
- Terentia asks whether, since the miscarriage, Lucius has tried to speak specifically about this breach—not Aemilia's health, work, eating or sleeping, but the quarrel, his broken promise and the damage to trust. Aemilia answers **“No.”**
- Terentia takes the answer seriously. She does not infer that Lucius is indifferent: his care for Aemilia's health and his restraint since the loss remain established. But she concludes from Aemilia's account that Lucius has allowed the central marital dispute to remain untouched while attending to its aftermath. She tells Aemilia that grief may explain his silence but cannot repair what silence leaves unresolved.
- Terentia advises that Aemilia has already made the grievance known and should not be required to chase Lucius for acknowledgment. If the marriage is to recover, Lucius must eventually choose to reopen the matter himself and address his conduct, rather than relying on time, household routine or resumed intimacy to erase it.
