Eros insists she intended temporary removal and return, not onward sale; Celer independently corroborates that stated original scope.

- Consideration to Celer consisted of small personal funds plus promised commercially useful Valerian household information. Eros delivered some limited ordinary purchasing/supplier information to prove usefulness. Wider promised disclosures were not completed. Lucius has ordered an audit rather than assuming catastrophic compromise.
- Eros later learned directly from Celer that Pia had not been returned and the scheme had moved into commercial danger. She did not know Pia's exact location/full downstream chain, but knew Celer and knew the temporary arrangement had failed. She remained silent to protect herself despite having ordinary channels through which she could have warned the household.
- Aemilia rejects Eros's claimed temporary intent: **“I don’t believe you. To think so little of me that I would fall apart without Pia for a few days? No. You didn’t intend her to return.”** Lucius tells her Celer independently corroborates the stated temporary-removal scope, reopening the old wound of Lucius appearing to qualify/defend Eros.
- Aemilia remains furious through the interrogation. Afterward, in Lucius's study, she says: **“You defended her again.”** Lucius acknowledges that he did defend Eros against one unsupported accusation while not defending her conduct, and recognizes that contradicting Aemilia before Eros repeated the shape of an old marital wound.
- Aemilia says: **“You undermine our marriage once again.”** Lucius accepts that by choosing to correct Aemilia before Eros he gave Eros another view of division between husband and wife. He calls it a failure of judgment and marital solidarity while maintaining he could not truthfully endorse an unsupported accusation.
- Aemilia's anger gives way to defeat: **“But you did.”** Lucius answers **“Yes.”** He does not repeat his justification, touch her, or ask immediate forgiveness. He says: **“I cannot make that moment unhappen.”**
- Aemilia says Eros has committed crimes against the house and punishment must match the crime. Lucius agrees punishment must rest on established acts and asks for Aemilia's promised judgment.
- Aemilia's judgment: **“Selling her would be too cruel, as much as I’d like her to hurt in retaliation. Pia has stated she never wants to see her again or have access to either us to do it again. I say send her to the fields, her children stay here. She will see them less.”**
- Proposed disposition: Eros removed from urban/domestic household and sent to agricultural labor/property away from Roman domus; Fortunata and Felix remain in household; Eros loses ordinary daily access to children and all ordinary access to Aemilia/Pia. Aemilia explicitly rejects sale as too cruel despite retaliatory anger.

- Lucius agrees that a grave household betrayal cannot end in a consequence so slight that others learn Aemilia can be targeted without meaningful cost. He distinguishes deterrence from indiscriminate terror: the household need not be brutalized, but the consequence must be visible enough that servants understand Eros was removed for deliberately acting against the domina and the house.

He says in substance that he cannot ask Aemilia to carry the whole burden of proving she is not weak while he repeatedly gives others reason to expect division between them.

Before the final transfer is executed, existing corroboration/audit work will be completed and practical placement on a Valerian agricultural property arranged. Fortunata and Felix remain in the Roman household and are not punished for Eros's acts.
