# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, several weeks after S01. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant. The pregnancy is more visibly apparent but no complication is established.
- Morning at Lucius and Aemilia's Valerian domus in Rome. Lucius is away from the domus. The discreet Rufus-Valeria gathering is now several weeks past. Terentia remains cautiously favorable; Valeria and Rufus remain unaware of the matchmaking purpose; Marcus Valerius Messalla has not been told. Rufus's Atalanta-poem search remains unresolved.
- Aemilia's standing within the household has continued to consolidate. Her pregnancy makes her dynastic importance more visible to visitors and dependents as well as to the family.
- Aemilia receives Tullia in the tablinum with Pia attending. Tullia is an adult married Roman woman in her thirties from a respectable senatorial-connected family, known to Aemilia by sight from elite social and religious occasions but not as an intimate. Tullia's own attendant remains near the doorway.
- Tullia says she came because she would rather put the matter before the mistress of the Valerian house before involving either woman's husband.
- Tullia explains that one of her husband's freedmen, Publius, has been pressed for payment on an obligation by a Valerian household agent. The debt itself is not denied. The agent has threatened to seize pledged workshop equipment and stock if payment is not produced promptly. Publius and his wife say this would prevent the workshop from continuing to earn. Tullia asks for the collection to be paused long enough for the accounts and pledged property to be examined.
- Aemilia asks whether Publius's situation was explained to the agent. Tullia says yes according to Publius and his wife, while carefully acknowledging she did not witness the exchange. The reported answer was that the pledge exists precisely so the Valerian household does not bear the risk of Publius's bad season.
- Aemilia answers: 'This household would not want a man's livelihood stripped without consideration. I will take this to my husband when he returns.' She thereby promises review and intercession with Lucius, but does not forgive the debt, declare the agent wrong, order the collection stopped on her own authority, or accept Publius's account as proven.
- Tullia visibly relaxes at the distinction. She thanks Aemilia and says that consideration is all she came to ask for. She does not press Aemilia for a stronger promise.
- The identity of the Valerian agent, the amount and origin of the debt, whether the threatened seizure is contractually justified, and whether Publius's account is truthful remain unestablished. The matter now awaits Lucius's return and Aemilia's decision about how to present it to him.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Tullia has received Aemilia's promise to raise the matter with Lucius. The audience is naturally approaching its close unless Aemilia asks further questions.
