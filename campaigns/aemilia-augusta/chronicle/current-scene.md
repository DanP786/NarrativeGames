# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, several weeks after S01. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant. Morning at Lucius and Aemilia's Valerian domus in Rome; Lucius is away.
- Earlier Aemilia received Tullia concerning Publius's debt to Lucius's household and independently checked the relevant accounts. The records substantially support Tullia's description of the Valerian side of the obligation.
- While replacing those records, Aemilia accidentally dislodged a private tablet packet in Lucius's hand referring to her father Marcus Aemilius Lepidus, her marriage, and her dowry in the period before her eighteenth birthday. The packet had been kept separate from ordinary accounts.
- Aemilia tells Pia, 'Stand outside.' Pia obeys and leaves the office, remaining outside the door. Aemilia then deliberately reads the private tablets rather than merely replacing them.
- The packet records inquiries Lucius quietly ordered after Marcus Aemilius began pressing, during Aemilia's later adolescence, for clearer assurances about the marriage's future because it remained without a legitimate child. Lucius suspected that Marcus might eventually use continued childlessness to argue for ending/rearranging the marriage or to seek return/redeployment of Aemilia's substantial dowry.
- The collected reports show Marcus Aemilius had privately asked legal and financial advisers about what would happen to Aemilia's dowry if the marriage ended without children and about the practical leverage available to her natal family. He also had valuations made concerning portions of the dowry property. These inquiries were exploratory rather than proof that a divorce or removal had been decided.
- More personally, one report records that Marcus Aemilius had sounded out, through an intermediary and without Aemilia's knowledge, whether another respectable senatorial household might be receptive to Aemilia as a future marriage prospect if the Valerian marriage failed to produce an heir and was dissolved. No formal betrothal, agreement, named replacement husband, or completed plan is established in the packet.
- Lucius's own notes show he obtained these reports secretly and deliberately did not confront Marcus Aemilius at the time. He regarded disclosure of his refusal to consummate the marriage while Aemilia was a child as dangerous because it could intensify pressure from both families, expose the marriage's private history, and potentially place Aemilia herself at the center of an elite dispute. He therefore monitored Marcus's inquiries while keeping the information from Aemilia.
- A later note, from shortly before Aemilia turned eighteen, states in substance that Marcus's inquiries had not advanced to a formal arrangement and that Lucius intended to hold the marriage rather than permit Aemilia to be treated as a failed dynastic placement because of a childlessness Lucius himself had deliberately caused by waiting. The note is protective but also controlling: Lucius made this judgment and conducted the surveillance without consulting Aemilia.
- Nothing in the packet establishes that Marcus Aemilius committed an illegal act or definitively resolved to remove Aemilia from the marriage. It establishes contingency planning and discreet exploration. Nothing establishes that Lucius fabricated the reports, but Aemilia has not independently verified their sources.
- Pia has not read the packet and remains outside. Lucius does not yet know Aemilia has found or read it.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia stands alone in Lucius's office with the private packet open. She has learned that before she turned eighteen, her father discreetly explored the financial consequences of ending her childless marriage and the possibility of another future match, while Lucius secretly monitored those efforts and withheld the entire matter from her. She must decide what to do with that knowledge and with the tablets before Lucius returns.
