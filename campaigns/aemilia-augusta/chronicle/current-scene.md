# Current scene

Roughly a week has passed at the Valerian country villa since Aemilia and Lucius first resumed marital intimacy; the pregnancy loss is now more than two weeks in the past, though exact day count remains loose. Aemilia is eighteen and has returned with Lucius to the Roman domus.

She still does not smile freely and does not laugh. Their adult marital intimacy has resumed voluntarily. No new pregnancy is established.

The initial discreet information-only inquiry produced its first uncertain lead: a witness along the route associated with Pia's genuine leave recalls a woman broadly matching Pia's description with unfamiliar men and remembers that she did not appear to be moving with them freely. The witness cannot establish identity with certainty or say where the group ultimately went.

A genuinely voluntary escape remains a distinct case. Lucius supports the order and places Valerian resources and connections behind it.

Lucius refuses false certainty: he acknowledges that they may wish they had searched sooner, but does not presume Pia's eventual forgiveness or invent specific abuses before evidence exists.

During genuine leave she was unlawfully seized and subsequently held/sold onward as enslaved property despite already belonging to the Valerian household. Eros had a hand in setting the initial interception in motion: seeking to isolate Aemilia and remove Pia's close support, Eros deliberately passed information or otherwise facilitated an outside interception of Pia. Eros nevertheless bears real responsibility for creating the opportunity that led to Pia's seizure and disappearance. Aemilia, Lucius and the household do not know this hidden truth; it must be uncovered through plausible investigation. Exact intermediary, method, intended duration/outcome, payment if any, and Eros's current knowledge of Pia's ultimate whereabouts remain to be established through play. No specific abuse beyond unlawful seizure, coercive detention and onward enslavement/sale is canonized.
- OOC GM canon: Eros did **not** poison Aemilia and did not cause the miscarriage. The pregnancy loss remains medically unresolved and must not later be converted into an Eros poisoning plot absent an explicit future retcon.
- Aemilia's one-month trial prohibiting punitive beatings continued in Rome during the retreat. Earlier records showed no material deterioration in serious discipline. The full-month assessment has not yet been formally presented to Aemilia.
- Eros remains in Rome. Lucius has ended her former privileged access: she has no discretionary direct/private access to him and is not to be received alone; necessary child or household matters pass through ordinary channels or occur with others present. This restriction has not lapsed during the villa week. Eros knows her own prior approach to Lucius and his refusal, but not the spouses' private exchanges beyond what she directly witnessed or was told.

Household members can observe Aemilia and Lucius returning together and functioning as husband and wife, but cannot legitimately infer that grief or the marriage is completely healed.

Four-year-old Fortunata runs to him calling **“Father!”**, with approximately two-year-old Felix and six-year-old Hilarus following and Eros accompanying them. Lucius receives all three children warmly without restoring Eros's former private access.
- Aemilia answers Fortunata's concern about her health with **“I am almost better,”** and softness reaches her eyes. Fortunata then asks with a four-year-old's directness, **“Is it because of the baby? Mother said it died.”** Eros has given Fortunata only a simple child-level explanation that Aemilia's baby died; no private marital detail, medical cause, blame, fetal sex or other circumstances are established as having been told to the child.
- Fortunata then asks where the baby went. Already emotionally strained by the Pia news, Aemilia swallows, hesitates with **“umm,”** looks briefly to Lucius and back to Fortunata, bites her lip, says **“Excuse me,”** and leaves rather than answering. She is visibly fighting her emotions. This is not anger at Fortunata and does not establish hostility toward the child.

- Aemilia finds a quiet, out-of-the-way corner within the domus and braces herself against the wall. Her physical recovery is incomplete, and the combination of the Pia news and Fortunata's innocent question overwhelms the composure she maintained on returning home.

They discuss Eros bringing Lucius's children to him immediately after his week away with Aemilia. Their judgment is household gossip rather than established fact: one calls the timing cruel to the domina after the loss; the other cautions that the children naturally wanted their father and that Eros could plausibly claim she was only bringing them to him. They agree the appearance is ugly given Eros's recent loss of privileged access and Aemilia's visible grief. They explicitly distinguish blame toward Eros from blame toward the children.

They do not know Eros helped set Pia's interception in motion, do not know the spouses' private discussions, and have no evidence Eros intended Fortunata's questions to hurt Aemilia.

When she passes the servants, she does not rebuke or expose them. Instead she gently places a hand on the first servant's arm and meets her eyes for a beat before continuing. The gesture makes clear enough that Aemilia heard without threatening punishment and preserves the servant's dignity. Aemilia then goes directly to her bedchamber.
