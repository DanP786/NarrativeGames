# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, later the same morning after Aemilia first felt repeated possible fetal movement. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant, at the Valerian domus in Rome.
- The previous day Aemilia received Tullia concerning Publius's debt and independently checked Lucius's household records. Publius originally received 60,000 sesterces for workshop capital; 18,000 is presently due as an overdue scheduled payment plus interest/arrears; earlier payments were made; workshop assets are pledged; Gnaeus Fabius Priscus appears to have been enforcing within ordinary delegated authority.
- While replacing those records, Aemilia deliberately read private tablets belonging to Lucius. They showed that during her later adolescence her father Marcus Aemilius discreetly explored the financial consequences of ending her still-childless marriage and another possible future match, while Lucius secretly monitored those efforts and withheld the matter from Aemilia. No established replacement match resulted.
- Aemilia also read premarital due-diligence reports about herself at about twelve. Lucius's annotations rejected confident predictions about a child's fertility or adult character and showed particular interest in reports that quiet Aemilia listened closely to adult political and estate discussions and remembered details.
- Aemilia restored the records. Pia did not read them. Aemilia ordered Pia, 'We never entered the study.' Pia accepted the command and knows only that Aemilia entered the study, spent time alone, and wants the fact concealed. Lucius still does not know of this concealment order.
- Aemilia confessed to Lucius that she read both sets of private reports. Lucius said she crossed a boundary and asked her not to read his private papers again, while also apologizing for withholding her father's inquiries once she was old enough to understand them. He accepted her apology and refused further punishment or humiliation.
- Aemilia explained she had already inferred that Marcus would investigate dowry recovery and remarriage because Marcus had asked her to end the marriage. Her principal concern is whether Marcus still seeks dissolution. Lucius says he has no current evidence that Marcus does, but promises to tell Aemilia if renewed efforts come to his attention.
- At supper Aemilia proposed investigating Publius's other creditors and potentially consolidating claims to preserve the productive workshop and gain control over recovery. Lucius saw possible commercial merit but wanted full accounts first. He decided Priscus was to pause Valerian collection while creditor amounts, securities, receivables, stock and earning capacity were examined. Aemilia would sit with Lucius when the figures arrived before he decided whether to restructure, consolidate, or proceed against the pledged workshop.
- During the evening Aemilia felt two unfamiliar painless flutters in her belly and interpreted them as their child. Lucius could not necessarily feel the subtle movement externally but became openly moved. Aemilia said, 'our child,' and Lucius echoed her words with unusual tenderness.
- The excitement led to affectionate kissing and mutually welcomed marital intimacy between the adult spouses; the sexual encounter remained off-page. No pain, bleeding or other complication was established.
- The following morning Aemilia and Lucius shared an extended affectionate conversation about marriage, authority and permanence. Lucius told her he intended to give her many children, spoke against allowing Marcus or other relatives to determine the marriage's future, kissed her and said, 'You are mine.' Aemilia answered with laughter and an embrace, then said she was pleased that he treated her as his wife rather than his pupil/ward. Lucius acknowledged the transition from protecting and educating a child-bride to expecting counsel, disagreement, adult responsibility, marital intimacy and investment in the domus from his adult wife.
- Later that morning the Publius matter becomes materially uglier before the Valerian pause can solve it. Another creditor, acting independently of Priscus and the Valerian claim, moves against Publius's workshop. Two adult enslaved metalworkers are removed as property against the outside obligation, and one is struck during resistance. This is not ordered by Lucius or Priscus. The removal damages the workshop's productive capacity and makes clear that human beings as well as tools and stock are caught inside the creditor contest.
- Aemilia asks whether they are too late. Lucius says the harm already inflicted cannot be undone, but money, purchase, assignment of claims or legal pressure may still recover the craftsmen if they have not been transferred onward. He warns that recovering them may mean the Valerian household itself purchasing legal ownership rather than freeing them.
- Aemilia commits herself to intervention, hoping Valerian control can preserve the workshop and stop people being beaten. Lucius agrees that preventing gratuitous violence and preserving productive capacity are compatible aims, but insists that acquiring control also makes their household responsible for discipline, retention and future disposition of enslaved workers.
- Aemilia learns that the Valerian household itself punishes enslaved people, though she has been shielded from harsher administration. Ordinary discipline is delegated; serious offenses can bring physical punishment, and outlying properties can be harsher or less visible. Her observation that servants bring her pregnancy chairs without explicit orders leads Lucius to explain that households operate through standing expectations, precedent and anticipated wishes; adult authority therefore includes responsibility for acts not individually commanded.
- Priscus arrives with initial documents. The outside creditor is still holding the two craftsmen in Rome rather than having sold them onward; the injured man is alive. The creditor asserts a secured claim connected to Publius's trade obligations, but priority against the Valerian security remains disputed/unverified. Delay increases the chance of onward sale or further seizure.
- Aemilia says they had no time to react and proposes two immediate steps: establish every creditor and amount owed, and send word to the creditor who seized the craftsmen, inviting that creditor to the Valerian domus to be hosted and negotiate an understanding. Lucius judges the approach sound as patronage/social strategy but warns that hospitality must not signal concession on priority or an agreement to pay whatever is demanded. He orders Priscus to compile the creditor schedule and send a carefully worded invitation in Lucius's name: the creditor is invited to discuss orderly satisfaction of legitimate claims and preservation of productive assets, while the Valerian household reserves its position on security and priority. The invitation also implicitly signals that a senatorial household is now personally attentive to the matter.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia, Lucius and Priscus are at the worktable. Aemilia's proposal has moved the response from reactive alarm to an active negotiation strategy. Priscus is to identify the full creditor field and summon the creditor holding the two craftsmen to the domus without conceding legal priority. The creditor has not yet accepted, and the complete debt total remains unknown.
