# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, more than a week after Aemilia's pregnancy loss; exact day count remains loose. Aemilia is eighteen. She and Lucius are at a Valerian country villa outside Rome for a temporary retreat with a small domestic retinue.
- Earlier events remain active canon: Eros's disclosure caused a severe marital argument; Lucius twice physically stopped Aemilia leaving and the second restraint broke his explicit promise not to stop her again. He later admitted **“I was wrong.”** The breach is now entering an explicitly acknowledged process of repair, but is not erased or fully resolved.
- Aemilia lost her approximately four-month pregnancy after hours of pain and bleeding in the shared bedchamber despite attendance by Demetrios and the trusted midwife. No medical cause was established. She then remained unconscious for about a day from severe weakness/blood loss before waking.
- Pia failed to return from her first genuine day of leave and remains absent. Her reason and whereabouts/fate remain unestablished to Aemilia and Lucius.
- After grief-driven withdrawal, Aemilia returned to household duties in a changed manner: controlled and soft-voiced but without her former warmth, dimpling smiles or instinct to cushion commands. She remains physically weakened after major blood loss, prolonged inactivity and limited eating. No fever, renewed hemorrhage or new medical complication is established.
- Terentia knows the substance of the marital breach involving Eros and Lucius, including Lucius physically stopping Aemilia twice and breaking his promise. Terentia advised that mercy toward Eros does not require confidence or warmth and that Lucius remains responsible for his own choices.
- Valeria Tertia is well, remains unmarried and unaware that her parents are considering Gaius Vibius Rufus as a possible husband. Marcus is investigating Rufus as a dynastic prospect while Terentia scrutinizes his temperament and motives. No engagement has been made.
- In Rome, Aemilia's one-month trial prohibiting punitive beatings continues. The steward's records so far show no material increase in serious disciplinary incidents compared with the prior household norm, though the period is too short and disrupted to prove positive improvement.
- A discreet information-only inquiry into Pia is underway in Rome at Aemilia's order. It seeks sightings and information concerning injury, detention, or other circumstances without automatically ordering Pia seized or punished if found. No result has yet returned.
- Lucius has disclosed that during Aemilia's post-loss withdrawal Eros privately sought renewed sexual access in a manner recalling their former sexual relationship. Lucius understood and was tempted but refused and remained faithful. Aemilia challenged Eros's continuing personal access; Lucius accepted that Eros will no longer have discretionary direct access to him or be received alone. Necessary child or household matters must pass through ordinary channels or occur in the presence of others. The restriction is an explicit consequence and has removed Eros's former privileged route to Lucius.
- Aemilia told Lucius, **“I don’t want a child because I’m expected to. I want a child because I want you to look at me the way you do her, to defend me the way you do her.”** Lucius acknowledged that his conduct made Eros's place something he explained and protected while Aemilia had to argue for her own security.
- Aemilia told Lucius, **“We may be married 6 years, but you have only had a wife for 5 months.”** Lucius accepted the distinction and admitted habits formed while he was husband to a child had not prepared him adequately for adult marriage.
- Aemilia told Lucius of the breach and loss, **“It cost me my child.”** Lucius did not accept or deny an unestablished medical causal claim. He acknowledged that his actions cost her safety and trust on the night the pregnancy loss began. The sex of the lost child remains unknown.
- Aemilia has openly said she misses Pia, intended to free her, and still needs her. She hopes Pia escaped and is safe rather than injured or dead, but fears both learning Pia chose to leave and failing Pia if she is in danger.
- Recent voluntary closeness between Aemilia and Lucius includes Aemilia calling specifically for him when too weak to climb stairs, allowing him to carry her and resting against his chest, deliberately meeting his gaze after weeks of avoidance, saying **“I feel better,”** and reaching for his hand. These acts preceded but did not themselves constitute reconciliation.
- Lucius arranged the temporary retreat from Rome for himself and Aemilia at a Valerian country villa with only a small domestic retinue. The greater household remains in Rome. The retreat gives Aemilia physical quiet, distance from the rooms associated with the pregnancy loss, and the couple greater privacy.
- Aemilia and Lucius have completed the journey and arrived. The villa is quieter than the urban domus, with open cultivated country around it and far fewer household eyes. Aemilia is fatigued by the journey but medically stable. Exact villa location, duration of stay, and named attendants remain unestablished.
- After settling in, Aemilia goes into the villa gardens and sits alone in the open air. Her dark hair is still dressed by her own hands in the simpler manner she has worn since Pia disappeared: loose through most of its length, with only the front drawn back from her face.
- Lucius finds Aemilia in the garden and sits at a respectful distance. He tells her he chose the villa partly because there are fewer people watching them and less pressure to perform recovery or pretend their marriage is repaired. He says he does not want to treat her recent voluntary closeness as forgiveness.
- Aemilia tells Lucius plainly, **“You finally understood my perspective rather than jumping to defend Eros. You say you are going to block her access to you. I think we can begin to repair.”** This is the first time Aemilia explicitly states that she believes marital repair can begin. She identifies Lucius finally understanding her grievance and restricting Eros's privileged access as conditions making repair possible.
- Lucius confirms that the restriction on Eros is not temporary appeasement: Eros will not regain discretionary private access merely because time passes. He acknowledges repair must include his own conduct, especially his broken promise and physical prevention of Aemilia leaving. He tells Aemilia that in a future argument she may leave and he will not physically stop her; he understands that he must demonstrate this change rather than demand trust in another promise.
- Aemilia asks, **“Why were you so angry?”** Lucius says the anger involved wounded judgment, challenged household authority, and his inability in the moment to accept Aemilia ending the argument on her own terms. He admits that he was accustomed to making people remain, listen and obey; when Aemilia tried to leave, he treated his authority as though it entitled him to her continued presence. He says the second restraint was worse because he already knew what he had promised and chose control again anyway.
- Aemilia answers, **“By your definition my argument was weak. And you stopped me leaving so I would concede. Which I did.”** Lucius does not dispute the result or claim her concession validated his argument. He acknowledges that once he used physical authority to prevent her leaving, her eventual concession ceased to be evidence that he had persuaded her. He says he wanted a voluntary conclusion while making refusal physically unavailable, an impossible contradiction. He recognizes that Aemilia yielded because he had demonstrated that attempting to end the argument would not be respected, and that he then benefited from treating the resulting quiet as though the dispute had been settled. He explicitly rejects using her concession as proof that his position was stronger. He also recognizes a deeper corruption of the intellectual relationship he had cultivated with her: for years he taught her that arguments should stand on reasons, but when her reasoning threatened an arrangement he wanted preserved, he invoked the power he possessed as husband and master instead of accepting that persuasion had failed. This acknowledgement strengthens the opening toward repair but does not erase the coercion or its consequences.

These facts support household inference and gossip that serious trouble involved Eros and that Eros has fallen from favour, but the full marital breach remains confined to Aemilia, Lucius, Cornelia and Terentia in differing detail. Eros knows her own actions and exchanges but not the spouses' private conversations. No household inference establishes that Eros or the marital quarrel medically caused the pregnancy loss.
