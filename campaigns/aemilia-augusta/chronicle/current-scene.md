# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, later the same morning after Aemilia first felt repeated possible fetal movement. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant, at the Valerian domus in Rome.
- The previous day Aemilia received Tullia concerning Publius's debt and independently checked Lucius's household records. Publius originally received 60,000 sesterces for workshop capital; 18,000 is presently due as an overdue scheduled payment plus interest/arrears; earlier payments were made; workshop assets are pledged; Gnaeus Fabius Priscus appears to have been enforcing within ordinary delegated authority.
- While replacing those records, Aemilia deliberately read private tablets belonging to Lucius. They showed that during her later adolescence her father Marcus Aemilius discreetly explored the financial consequences of ending her still-childless marriage and another possible future match, while Lucius secretly monitored those efforts and withheld the matter from Aemilia. No established replacement match resulted.
- Aemilia confessed to Lucius that she read both sets of private reports. Lucius said she crossed a boundary and asked her not to read his private papers again, while also apologizing for withholding her father's inquiries once she was old enough to understand them. He accepted her apology and refused further punishment or humiliation. Lucius has no current evidence that Marcus still seeks dissolution but promised to tell Aemilia if renewed efforts come to his attention.
- At supper Aemilia proposed investigating Publius's other creditors and potentially consolidating claims to preserve the productive workshop and gain control over recovery. Lucius saw possible commercial merit but wanted full accounts first. He decided Priscus was to pause Valerian collection while creditor amounts, securities, receivables, stock and earning capacity were examined. Aemilia would sit with Lucius when the figures arrived before he decided whether to restructure, consolidate, or proceed against the pledged workshop.
- During the evening Aemilia felt repeated unfamiliar painless flutters in her belly and interpreted them as their child. Lucius became openly moved. The following morning their conversation reinforced the transition from Lucius protecting and educating a child-bride to expecting counsel, disagreement, adult responsibility, marital intimacy and investment in the domus from his adult wife.
- Later that morning another creditor, acting independently of Priscus and the Valerian claim, moves against Publius's workshop. Two adult enslaved metalworkers are removed as property against the outside obligation, and one is struck during resistance. This is not ordered by Lucius or Priscus. Both craftsmen remain in Rome in the outside creditor's possession and the injured man is alive. The creditor asserts a secured trade claim and priority over the Valerian security, but that priority remains disputed/unverified.
- Aemilia commits herself to intervention. Lucius warns that recovery may require buying the claim or purchasing legal ownership of the craftsmen, not freeing them, and that control brings responsibility for discipline and future disposition.
- Aemilia learns that the Valerian household itself punishes enslaved people, though she has been shielded from harsher administration. Ordinary discipline is delegated; serious offenses can bring physical punishment, and outlying properties can be harsher or less visible. Lucius explains that household authority includes responsibility for acts carried out under standing expectations even when the master or mistress did not individually command them.
- Aemilia proposes establishing every creditor and amount owed, then inviting the creditor who seized the craftsmen to the Valerian domus to negotiate. Lucius adopts the strategy but insists hospitality must not concede priority or promise payment. Priscus is ordered to compile the creditor schedule and send a carefully worded invitation in Lucius's name. Lucius specifies that Aemilia will be present because the settlement strategy was hers.
- Lucius gives Aemilia a social role in the negotiation: charm and disarm through hospitality, status, tact and observation. She is to make the creditor comfortable enough to reveal what he values without flirting, promising money, or revealing how urgently they want the craftsmen returned.
- Before the meeting Aemilia has Pia dress her deliberately more modestly than her wealth would ordinarily allow. Pia chooses restrained but unmistakably respectable matronly presentation: good cloth without ostentatious display, minimal jewelry, and neat low-tension hair suitable for pregnancy. The presentation cannot disguise elite wealth but avoids advertising abundance.
- In a private conversation while dressing, Aemilia apologizes to Pia for having assumed people in their service were not punished merely because Aemilia never saw it. Pia explains that enslaved people learn what masters wish and do not wish to see, and that Aemilia's personal kindness does not erase her authority. Pia acknowledges having received ordinary disciplinary penalties and having been physically struck when younger. Asked directly about sexual coercion, Pia says no one has forced sexual access on her, but explains that as an enslaved woman she has always lived under that vulnerability and that Aemilia's personal protection has mattered in practice without changing Pia's legal condition.
- Aemilia reacts with unprecedented anger and declares that anyone who sexually forced Pia would know 'the wrath of a domina.' She orders Pia to arrange a meeting with the household steward tomorrow to discuss how people in the household are treated. Pia advises Aemilia to ask questions before announcing reforms, because a steward who knows the desired answer may claim the household already conforms to it. Aemilia thanks Pia and acknowledges she has much to learn. The steward's identity and personal history remain unestablished.
- Aemilia leaves for the creditor meeting and finds Lucius in the room prepared for receiving the visitor. Lucius notices her deliberately restrained presentation and approves the tactic while noting that no one inside a Valerian domus will mistake them for poor.
- Before the creditor enters, Aemilia deliberately positions herself as a conventionally deferential Roman wife: seated slightly subordinate to Lucius, attentive to him, allowing him to open the business and visibly preserving his household authority. This is deliberate use of her Adept Elite Etiquette and Novice Politics rather than passivity. Lucius recognizes the tactic and does not expose how much of the strategy originated with her.
- The outside creditor is Numerius Aufidius Crispus, a freeborn Roman businessman in his later forties from a respectable but non-senatorial family, prosperous through metal supply, contracting and credit. He dresses well without senatorial display. He is commercially minded and status-aware, especially wary of being overridden by a senatorial creditor.
- Lucius opens formally, noting competing Valerian and Aufidian claims and arguing that piecemeal seizure may destroy the productive value from which creditors hope to recover. He does not concede Aufidius's asserted priority.
- Aufidius says Publius owes him for metal supplied on credit and missed agreed payments; after promises and delay, Aufidius seized reachable assets before other creditors could do so. He treats the craftsmen as valuable skilled property. One resisted removal and was struck; Aufidius says the injured man is alive, guarded and has received basic attention because damage reduces his value.
- Aemilia uses hospitality, professional curiosity and pointed questions to expose Aufidius's position. He admits Publius complained about part of a bronze delivery and received a limited adjustment, while the extent/cause of defects remains disputed. Aufidius chose the two craftsmen because they were easier, more certain collateral than receivables or unfinished commissions, even though removing them may reduce workshop productivity.
- Aufidius does not especially want to keep the craftsmen. No sale is yet contracted. If paid or adequately secured promptly, he will release them as part of settlement; otherwise he intends to realize their value, potentially through separate sales. He wants prompt certainty more than immediate coin because the unresolved claim ties up capital in his broader metal and credit business.
- Aemilia intensifies her charm without flirting: warm smile, visible dimples, sympathetic conversation and respect for Aufidius's competence. His guardedness eases without making him foolish. He discloses his preferred structure: a credible person assumes or guarantees a defined amount promptly, allowing him to release the craftsmen and close the Publius exposure. He still refuses to name a discounted figure before accounts/security are on the table.
- Aemilia hands the negotiation back to Lucius with a markedly warmer smile for her husband than the socially calibrated charm she gave Aufidius. Lucius resumes the lead, demands Aufidius's complete account/security papers and the disputed-bronze adjustment, and promises only prompt examination against Publius's records and other claims. No guarantee/payment/priority concession is made.
- Lucius secures a short standstill: Aufidius agrees neither craftsman will be sold before the following day while documents are exchanged and reviewed, provided the Valerian household acts promptly.
- After Aufidius leaves, Aemilia and Lucius share brief affectionate teasing and a kiss. Pia arrives because she heard the creditor had departed, sees the kiss, and begins to withdraw. Aemilia calls her back; Pia explains she thought her mistress might need her. Lucius remains physically affectionate rather than pretending embarrassment before an enslaved attendant.
- Aemilia proposes giving Pia one genuine day of rest every week. Lucius accepts the principle but points out that unless Aemilia explicitly prevents reassignment, the steward may simply use Pia elsewhere. Pia cautiously asks whether Aemilia means an entire day without ordinary duties.
- Aemilia clarifies directly: yes, the whole day. Pia may choose what she does with it, including going into the city or sleeping all day; it is 'your day.' The grant is therefore intended as genuine discretionary time rather than merely relief from attending Aemilia. It remains a privilege granted by her domina, not legal freedom or ownership of her own time in the freeborn sense.
- Pia is visibly moved but also immediately tests the practical boundary: she asks whether she may truly leave the domus on that day without seeking a fresh permission each time, provided she returns when required and causes no household scandal. Lucius interjects that Aemilia can authorize such ordinary outings for her personal attendant, but the household must still know where Pia has gone and Pia remains subject to the household's authority and curfew/security expectations. Aemilia's phrase 'your day' is emotionally meaningful but does not erase Pia's enslaved status.
- Lucius tells Aemilia that if she wants the arrangement to survive beyond a generous impulse, she should have it entered into the steward's standing instructions and designate another attendant to rotate into Pia's place. He does not oppose the weekly rest day.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Pia has been promised a genuine weekly day free from ordinary labor, with discretion to rest or go into the city subject to basic household knowledge/security expectations. She is moved and surprised. The practical arrangement still needs to be given to the steward as a standing instruction and replacement coverage established. The one-day standstill for Publius's two craftsmen remains in force and Priscus must urgently examine the creditor documents.
