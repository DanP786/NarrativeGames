# Current scene

- S01, approximately 12 BC, around five and a half years after the wedding-night opening.
- Aemilia is seventeen, a few months from her eighteenth birthday. Lucius is approximately thirty.
- Their marriage remains privately unconsummated. Publicly Lucius behaves as an established husband and protects the secret without hesitation.
- At an elite private gathering in Rome, an older matron probes Aemilia about the marriage's lack of children. Aemilia answers lightly that Venus will bless them with a child. Lucius deflects further scrutiny with jokes about hoping for a daughter and valuing his wife's opinions.
- Gaius Fabius Crispus later approaches Aemilia near the household shrine while Lucius is occupied. He says rumours are forming around the long-childless marriage and tests whether it conceals a private arrangement.
- Asked whether Lucius has been kind, Aemilia smiles and answers, 'Unimaginably kind.' Crispus accepts the affection as genuine but presses the political/familial point: Lucius' childless household gives rivals material for gossip and deprives the couple of the social/legal advantages attached to producing children. He points out that Aemilia has never even been pregnant.
- Crispus' formulation about exactly 'three children' is treated as social shorthand rather than a literal rule that Lucius cannot gain Senate favour before fathering three children; the real danger in the scene is reputation, dynastic expectation and the privileges Augustan policy attaches to marriage and parenthood.
- Lucius remains across the room, aware Crispus is speaking privately with Aemilia but not yet intervening.
