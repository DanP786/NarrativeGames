- Aemilia conducts a private household-discipline inquiry with senior steward Gaius Philargyrus. She learns physical punishment occurs several times in an ordinary month and that lesser delegated supervisors sometimes strike enslaved subordinates without reporting each incident.
- Aemilia orders a one-month trial in the urban domus under Philargyrus's immediate administration: no punitive beating or striking by anyone. Necessary immediate force to stop assault or protect someone from bodily danger remains permitted, including protection of Aemilia and her pregnancy, but may not become punishment afterward.
- Aemilia orders immediate modest rewards for competent ordinary work rather than vague future promises or rewarding exhaustion. Philargyrus will track offenses, responses and rewards so the trial can be assessed. He will inform Lucius because Lucius retains ultimate household authority; unless countermanded, implementation begins immediately.
- Pia's weekly day free ordinary duties still needs formal standing instruction; Pia remains off duty today.
- Aemilia summons Eros, adult enslaved woman and mother of Fortunata (~4) and Felix (~2) by Lucius. Their first substantive private conversation is cautious because of the stark power imbalance.
- Eros explains servants conceal quarrels, injuries, misconduct and complaints from masters, and that ordering truth does not make candor safe. Aemilia explicitly promises not to punish Eros or use her words against her.
- Asked about Lucius, Eros distinguishes caring for him and gratitude for his acknowledgment/care of their children from free choice. She says there were occasions she did not want Lucius to come to her but did not understand refusal as an available option; at other times she felt affection, favor or security. No physical force/threat/punishment for refusal is established.
- Asked whether she wishes to stay, Eros says she prefers this household to sale/transfer while enslaved because her children and practical security are here, but would choose freedom if she could remain near/protect Fortunata and Felix.
- Aemilia says Fortunata and Felix are dear to her, that seeing Lucius enjoy them makes her happy, and that neither she nor Lucius wishes to separate Eros from them. No manumission/status-change promise is made.
- Eros discloses that a delegated male supervisor in the urban service/cellar side abuses his authority through punitive/humiliating striking and warns that a no-beating rule could redirect retaliation into worse duties, food access, sleep disruption, accusations or other privileges.
- Aemilia demands: 'Name, and duty.' Eros identifies the man as **Menophilus**, an enslaved senior cellar/store servant who has delegated authority over several workers handling wine, oil, lamps, serving stores and related supplies. He answers upward through the household administrative chain to Philargyrus.
- Eros says Menophilus's useful competence is part of why his conduct has remained tolerated or unseen: stores are generally accounted for, shortages are noticed, and work above stairs gets done. His violence and intimidation occur mainly among people subordinate to him and away from elite household eyes.
- Menophilus's exact age, origin, appearance, years in the household and full history remain unestablished. Eros has not alleged sexual abuse, theft, falsified accounts or direct orders from Philargyrus/Lucius. The specific accusation is abuse of delegated authority through arbitrary striking/intimidation and likely capacity for less visible retaliation.
