# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, the following morning. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant, at the Valerian domus in Rome.
- Publius originally received 60,000 sesterces for workshop capital; 18,000 is presently due to the Valerian household as an overdue scheduled payment plus interest/arrears; earlier payments were made; workshop assets are pledged. Aemilia proposed investigating all creditors and potentially consolidating claims to preserve the productive workshop.
- Another creditor, Numerius Aufidius Crispus, seized two adult enslaved metalworkers from Publius's workshop. One was struck while resisting removal but remains alive. Aufidius claims secured priority over Valerian interests, still disputed/unverified. He does not particularly want the craftsmen and prefers prompt certainty on his debt.
- During negotiation at the Valerian domus, Aemilia used hospitality and calibrated charm to learn Aufidius's interests. Lucius then secured a standstill: neither craftsman would be sold before the following day while documents were exchanged and reviewed, provided the Valerians acted promptly. That day has now arrived.
- Aemilia has learned the Valerian household itself punishes enslaved people and that she had been shielded from harsher administration. Pia disclosed that she was physically struck when younger and has lived with the structural sexual vulnerability of enslavement, though no one has sexually forced Pia. Aemilia ordered a meeting with the household steward for today to investigate treatment within the household.
- Aemilia granted Pia one genuine weekly day free from ordinary labor and begins it immediately today rather than making her wait a week. Another enslaved household woman dresses Aemilia and arranges her hair. Aemilia wears her usual clothing, but her hair is visibly different because the substitute lacks Pia's years of familiarity with her preferences and pregnancy comfort. Lucius notices and mildly teases her about the visible consequence. Pia is genuinely off ordinary duties today.
- Aemilia sits beside Lucius as instructed and begins reading Priscus's compiled material herself. Her Adept Literacy lets her work confidently through the documents, while Novice Accounts & Estate Business means she can follow the main structure and spot obvious pressure points but does not instantly resolve every disputed legal/accounting question.
- Priscus's preliminary creditor schedule establishes three material outside pressures beyond the Valerian claim. Numerius Aufidius Crispus claims 14,400 sesterces gross on metal supplied and arrears; his own records acknowledge a 1,600-sesterce adjustment connected with the disputed bronze, leaving 12,800 he presently demands. A second trade creditor is owed 6,200 sesterces for charcoal, transport and workshop supplies. A smaller private obligation of 3,600 sesterces is also overdue. Together with the 18,000 presently due to the Valerian household, immediately pressing obligations total about 40,600 sesterces before any further costs or disputed adjustments.
- Publius's position is not empty. The papers show approximately 21,000 sesterces in customer receivables, but Priscus marks only about 12,000 as likely collectible promptly; several customers dispute workmanship or delivery timing. Finished and partly finished stock has value but would realize substantially less in a forced sale than through completion. The workshop's tools, furnaces and remaining material are productive assets rather than easy cash.
- The most important commercial finding is uncomfortable rather than catastrophic: the workshop appears capable of earning its way out over time if kept operating, but it cannot satisfy all pressing creditors now. Piecemeal seizure would probably destroy much of the value needed for repayment. Consolidation or a controlled standstill therefore has economic logic, but requires the Valerian household to put its own credit and authority behind Publius and accept the risk that future earnings disappoint.
- Aufidius's asserted priority is not cleanly proven by the papers delivered so far. His documents support a real debt and some security interest connected to workshop property, but Priscus notes ambiguity over whether his claim reaches the two craftsmen ahead of the earlier Valerian pledge. Resolving that point conclusively could take longer than today's standstill. Lucius therefore cannot rely on a quick legal victory to recover the men before Aufidius is free to sell.
- The bronze dispute is also real but limited. Aufidius's own adjustment corroborates that some supplied material was challenged; nothing in the current papers proves that defective bronze caused Publius's broader losses. Publius cannot plausibly erase the entire Aufidian debt on that basis.
- Lucius allows Aemilia to reach the structure herself before summarizing: the workshop is not obviously fraudulent or worthless, but saving it means choosing to carry more risk rather than discovering a painless accounting trick. If they want the craftsmen returned today, they need either a negotiated security/guarantee with Aufidius or a payment arrangement, while separately trying to hold the other creditors and restructure Publius's obligations.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia is seated with Lucius over the open tablets. She now has concrete preliminary figures: Aufidius demands 12,800 after a documented 1,600 bronze adjustment; other pressing outside debts are 6,200 and 3,600; Valerian amount presently due is 18,000; total pressing obligations about 40,600. Customer receivables total about 21,000 but only ~12,000 look promptly collectible. Workshop productive value is better preserved by continued operation than forced sale. Aufidius's priority over the two craftsmen remains legally ambiguous and cannot safely be resolved before today's deadline. A decision or further negotiation is required today. The steward meeting remains due later, and Pia is off duty.
