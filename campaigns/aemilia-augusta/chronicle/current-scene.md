# Current scene

- S01, wedding night, Valerian domus in Rome.
- Aemilia, age twelve, and Lucius Valerius Messalla, age twenty-four, are alone in the bedchamber after the ceremonies.
- Lucius immediately removes ambiguity about the wedding night: he tells Aemilia he will not consummate the marriage while she is a child and that she will sleep undisturbed.
- He tells her that obedience alone is not what he wants from her. Beginning tomorrow, he intends to expand her education beyond what her father permitted: reading, Greek, history, law, accounts and eventually the political business of the household.
- Lucius makes the hierarchy explicit rather than presenting modern equality: the domus remains under his authority and final decisions are his, but he wants Aemilia eventually capable of understanding his reasons, challenging him privately when she believes him wrong, and offering useful judgment.
- Aemilia, confused, answers with the duty she has been taught: that she is to give him children. Lucius agrees children will eventually be expected, but only when she is no longer a child herself.
- When Aemilia points out the wedding zona, Lucius refuses the bridegroom's customary untying and says one of her women can help her instead.
- Aemilia then reveals that she has already absorbed fragments of Augustus' marriage policy, anxiously mentioning penalties for childlessness, independence and inheritance. Lucius distinguishes marriage law, inheritance restrictions and the privileges attached to motherhood, telling her that adults have compressed complicated law into frightening rules for her. He insists there is no immediate legal necessity for her to become pregnant at twelve and uses her questions as the first evidence that her education should include actual law rather than household warnings.
