# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, several weeks after S01. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant. The pregnancy is more visibly apparent but no complication is established.
- Morning at Lucius and Aemilia's Valerian domus in Rome. Lucius is away from the domus. The discreet Rufus-Valeria gathering is now several weeks past. Terentia remains cautiously favorable; Valeria and Rufus remain unaware of the matchmaking purpose; Marcus Valerius Messalla has not been told. Rufus's Atalanta-poem search remains unresolved.
- Aemilia has just received Tullia in the tablinum regarding Publius, a freedman of Tullia's husband who owes money to Lucius's household. Aemilia promised only to bring the matter to Lucius and then questioned Tullia carefully about the debt.
- Tullia's account: 18,000 sesterces is presently demanded on a larger obligation advanced for Publius's metalworking workshop; equipment and stock are pledged; delayed customer payments and allegedly poor-quality purchased metal have squeezed his cash; he also owes ordinary trade creditors, with at least one supplier overdue. Tullia can vouch for the workshop and Publius's past reliability but not his books. Valerian agent Gnaeus Fabius Priscus has threatened seizure of pledged assets.
- After Tullia departs, Aemilia goes into Lucius's office to find the relevant household accounts rather than waiting passively for his return. Her Adept Literacy and Novice Accounts & Estate Business are sufficient to locate and follow the entry without difficulty; she is not acting as a professional jurist or independently rewriting the obligation.
- The Valerian record identifies Publius's obligation as an original advance of 60,000 sesterces for workshop capital. The current 18,000-sesterce demand is an overdue scheduled payment plus accrued interest/arrears rather than the entire debt. After that payment, a substantial balance would still remain.
- The record confirms that specified workshop equipment and a portion of stock stand as security. It also shows that Publius has paid earlier scheduled amounts rather than having defaulted from the outset. The present default is therefore a deterioration in a previously performing obligation.
- Priscus's notation records that payment was demanded, an extension was requested, and enforcement against pledged property was authorized under the standing terms if payment was not forthcoming. The record does not show that Lucius personally reviewed Publius's recent request for additional time; Priscus appears to be acting within ordinary delegated authority rather than under a fresh case-specific command from Lucius.
- The household books cannot verify Publius's claims about bad metal, delayed customers, or his other creditors. Those require evidence from Publius's side. Nor do the entries by themselves establish whether immediate seizure is commercially wiser than restructuring the payment.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia is in Lucius's office with the relevant account before her. She now knows Tullia was substantially accurate about the structure of the debt, that Publius previously made payments, and that Priscus appears to be enforcing the security under delegated authority without a fresh personal decision from Lucius. Lucius remains away.
