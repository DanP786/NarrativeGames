# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, the following morning. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant, at the Valerian domus in Rome.
- Publius originally received 60,000 sesterces for workshop capital; 18,000 is presently due to the Valerian household as an overdue scheduled payment plus interest/arrears; earlier payments were made; workshop assets are pledged. Aemilia proposed investigating all creditors and potentially consolidating claims to preserve the productive workshop.
- Another creditor, Numerius Aufidius Crispus, seized two adult enslaved metalworkers from Publius's workshop. One was struck while resisting removal but remains alive. Aufidius claims secured priority over Valerian interests, still disputed/unverified. He does not particularly want the craftsmen and prefers prompt certainty on his debt.
- During negotiation at the Valerian domus, Aemilia used hospitality and calibrated charm to learn Aufidius's interests. Lucius then secured a standstill: neither craftsman would be sold before the following day while documents were exchanged and reviewed, provided the Valerians acted promptly. That day has now arrived.
- Aemilia has learned the Valerian household itself punishes enslaved people and that she had been shielded from harsher administration. Pia disclosed that she was physically struck when younger and has lived with the structural sexual vulnerability of enslavement, though no one has sexually forced Pia. Aemilia ordered a meeting with the household steward for today to investigate treatment within the household.
- Aemilia granted Pia one genuine weekly day free from ordinary labor and begins it immediately today rather than making her wait a week. Another enslaved household woman dresses Aemilia and arranges her hair. Aemilia wears her usual clothing, but her hair is visibly different because the substitute lacks Pia's years of familiarity with her preferences and pregnancy comfort. Lucius notices and mildly teases her about the visible consequence. Pia is genuinely off ordinary duties today.
- Aemilia sits beside Lucius and reads Priscus's compiled material herself. Her Adept Literacy lets her work confidently through the documents, while Novice Accounts & Estate Business lets her follow the main structure and spot pressure points without resolving every accounting/legal ambiguity.
- Priscus's preliminary creditor schedule establishes three material outside pressures beyond the Valerian claim. Numerius Aufidius Crispus claims 14,400 sesterces gross on metal supplied and arrears; his own records acknowledge a 1,600-sesterce adjustment connected with the disputed bronze, leaving 12,800 he presently demands. A second trade creditor is owed 6,200 sesterces for charcoal, transport and workshop supplies. A smaller private obligation of 3,600 sesterces is also overdue. Together with the 18,000 presently due to the Valerian household, immediately pressing obligations total about 40,600 sesterces before further costs/disputed adjustments.
- Publius has approximately 21,000 sesterces in customer receivables, but Priscus marks only about 12,000 as likely collectible promptly; several customers dispute workmanship or delivery timing. Finished/partly finished stock has value but would realize substantially less in forced sale than through completion. Tools, furnaces and remaining material are productive assets rather than easy cash.
- Workshop appears capable of earning its way out over time if kept operating, but cannot satisfy all pressing creditors now. Piecemeal seizure would probably destroy much of the value needed for repayment. Consolidation/controlled standstill has economic logic but requires Valerian household to put its own credit/authority behind Publius and accept risk future earnings disappoint.
- Aufidius's asserted priority is not cleanly proven. Documents support real debt and some security interest, but ambiguity remains whether his claim reaches the two craftsmen ahead of earlier Valerian pledge. Conclusive resolution could take longer than today's standstill. Bronze dispute is real but limited; current papers do not prove defective bronze caused broader losses.
- The roughly 9,000-sesterce gap between total customer receivables and the 12,000 judged promptly collectible is doubtful/delayed rather than established loss. Some may arrive later, be reduced through disputes, or never be recovered.
- Aemilia proposes that the Valerian household satisfy or secure the outside creditors, keep the workshop operating, consolidate the debt under Valerian control and charge Publius more interest. Lucius accepts this as coherent in principle but requires stronger controls: no new borrowing without approval, direct account visibility, priority application of collected customer debts, and adequate security.
- Aemilia then identifies the ugly downside: if the workshop is liquidated and its assets/receivables do not cover the enlarged Valerian exposure, some further arrangement would be needed; she raises 'servitude even' while thinking through what security might remain.
- Lucius immediately distinguishes commercial control from simply enslaving a debtor. Publius is a freedman and his personal legal status cannot casually be converted into slavery merely because his business fails. Lucius warns Aemilia not to treat a free man's body as an ordinary extension of pledged workshop property. The Valerians can pursue pledged assets, guarantees, enforceable obligations and control arrangements within the law/custom available to them, but the exact remedy must not be invented from desperation.
- Lucius nevertheless agrees with the core risk Aemilia sees: if they pay the outside 22,600 sesterces and Publius later fails, the household may recover substantially less than the enlarged claim. That is why the decision depends on whether the business is viable and whether Publius's failure arose mainly from a temporary cash-flow shock or from persistent bad management/concealment.
- Aemilia turns back to the tablets to answer Lucius's question about why Publius is in this position. The existing documents provide clues but not yet a single conclusive cause: delayed/disputed customer payments, a genuine but limited bronze-quality dispute, and overlapping short-term trade obligations all contributed. The pattern also suggests Publius continued taking material/credit while collections deteriorated instead of reducing commitments early enough. Whether that was a defensible attempt to keep commissions moving or reckless overextension remains the key judgment to be made.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia is studying the accounts to distinguish temporary misfortune from bad management. Concrete figures remain: Aufidius 12,800; other outside debts 6,200 and 3,600; Valerian amount presently due 18,000; pressing total ~40,600. Receivables 21,000 total, ~12,000 promptly collectible. Rescue would require roughly 22,600 of additional Valerian exposure to clear outside pressing debts before considering costs/adjustments. Liquidation may not cover enlarged exposure. Publius's free status is not casually convertible to slavery for debt. Aufidius's standstill expires today. Steward meeting remains due later; Pia is off duty.
