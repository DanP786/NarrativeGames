# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC. The next day has begun in Rome after Aemilia and Lucius's private conversation in the report room. The pregnancy loss is more than two weeks in the past, exact day count loose. Aemilia is eighteen.
- The severe marital breach remains canon: Lucius twice physically stopped Aemilia leaving an argument and the second restraint broke his explicit promise not to do so again. He later admitted **“I was wrong.”** The breach is actively being repaired but is not erased. Their voluntary adult intimacy and affectionate closeness resumed at the country villa and continued through the return to Rome.
- Aemilia remains in physical recovery from severe blood loss and prolonged weakness. The villa improved her rest and endurance, but she is not fully restored. No fever, renewed hemorrhage or new medical complication is established.
- Pia remains missing. Evidence shows a woman strongly matching Pia was forcibly controlled, taken to a holding yard, transferred through Titus Fabius Naso, and then into the commercial network of **Quintus Numisius Faustus**, a Campanian dealer operating through Capua. Exact present location remains unknown. Aemilia's standing order remains immediate but **safe** recovery, with Lucius authorizing payment if fastest.
- OOC GM canon remains: Pia did not flee; Eros deliberately facilitated the initial interception to isolate Aemilia but did not intend/order onward sale and did not design the full chain. Pregnancy loss remains medically unresolved and unrelated to Eros.
- Naso is held in a functional/service area of the Valerian domus and is visibly bruised/beaten; responsibility for the beating remains unestablished.
- Valerian men are pursuing Faustus's Roman contacts and southward route. Recovery remains first priority.
- Naso has identified **Gaius Vettius Philargyrus** as the freedman whose prior word vouched for the two men who delivered Pia into the commercial chain. Philargyrus's own source/knowledge remains unknown; no evidence yet connects him to Eros.
- Naso has admitted Philargyrus told him in advance that the target was a household attendant who would be away from her mistress on legitimate leave and that the interceptors would know where to meet her.
- Aemilia immediately identifies the contradiction in Naso's claimed innocence: **“So you admit you knew Pia would be away from her mistress, not that her mistress allowed her sale.”**
- Naso cannot honestly claim he was told the mistress authorized the sale. He admits the message did **not** say the mistress had consented or ordered the disposal. It only asserted that the two men possessed authority and that Philargyrus vouched for them.
- Naso attempts to distinguish his inference from actual knowledge: he says he assumed the authority came from someone entitled to dispose of the woman, but he never established who that person was. He therefore knew she was attached to a mistress/household and legitimately away from that mistress, while possessing no direct confirmation from that mistress or a named owner that she was to be sold.
- Lucius recognizes Aemilia's point as materially damaging to Naso's innocent-broker defense. He does not falsely declare the legal question already adjudicated, but states that Naso's account is no longer simply that he unknowingly received an apparently ordinary sale: he knowingly accepted a household attendant from strangers while aware she was merely away from her mistress and without verifying the mistress/owner's authorization.
- This does not establish that Naso knew the mistress was Aemilia, knew the household was Valerian, or knew Pia was being abducted. It does establish that he consciously accepted a significant gap in authority/title because he trusted Philargyrus's assurance.
- Naso's incentive to cooperate against Philargyrus increases substantially. Lucius makes no promise of immunity.
- No evidence known to Aemilia or Lucius yet connects Eros to Naso, Faustus, or Philargyrus. Philargyrus remains the next backward witness needed to identify the source of the advance information.
- Eros remains in Rome with former privileged access ended. Aemilia's one-month trial prohibiting punitive beatings continues; full-month assessment not yet formally presented.
- Exact sex/appearance and handling of the lost child remain unestablished. Publius/Aufidius creditor deadline outcome remains unestablished.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia has pinned Naso to the crucial distinction: he knew the woman was a household attendant temporarily away from her mistress, but had no statement or mandate showing that mistress or any named owner authorized her sale. Naso can only answer that he relied on Philargyrus's assurance that the delivery men had authority.
