Lucius ended the argument and wanted her to rest; Aemilia insisted **“I'm fine”** and moved slowly to bed. No bleeding had yet been established then.

This is an alarming symptom at approximately four months and is treated as an emergency without assuming miscarriage or fetal death.

- Demetrios now arrives at the bedchamber. Aemilia lies nearly motionless in the bed, crying and clutching her abdomen. Her immobility is distress/guarding, not established unconsciousness or paralysis; she remains awake.
- Demetrios immediately treats the situation as serious. He orders adequate light, clean water and clean cloths, limits unnecessary people around the bed, and first assesses Aemilia's responsiveness/general condition and asks simple questions about pain and bleeding. He does not make a confident diagnosis from the doorway and does not promise the pregnancy can be saved.

His methods remain ancient and limited: observation, touch, symptom history and the midwife's practical obstetric experience; no modern fetal monitoring or diagnostic certainty.
- Lucius remains close but yields practical control of the medical crisis to Demetrios. He is visibly frightened and obeys requests for space/light/linens rather than interfering. He does not blame Aemilia, Eros, himself or the earlier argument as the medical cause; causation remains unknown.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Deep night. Demetrios has reached Aemilia, who lies awake, crying, nearly motionless and clutching her abdomen while bleeding. He is beginning an urgent assessment; the trusted midwife is expected/arriving separately. Pregnancy outcome and Aemilia's condition remain unresolved.
