# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, morning. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant, at the Valerian domus in Rome.
- Publius workshop crisis active. Figures: Aufidius 12,800; other pressing outside debts 6,200 and 3,600; Valerian due 18,000; pressing total ~40,600. Receivables ~21,000/~12,000 promptly collectible. Forced liquidation likely destroys value/may not cover Valerian claim. Lucius leans controlled rescue but has not executed it; Aufidius standstill on two seized enslaved craftsmen expires today.
- Pia's first genuine weekly day free ordinary labor is today. Another enslaved household woman dressed Aemilia/did hair; usual clothing, visibly different hair. Pia genuinely off ordinary duties. Steward meeting remains due later.
- Aemilia's father Marcus Aemilius Lepidus, 49, senator/former praetor, arrived uninvited/without prior message. Conservative regarding daughters' education/female public visibility. Previously pressured Aemilia to end marriage and privately explored dowry/remarriage contingencies during long childless period. Private pregnancy message was sent around three months.
- Marcus intentionally came before Cornelia/family for private conversation. Cornelia is well and wanted to visit. Marcus acknowledges long childless years led him to consider ending/replacing marriage; pregnancy removes central premise. He explicitly reverses course: marriage should continue; he will not pursue/encourage dissolution or another match on old childlessness grounds.
- Marcus acknowledges Lucius could have ended marriage but did not, and Aemilia repeatedly said she wanted to remain. Marcus admits he discounted her judgment due youth/attachment and says her judgment was better than his. Aemilia tells him: 'I am happy here, Father.' Marcus accepts this as real, says he should have given her happiness/judgment more weight, and says he has no wish to take this life from her while Lucius treats her as a wife whose place he means to preserve.
- Lucius then arrives from the urgent business matter. He enters in time to catch the end of Marcus's statement about having no wish to 'take this life' from Aemilia. He greets Marcus with proper senatorial/familial civility but immediately corrects the implied authority.
- Lucius tells Marcus, in controlled rather than shouted terms, that Aemilia's place in the Valerian household is not something Marcus can now simply take from her. Lucius states that Aemilia is his wife, the marriage continues with his intention and hers, and he will not consent to her being removed or traded into another arrangement because her natal family changes its calculations. He frames final household/marital authority as his, while also explicitly noting Aemilia herself has chosen to remain.
- Lucius does not claim Roman marriage is legally indissoluble or that Marcus has no possible influence. His assertion is personal, political and household: as husband and head of this domus, he would resist any attempt by Marcus to break the marriage; Aemilia's expressed wish aligns with his decision.
- Marcus does not immediately escalate. He registers Lucius's correction as deliberate and status-conscious. Since Marcus has already reversed his position, the dispute is now partly over who possessed the right to decide, not whether Aemilia should presently leave.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia and Marcus are seated; Lucius has just entered and asserted that Aemilia's continued place as his wife is not Marcus's to grant or withdraw. Marcus has already said the marriage should continue, so tension now centers on paternal versus marital authority and the history of Marcus's attempted interference. Publius deadline active; steward meeting later; Pia off duty.
