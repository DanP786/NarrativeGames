# Current scene

- S01, approximately 12 BC, around five and a half years after the wedding-night opening. Aemilia is seventeen, a few months from eighteen; Lucius is approximately thirty.
- Late at night in Lucius' private study, Aemilia finally releases part of the speech she had been suppressing. She tells Lucius that he has fathered seven babies during their marriage, of whom three survive, while she has spent those years learning Greek. She has witnessed the household births and personally comforted the enslaved mothers when four of those children died. She names motherhood, birth, grief and family-making as a part of life from which Lucius' decision excluded her even while it protected and educated her.
- Canon established: across the roughly five and a half years of the marriage, Lucius has fathered seven children by adult enslaved women of his household; three are currently alive and four died in infancy or early childhood. Valeria is one of the three survivors. The identities of the other mothers and surviving children remain to be established in play rather than invented retroactively.
- Lucius does not answer Aemilia's grief by pretending his choice cost her nothing. He explains that his refusal to expose her to pregnancy when she was very young was driven substantially by the dangers of pregnancy and childbirth, which are severe in their world and especially troubling to him for a girl whose body was still developing. He notes that Roman society commonly accepts early marriage, pregnancy and maternal death as ordinary costs of family continuity rather than treating the girl's safety as decisive.
- Lucius avoids invented numerical mortality rates or false medical precision. His explanation is grounded in the repeated deaths and dangerous births visible in Roman household life: hemorrhage, obstructed labor, fever and infection can kill mothers, and infants die frequently even when the mother survives.
- He maintains that he would make the original decision again, but acknowledges for the first time in explicit terms that protecting Aemilia from one set of dangers also excluded her from experiences she wanted and forced her to witness him have children elsewhere. He does not characterize that pain as childish jealousy.
- Aemilia remains seventeen; the scene contains no sexual contact or erotic conduct.
