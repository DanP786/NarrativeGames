# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, more than a week after Aemilia's pregnancy loss; exact day count remains loose. Aemilia is eighteen. She and Lucius have now arrived at a Valerian country villa outside Rome for a temporary retreat with a small domestic retinue.
- Earlier events remain active canon: Eros's disclosure caused a severe marital argument; Lucius twice physically stopped Aemilia leaving and the second restraint broke his explicit promise not to stop her again. He later admitted **“I was wrong.”** The marital breach remains unresolved.
- Aemilia lost her approximately four-month pregnancy after hours of pain and bleeding in the shared bedchamber despite attendance by Demetrios and the trusted midwife. No medical cause was established. She then remained unconscious for about a day from severe weakness/blood loss before waking.
- Pia failed to return from her first genuine day of leave and remains absent. Her reason and whereabouts/fate remain unestablished to Aemilia and Lucius.
- After grief-driven withdrawal, Cornelia's visit and a second withdrawal after sending her mother home, Aemilia returned to household duties. She now works in a changed manner: controlled and soft-voiced but without her former warmth, dimpling smiles or instinct to cushion commands. This reserve applies broadly rather than solely to Eros.
- Aemilia remains physically weakened after major blood loss, prolonged inactivity and limited eating. No fever, renewed hemorrhage or new medical complication is established.
- Earlier at the Rome domus, Terentia visited and learned the substance of the unresolved marital breach involving Eros and Lucius, including Lucius physically stopping Aemilia twice and breaking his promise not to stop her again. Terentia did not establish a causal link between the argument/restraint and the later miscarriage, but took the broken promise and physical restraint seriously.
- Terentia advised that Aemilia need not punish Eros or her children, but mercy does not require confidence or warmth; Lucius remains responsible for his own choices. Aemilia has already told Lucius what hurt her and need not repeatedly chase him for acknowledgment. If the marriage is to recover, Lucius must eventually choose to address the breach rather than allow ordinary married life to erase it.
- Valeria Tertia is well, remains unmarried and unaware that her parents are considering Gaius Vibius Rufus as a possible husband. Marcus is investigating Rufus as a dynastic prospect while Terentia scrutinizes his temperament and motives. Terentia is cautiously favorable. No engagement has been made.
- Before leaving Rome, Aemilia ordered the household steward to continue records for her one-month trial prohibiting punitive beatings and assess them after the full month. The records so far establish that serious disciplinary incidents have not materially increased compared with the prior household norm; the period remains too short and disrupted to establish positive improvement. Lesser penalties, reassignment, loss of privileges, closer supervision, restitution where possible, and verbal reprimand have been used instead.
- Aemilia also told the steward that Lucius had decided inquiries should be made regarding Pia and ordered him to see it done, **information only for now**. A discreet inquiry is underway in Rome aimed at establishing sightings, circumstances, injury, detention, or other information, without treating the inquiry itself as an order to seize or punish Pia if found. No result has yet returned.
- Before Aemilia left Rome, the steward cautiously told her that the household was sorry for the loss of her child and that the loss had been felt throughout the domus. Aemilia responded only with a nod. She neither rebuked him nor opened her grief further.
- In the private conversation with Lucius before the retreat, Aemilia challenged Eros's continued access to him. Lucius accepted a new practical boundary: Eros will no longer have discretionary direct access to him or be received alone; necessary household or child-related matters must pass through ordinary household channels or be raised in the presence of others. He acknowledged that he had allowed Eros an informality and personal route to him that he did not grant Aemilia and that this helped create Eros's significance within the marriage.
- Lucius has disclosed to Aemilia that during her post-loss withdrawal Eros privately sought renewed sexual access in a manner recalling their former sexual relationship. Lucius understood and was tempted but refused and remained faithful to Aemilia. He acknowledged that merely refusing Eros did not make the approach harmless and that he had initially imposed no proportionate consequence. The restriction on Eros's access is now an explicit consequence.
- Aemilia told Lucius, **“I don’t want a child because I’m expected to. I want a child because I want you to look at me the way you do her, to defend me the way you do her.”** Lucius acknowledged that his conduct had made Eros's place something he explained and protected while Aemilia had to argue for her own security, and that no child should have been necessary to make him defend his wife.
- Aemilia told Lucius, **“We may be married 6 years, but you have only had a wife for 5 months.”** Lucius accepted the distinction and admitted that habits formed while he was husband to a child had not prepared him adequately to live with Aemilia as a grown wife with claims, desires, anger, judgment and expectations.
- Aemilia told Lucius of the breach and loss, **“It cost me my child.”** Lucius did not accept or deny an unestablished medical causal claim. He acknowledged that his actions cost her safety and trust on the night the pregnancy loss began, leaving the breach and loss inseparable in Aemilia's memory even though medical causation remains unknown.
- The sex of the lost child remains unknown. Lucius once inadvertently used a masculine pronoun; when Aemilia tearfully asked whether it was a boy, he immediately corrected the slip and said he did not know.
- Aemilia has openly said she misses Pia, intended to free her, and still needs her. She hopes Pia escaped and is safe rather than injured or dead, but fears both learning Pia chose to leave her and failing Pia if she is in danger. Lucius proposed searching for information rather than automatically pursuing recapture; the current inquiry follows that distinction.
- During their recent private conversation Aemilia deliberately met Lucius's gaze for the first time since the severe argument, later said **“I feel better,”** and reached for his hand. Earlier that day, after becoming too weak to climb stairs, she called specifically for Lucius, permitted him to carry her, and voluntarily rested her head against his chest. These acts of voluntary closeness do not resolve the marital breach.
- Lucius arranged the temporary retreat from Rome for himself and Aemilia at a Valerian country villa, taking only a small domestic retinue rather than transferring the urban household. The purpose is practical as well as private: Aemilia can recover away from the crowded domus and the rooms associated with the pregnancy loss, while the reduced household gives husband and wife greater privacy. Lucius does not treat the journey as proof of reconciliation.

The villa is quieter than the urban domus, with open country immediately beyond the residence and far fewer household eyes around the couple. Aemilia is fatigued by the journey but no new medical complication is established. Exact villa location, duration of stay, and named attendants remain unestablished.
