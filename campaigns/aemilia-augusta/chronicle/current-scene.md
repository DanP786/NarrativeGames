# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, morning. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant, at the Valerian domus in Rome.
- Publius workshop crisis active. Figures: Aufidius 12,800; other pressing outside debts 6,200 and 3,600; Valerian due 18,000; pressing total ~40,600. Receivables ~21,000/~12,000 promptly collectible. Forced liquidation likely destroys value/may not cover Valerian claim. Lucius leans controlled rescue but has not executed it; Aufidius standstill on two seized enslaved craftsmen expires today.
- Pia's first genuine weekly day free ordinary labor is today. Another enslaved household woman dressed Aemilia/did hair; usual clothing, visibly different hair. Pia genuinely off ordinary duties.
- Marcus Aemilius Lepidus's unannounced visit has concluded. He withdrew prior pressure to end Aemilia's marriage, accepted her happiness and judgment, and agreed the marriage should continue. Aemilia and Marcus partially reconciled. Cornelia wants to be present during Aemilia's confinement/birth; Aemilia wants her there. Marcus has been told about the trusted unnamed midwife and Demetrios.
- After Marcus leaves, Aemilia and Lucius share an affectionate, impulsive adult marital interlude, non-explicitly narrated. Lucius then returns toward the urgent Publius/Aufidius matter before today's standstill expires.
- Aemilia turns to the promised household inquiry and summons senior steward Gaius Philargyrus for a private working conversation about household treatment and discipline. His legal status, age, origin and detailed personal history remain unestablished.
- Aemilia invites Philargyrus to sit and tells him she values every person who works for the Valerians. Philargyrus interprets this through Roman managerial assumptions: preserving useful, loyal people is compatible in his mind with hierarchy and discipline.
- Philargyrus distinguishes ordinary errors from deliberate offenses. Minor mistakes occur continually and are usually handled through correction, reprimand, supervision or reassignment rather than severe punishment.
- Deliberate offenses include theft, falsifying stores/accounts, repeated direct disobedience, deliberate damage, serious fighting, unauthorized absence, attempted flight/aiding flight, and deception concealing serious loss/offense. Intent and repetition matter.
- Aemilia asks how often Philargyrus has to correct such deliberate offenses. He does not invent a precise ledger total. He estimates that some disciplinary matter requiring his direct intervention reaches him every few days across the whole urban household, but most are minor or disputed and end with reprimand, restitution, changed duties, closer supervision or loss of discretionary privileges.
- Cases he considers serious enough for physical punishment occur less often: roughly several in an ordinary month across a large household, with variation. This is Philargyrus's practical estimate, not a formal statistic and not a claim about every Valerian property. Outlying estates/worksites may operate differently and are not covered by his immediate urban-house estimate.
- Philargyrus makes clear that not every correction reaches him personally: cooks, doorkeepers, workshop supervisors, senior attendants and other delegated superiors handle lesser failures within their remit. This means Aemilia and Lucius do not automatically know every disciplinary act in their own house.
- Aemilia's intended subjects remain: who may order punishment; what physical punishments are actually used; how offenses/punishments are recorded or reported; whether abuse can occur without Lucius or Aemilia knowing; and formalizing Pia's weekly day free ordinary duties. Pia remains off duty and is not summoned.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia and Philargyrus remain seated. She has learned that disciplinary intervention is routine and that some correction happens below the steward's direct notice, while physical punishment is less common but still an ordinary recurring feature rather than an extraordinary event. Lucius is occupied with Publius/Aufidius.
