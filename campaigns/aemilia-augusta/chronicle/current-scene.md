# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC. Two further days have passed in Rome since Aemilia's conversation with Lucius in their bedchamber. The pregnancy loss is now more than two weeks in the past, exact day count loose. Aemilia is eighteen.
- The severe marital breach remains canon: Lucius twice physically stopped Aemilia leaving an argument and the second restraint broke his explicit promise not to do so again. He later admitted **“I was wrong.”** The breach is actively being repaired but is not erased. Their voluntary adult intimacy and affectionate closeness resumed at the country villa and continued through the return to Rome.
- Aemilia remains in physical recovery from severe blood loss and prolonged weakness. The villa improved her rest and endurance, but she is not fully restored. No fever, renewed hemorrhage or new medical complication is established.
- Pia remains missing, but the search has now produced a materially stronger trail. The original witness recalled a woman broadly matching Pia with unfamiliar men and not appearing to move freely. Over the following two days, Valerian searchers retraced the route and found corroborating testimony: a carter remembers two men controlling an adult woman matching Pia's general description and taking her toward a small holding yard used for enslaved people and other laborers in transit. The identification is still not absolute, but the independent details make the lead substantially stronger.
- Inquiry at the holding yard establishes that a woman matching Pia was brought there by men who represented that they had lawful authority over her. She remained there only briefly before being transferred onward through a dealer/intermediary. The yard's people did not know she belonged to the Valerian household. The transaction was irregular enough that, once Lucius's name and ownership claim were pressed, those involved became markedly more cooperative.
- Aemilia impatiently asks who arranged the transfer. The search report names **Titus Fabius Naso**, a Roman freedman who brokers sales, transport and placements of enslaved laborers for multiple owners and dealers. He is not yet established as the person who ordered Pia's original interception. The holding yard identifies him as the intermediary who arranged the next transfer after Pia reached the yard.
- Naso has business premises/contacts in Rome and has not yet been questioned directly by the Valerian searchers at the moment this report reaches Aemilia and Lucius. Aemilia reacts sharply: **“I will tear through Rome myself if they don’t follow these leads through. Why is he not in custody already?”**
- Lucius explains that the searchers only obtained Naso's name immediately before returning to report and that a freedman broker cannot simply be treated as already convicted on the yard's word. More importantly, Lucius wants Naso found before he is warned or frightened into flight, and wants his records, premises and contacts secured with him. Lucius does not dismiss Aemilia's urgency; he shares it and immediately orders men sent to Naso's known premises and contacts, with instructions to keep him from disappearing and bring him for questioning while preserving whatever business records can identify Pia's onward destination.
- The current trail indicates Pia was taken onward rather than voluntarily disappearing. Her present location is not yet known. No specific abuse beyond coercive seizure, detention and onward sale/transfer is established, and no one in the household yet knows who originally arranged the interception.
- Aemilia's standing order remains immediate but **safe** recovery if Pia is being held against her will. Lucius continues to back it with Valerian money, clients and authority; payment is authorized if it is the fastest safe means to recover Pia, with disputes and punishment to follow later.
