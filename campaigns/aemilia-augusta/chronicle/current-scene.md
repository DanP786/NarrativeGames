- Earlier events remain active canon: Eros's disclosure caused a severe marital argument; Lucius twice physically stopped Aemilia leaving and the second restraint broke his explicit promise not to stop her again. He later admitted **“I was wrong.”** The marital breach remains unresolved.
- Aemilia lost her approximately four-month pregnancy after hours of pain and bleeding in the shared bedchamber despite attendance by Demetrios and the trusted midwife. No medical cause was established. She then remained unconscious for about a day from severe weakness/blood loss before waking.
- Pia failed to return from her first genuine day of leave and remains absent. Her reason and whereabouts/fate remain unestablished. Any search and its results remain unestablished unless played.

- Aemilia remains physically weakened after major blood loss, prolonged inactivity and limited eating. She is now on her second day back at household work. No fever, renewed hemorrhage or new medical complication is established.
- At midday Terentia, Lucius's stepmother and matron of the elder Messalla household, visits. Aemilia receives her in her plain working presentation, with much of her hair loose down her back.
- Aemilia tells Terentia **“I failed,”** explaining **“My duty to your family.”** She voices fear that the miscarriage may revive earlier pressure from both families to end the marriage. Terentia establishes that no one has presently threatened the marriage and Lucius has not said he wishes to put Aemilia aside.
- Aemilia eventually explains the marital breach: Eros repeated a private conversation to Lucius, including her judgment that Aemilia was sheltered/weak and likely to yield if Lucius disagreed; Lucius's response felt to Aemilia like defending Eros against his wife. During the argument Aemilia tried to leave. Lucius physically stopped her once, promised not to stop her again, then later shut the door and forced her shoulders/back against it when she tried to leave again. He recognized the broken promise and said **“I was wrong.”** Pain began during/after the argument; bleeding and miscarriage followed later that night. No medical cause was established, and neither Aemilia nor Terentia can establish that Lucius caused the loss.
- Terentia treats the second restraint seriously because Lucius broke an explicit promise and used force against his visibly pregnant wife, but does not claim the restraint caused the miscarriage.
- Aemilia adds that Lucius told her he does not love Eros but cares about her because Eros is the mother of two of his children. Aemilia's composure breaks on **“the mother of his children,”** because she has just lost her own first child and presently has no surviving child. She then says, **“She has a power over him.”**
- Terentia hears both layers: Aemilia's fear of Eros's influence over Lucius and the fresh grief embedded in the fact that Eros has borne surviving children by him while Aemilia's child died. Terentia does not deny that Eros has a form of influence: Eros has known Lucius intimately for years, is mother to two of his surviving children, and can gain his attention. But Terentia distinguishes influence from authority. Eros remains enslaved and subject to Lucius and Aemilia's household authority; Lucius's decision to listen to or defend her is ultimately Lucius's responsibility, not evidence that Eros controls him.
- Terentia does not know the OOC-established later background approach by Eros to Lucius and therefore cannot reference it. She also does not know whether Lucius's statement that he does not love Eros is emotionally exhaustive; she can only respond to what Aemilia reports.
