- Aemilia turns to the promised household inquiry and summons the senior steward for a private working conversation about household treatment and discipline.
- The steward is now established as **Gaius Philargyrus**, ordinarily addressed by Aemilia as Philargyrus. He is the senior household administrator responsible for ordinary domestic management beneath Lucius's ultimate authority. His legal status, age, origin and detailed personal history remain unestablished unless they emerge in play.
- Aemilia invites Philargyrus to sit, an intentional softening of the usual standing hierarchy, and tells him: 'I value every person who works for us.'
- Philargyrus accepts the seat only after a brief hesitation. He understands that Aemilia's wording encompasses the enslaved household as well as free/freed workers, though Roman household hierarchy remains intact. He does not assume she is proposing equality or manumission.
- He answers cautiously that a household depends upon the people who serve it and that good administration preserves useful, loyal people rather than wasting them. His language is more managerial and Roman than Aemilia's moral framing: he hears her concern but thinks in terms of duty, order, usefulness, discipline and preservation.
- Aemilia's intended subjects remain: who may order punishment of enslaved household members; what physical punishments are actually used; how offenses and punishments are recorded/reported; whether abuse can occur without Lucius or Aemilia knowing; and formalizing Pia's weekly day free of ordinary duties. Pia herself remains off duty and is not summoned.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia and Philargyrus are seated for the household inquiry. She has opened by telling him she values every person who works for the Valerians. He has responded respectfully but through the assumptions of an experienced Roman administrator. He awaits the substance of her concern.
