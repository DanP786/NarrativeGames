# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, several weeks after S01. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant. The pregnancy is more visibly apparent but no complication is established.
- Morning at Lucius and Aemilia's Valerian domus in Rome. Lucius is away from the domus. The discreet Rufus-Valeria gathering is now several weeks past. Terentia remains cautiously favorable; Valeria and Rufus remain unaware of the matchmaking purpose; Marcus Valerius Messalla has not been told. Rufus's Atalanta-poem search remains unresolved.
- Aemilia receives Tullia in the tablinum with Pia attending. Tullia is an adult married Roman woman in her thirties from a respectable senatorial-connected family, known to Aemilia by sight from elite social and religious occasions but not as an intimate. Tullia's own attendant remains near the doorway.
- Tullia explains that one of her husband's freedmen, Publius, has been pressed for payment on an obligation by a Valerian household agent. The agent has threatened to seize pledged workshop equipment and stock if payment is not produced promptly. Publius and his wife say this would prevent the workshop from continuing to earn.
- Aemilia promises review rather than relief, saying the household would not want a man's livelihood stripped without consideration and that she will take the matter to Lucius when he returns.
- Through questioning, Aemilia establishes: Publius says 18,000 sesterces is presently due on a larger Valerian obligation advanced for workshop stock and operating expenses; equipment and stock are pledged; his metalworking workshop has allegedly been squeezed by delayed customer payments and poor-quality purchased metal; the Valerian agent is Gnaeus Fabius Priscus; Publius also owes ordinary trade creditors, with at least one supplier already waiting beyond the customary date. Tullia knows of no other comparable secured loan but cannot guarantee none exists.
- Tullia has consistently distinguished firsthand knowledge from Publius's account. She can vouch for the workshop's existence and his long reliable service to her husband's household, but not for his books or every financial explanation. She openly acknowledges that simply delaying Lucius's collection could disadvantage him if other creditors seize assets first.
- Aemilia closes the audience by nodding and saying, 'Thank you for bringing the matter to me. I will certainly bring it to my husband's attention.' She makes no further promise concerning Publius's debt or Priscus's conduct.
- Tullia accepts the answer without pressing for more. She thanks Aemilia for hearing her and prepares to leave with her attendant. The audience has established Aemilia as a viable point of approach for discreet household intercession while preserving Lucius's final authority over his financial affairs.
- The full original Valerian amount and terms, remaining balance, composition of the 18,000 due, total outside debts, priority/security of other creditors, whether Publius's business account is accurate, and whether Priscus acted within Lucius's standing instructions remain unverified.
- CURRENT MOMENT: The audience with Tullia is ending. Aemilia has committed herself only to bringing the matter to Lucius. The next substantive step is likely checking household records and/or speaking to Lucius when he returns.
