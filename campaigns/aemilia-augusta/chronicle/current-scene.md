# Current scene

- S01, approximately 12 BC. Aemilia is approximately three months pregnant. The pregnancy is progressing without an established complication.
- The birthday gathering at the residence of Marcus Valerius Messalla Minor and Servilia has ended. Later that evening Aemilia and Lucius are privately preparing for bed.
- A private message has been sent to Aemilia's parents, Marcus Aemilius Lepidus and Cornelia, telling them directly of the pregnancy so they will not learn through Valerian gossip.
- Lucius's father, Marcus Valerius Messalla, has learned directly that Aemilia is about three months pregnant. The timing convinces him that Lucius truly did deliberately postpone conception until Aemilia was an adult; Marcus grudgingly acknowledges that Lucius's judgment may have been right.
- At the gathering Terentia drew Aemilia aside. Their exchange was openly affectionate and teasing. Terentia told Aemilia she has always been her favourite, excluding Terentia's own biological children Decimus and Valeria Tertia.
- Terentia and Aemilia discussed potential husbands for Valeria Tertia. Aemilia suggested the unconventional prospect Gaius Vibius Rufus, about twenty-two, from a prosperous non-senatorial equestrian family and useful to Lucius's network. Aemilia describes him as kind, clever and perceptive, and reports that he has never attended a brothel and only occasionally attends public bathhouses. Aemilia further explained that Rufus's ambitions appear directed not toward political office but toward information and that he is not controlling but rarely concedes once persuaded. Terentia takes the proposal seriously despite the pedigree problem.
- Aemilia conspiratorially suggested that Lucius might happen to invite Rufus to their villa on the same day Aemilia happens to invite Terentia, allowing Terentia to observe Rufus without formally declaring him a marriage prospect. Terentia approved the discreet arrangement.
- At the gathering Valeria Messalla enthusiastically said she had prayed for Aemilia to become pregnant. Valeria teasingly remarked that Lucius has always loved women and that their father spent heavily on silphium/laser as a contraceptive in Lucius's youth. Servilia added that it was evidently not used reliably with the enslaved women after Lucius married Aemilia, an allusion to his three surviving enslaved children and four children who died.
- Later that evening, while privately preparing for bed with Lucius, Aemilia asks about his past after Valeria's teasing. Lucius admits that across the years between their marriage and Aemilia's adulthood he exercised sexual access to six adult enslaved women in total, including Eros and Daphne. The relationships existed under slavery and were not freely consenting. Pia was not among them. Some women belonged to their immediate household and others were encountered through wider Valerian properties/establishments. No additional identities are established.
- Aemilia tells Lucius, 'I didn't know your need for a woman's company until recently.' She asks how many women and whether they were from this household, then declines the offered names. She says, 'No, our true marriage didn't start until I was eighteen. I should not act otherwise.' She deliberately separates his conduct during the years when he maintained a childhood boundary from the fidelity she expects in their adult marriage.
- Aemilia asks whether Lucius would keep his fidelity promise even if pregnancy, childbirth, illness, medical caution, or another circumstance prevents marital intimacy for a time. Lucius answers unequivocally yes: the promise was fidelity, not fidelity conditional on her availability. Aemilia says, 'Ok, I will forgive what came before.' Lucius accepts this seriously without treating it as absolution of the coercive realities of his past conduct.
- Aemilia takes Lucius's offered hand and tells him she would like to invite Terentia and Valeria Tertia whenever he happens to invite Gaius Vibius Rufus next. Lucius recognizes the deliberate coincidence and understands that Aemilia is considering Rufus as a possible husband for his thirteen-year-old half-sister. He is surprised by the socially unconventional prospect but does not dismiss it; Rufus is a useful and intelligent equestrian contact whose practical value Lucius knows well.
- Aemilia argues that Lucius knows Rufus's value better than anyone. She notes that relatively few currently plausible matches offer a young unmarried man rather than a substantially older man with prior marriages, and says she will not suggest her own unmarried brother Publius Aemilius Lepidus because Lucius already possesses her family's favour. Lucius recognizes the strategic logic: Publius would reinforce an alliance the Valerii already possess through Aemilia, whereas Rufus could add a different network of equestrian wealth, credit, contracts and information.
- Aemilia explains the point of separate invitations: Terentia and Valeria Tertia can visit as family while Rufus is independently present on Lucius's business/social terms, so Marcus need not first be persuaded to authorize an explicit meeting with a marriage candidate. Lucius agrees that this preserves deniability and lets both Terentia and Valeria observe Rufus before Marcus is asked to consider anything. He cautions that the meeting must remain genuinely exploratory rather than a concealed betrothal negotiation. Lucius agrees in principle to arrange Rufus's invitation; the exact date is not yet established.
- Aemilia and Lucius remain committed to the adult marriage. Lucius remains faithful to his promise. The pregnancy is their hoped-for legitimate child, though its outcome cannot be guaranteed.
- Aemilia's gentler hairstyle preference remains established: soft, low-tension braids kept off her neck, with more elaborate arrangements reserved for occasions when needed.
- Demetrios's household health routines remain in force. The recurring mock gladiatorial games remain a household tradition.
