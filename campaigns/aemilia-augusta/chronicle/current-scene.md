# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, more than a week after Aemilia's pregnancy loss; exact day count remains loose. Aemilia is eighteen, at the Valerian domus in Rome. It is midday on the second day of Aemilia's return to household duties.
- Earlier events remain active canon: Eros's disclosure caused a severe marital argument; Lucius twice physically stopped Aemilia leaving and the second restraint broke his explicit promise not to stop her again. He later admitted **“I was wrong.”** The marital breach remains unresolved.
- Aemilia lost her approximately four-month pregnancy after hours of pain and bleeding in the shared bedchamber despite attendance by Demetrios and the trusted midwife. No medical cause was established. She then remained unconscious for about a day from severe weakness/blood loss before waking.
- Pia failed to return from her first genuine day of leave and remains absent. Her reason and whereabouts/fate remain unestablished. Any search and its results remain unestablished unless played.
- After grief-driven withdrawal, Cornelia's visit and a second withdrawal after sending her mother home, Aemilia returned to household duties. She now works in a changed manner: controlled and soft-voiced but without her former warmth, dimpling smiles or instinct to cushion commands. This reserve applies broadly to everyone, including Eros.
- Aemilia remains physically weakened after major blood loss, prolonged inactivity and limited eating. She is now on her second day back at household work. No fever, renewed hemorrhage or new medical complication is established.
- At midday Terentia, Lucius's stepmother and matron of the elder Messalla household, visits. Aemilia receives her in her plain working presentation, with much of her hair loose down her back.
- Aemilia tells Terentia **“I failed,”** explaining **“My duty to your family.”** She also voices fear that the miscarriage may revive earlier pressure from both families to end the marriage. Terentia establishes that no one has presently threatened the marriage and Lucius has not said he wishes to put Aemilia aside.
- When Terentia asks whether Aemilia and Lucius are well with one another, Aemilia's jaw tightens and she shakes her head. Asked whether Lucius frightened or harmed her, Aemilia says **“No.”** Asked what happened, she initially says her mother was on Lucius's side and that it will be her burden to bear.
- After Terentia says she will neither promise to take Aemilia's side nor automatically take Lucius's merely because he is her husband's son, Aemilia sighs and explains the dispute.
- Aemilia tells Terentia, in substance, that Eros had spoken privately with Aemilia about the treatment and future of enslaved people in the household; Eros later went to Lucius and repeated the conversation, including her judgment that Aemilia was sheltered/weak and likely to yield if Lucius disagreed. Aemilia experienced Lucius's subsequent effort to explain or defend Eros's position as taking Eros's side against his wife.
- Aemilia also explains that during the argument she tried to leave. Lucius stopped her physically once, then promised he would not stop her again; when she later tried to leave again, he moved faster, shut the door and forced her shoulders/back against it while avoiding her pregnant belly. He then recognized that he had broken his promise and admitted **“I was wrong.”** Aemilia's earlier answer that Lucius had not frightened or harmed her remains her own characterization and is not retconned by this disclosure.
- Aemilia explains that abdominal pain began during/after this argument and that later that night she began bleeding and subsequently lost the pregnancy. She does not claim medical certainty that Lucius caused the miscarriage; no medical cause was established by Demetrios or the midwife. Terentia therefore hears the temporal sequence but has no basis to declare causation.
- Terentia now knows substantially more than before: Eros's disclosure, Aemilia's sense that Lucius defended Eros over her, Lucius physically preventing Aemilia from leaving twice, his broken promise, his admission that he was wrong, and the onset of pain after the argument before the later miscarriage. She does not know any facts Aemilia has not conveyed and does not know the later OOC-established background approach by Eros to Lucius.
- Terentia's immediate response is serious and Roman rather than modern: she does not treat a husband laying hands on his wife as inherently outside Roman marital authority, but she regards the second restraint as significant because Lucius had explicitly promised not to stop Aemilia again, because Aemilia was visibly pregnant, and because he used enough force to pin/force her against the door. She also refuses to declare that the restraint caused the miscarriage when the physician and midwife established no cause.

Lucius understood the invitation and was genuinely tempted, but did not resume sexual access or have sex with Eros because his fidelity promise to Aemilia remains a hard line. Aemilia and Terentia do not know this encounter occurred unless later informed.
- Eros's motive in making the approach remains unresolved/mixed rather than established as malicious. Their former sexual relationship existed under enslavement and ownership and is not treated as freely consensual romance.
- Relevant family history remains active: both houses previously pressured the childless marriage; Aemilia's father withdrew his pressure before the miscarriage. Marcus Valerius Messalla privately knows from Lucius that the first six childless years resulted from Lucius's deliberate refusal to attempt conception while Aemilia was a child; whether Marcus told Terentia remains unresolved.
