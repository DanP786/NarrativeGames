# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, morning. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant, at the Valerian domus in Rome.
- Publius workshop crisis active. Figures: Aufidius 12,800; other pressing outside debts 6,200 and 3,600; Valerian due 18,000; pressing total ~40,600. Receivables ~21,000/~12,000 promptly collectible. Forced liquidation likely destroys value/may not cover Valerian claim. Lucius leans controlled rescue but has not executed it; Aufidius standstill on two seized enslaved craftsmen expires today.
- Pia's first genuine weekly day free ordinary labor is today. Another enslaved household woman dressed Aemilia/did hair; usual clothing, visibly different hair. Pia genuinely off ordinary duties.
- Marcus Aemilius Lepidus's unannounced visit has concluded. He withdrew prior pressure to end Aemilia's marriage, accepted her happiness and judgment, and agreed the marriage should continue. Aemilia and Marcus partially reconciled. Cornelia wants to be present during Aemilia's confinement/birth; Aemilia wants her there. Marcus has been told about the trusted unnamed midwife and Demetrios.
- After Marcus leaves, Aemilia and Lucius share an affectionate, impulsive adult marital interlude, non-explicitly narrated. Lucius then returns toward the urgent Publius/Aufidius matter before today's standstill expires.
- Aemilia conducts a private household-discipline inquiry with senior steward Gaius Philargyrus. She learns physical punishment occurs several times in an ordinary month and that lesser delegated supervisors sometimes strike enslaved subordinates without reporting each incident.
- Aemilia orders a one-month trial in the urban domus under Philargyrus's immediate administration: no punitive beating or striking by anyone. Necessary immediate force to stop assault or protect someone from bodily danger remains permitted, including protection of Aemilia and her pregnancy, but may not become punishment afterward.
- Aemilia orders immediate modest rewards for competent ordinary work rather than vague future promises or rewarding exhaustion. Philargyrus will track offenses, responses and rewards so the trial can be assessed. He will inform Lucius because Lucius retains ultimate household authority; unless countermanded, implementation begins immediately.
- Pia's weekly day free ordinary duties still needs formal standing instruction; Pia remains off duty today.
- Aemilia summons Eros, adult enslaved woman and mother of Fortunata (~4) and Felix (~2) by Lucius. Their first substantive private conversation is cautious because of the stark power imbalance.
- Eros explains servants conceal quarrels, injuries, misconduct and complaints from masters, and that ordering truth does not make candor safe. Aemilia explicitly promises not to punish Eros or use her words against her.
- Asked about Lucius, Eros distinguishes caring for him and gratitude for his acknowledgment/care of their children from free choice. She says there were occasions she did not want Lucius to come to her but did not understand refusal as an available option; at other times she felt affection, favor or security. No physical force/threat/punishment for refusal is established.
- Asked whether she wishes to stay, Eros says she prefers this household to sale/transfer while enslaved because her children and practical security are here, but would choose freedom if she could remain near/protect Fortunata and Felix.
- Aemilia says Fortunata and Felix are dear to her, that seeing Lucius enjoy them makes her happy, and that neither she nor Lucius wishes to separate Eros from them. No manumission/status-change promise is made.
- Eros identifies **Menophilus**, an enslaved senior cellar/store servant, as abusing delegated authority through arbitrary striking and intimidation. He oversees several workers handling wine, oil, lamps, serving stores and related supplies and answers upward through the administrative chain to Philargyrus. He is useful and competent enough that his abuses have remained hidden/tolerated below stairs.
- Aemilia asks whether someone else can perform Menophilus's job without beating people.
- Eros says yes, but cautions that replacement is not as simple as selecting the gentlest servant. She identifies **Chryseros**, an enslaved stores clerk/assistant who already handles portions of tallying, issue and receipt of supplies under Menophilus. Chryseros is regarded below stairs as exacting and not especially warm, but Eros has not known him to strike subordinates merely from temper or humiliation.
- Eros believes Chryseros could probably manage much of Menophilus's work, but she cannot certify all of his competence from her own knowledge; Philargyrus would know whether he can handle the full accounts, suppliers and higher-responsibility duties. Promoting him could also create resentment or reveal that someone informed against Menophilus if done abruptly.
- Chryseros's exact age, origin, appearance, years in household and broader personality remain unestablished. No claim is made that he is morally exemplary, only that he is a plausible internal replacement and is not known by Eros for Menophilus's arbitrary violence.
- Menophilus's exact age, origin, appearance, years in household and full history remain unestablished. Eros has not alleged sexual abuse, theft, falsified accounts or direct orders from Philargyrus/Lucius.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia and Eros remain seated alone behind the closed door. Eros has offered Chryseros, an enslaved stores clerk/assistant already doing part of the work, as a plausible replacement for Menophilus, while warning that Philargyrus must assess full competence and that abrupt replacement could expose the existence of an informant.
