- Marcus Aemilius Lepidus's unannounced visit has concluded. He withdrew prior pressure to end Aemilia's marriage, accepted her happiness and judgment, and agreed the marriage should continue. - After Marcus leaves, Aemilia and Lucius share an affectionate, impulsive adult marital interlude, non-explicitly narrated. Lucius then returns toward the urgent Publius/Aufidius matter before today's standstill expires.
- Aemilia conducts a private household-discipline inquiry with senior steward Gaius Philargyrus. She learns physical punishment occurs several times in an ordinary month and that lesser delegated supervisors sometimes strike enslaved subordinates without reporting each incident.
- Aemilia orders a one-month trial in the urban domus under Philargyrus's immediate administration: no punitive beating or striking by anyone. Necessary immediate force to stop assault or protect someone from bodily danger remains permitted, including protection of Aemilia and her pregnancy, but may not become punishment afterward.
- Aemilia orders immediate modest rewards for competent ordinary work rather than vague future promises or rewarding exhaustion. Philargyrus will track offenses, responses and rewards so the trial can be assessed. He will inform Lucius because Lucius retains ultimate household authority; unless countermanded, implementation begins immediately.
- Pia's weekly day free ordinary duties still needs formal standing instruction; Pia remains off duty today.
- Aemilia summons Eros, adult enslaved woman and mother of Fortunata (~4) and Felix (~2) by Lucius. Their first substantive private conversation is cautious because of the stark power imbalance.
- Eros explains servants conceal quarrels, injuries, misconduct and complaints from masters, and that ordering truth does not make candor safe. Aemilia explicitly promises not to punish Eros or use her words against her.
- Asked about Lucius, Eros distinguishes caring for him and gratitude for his acknowledgment/care of their children from free choice. She says there were occasions she did not want Lucius to come to her but did not understand refusal as an available option; at other times she felt affection, favor or security. No physical force/threat/punishment for refusal is established.
- Asked whether she wishes to stay, Eros says she prefers this household to sale/transfer while enslaved because her children and practical security are here, but would choose freedom if she could remain near/protect Fortunata and Felix.
- Aemilia says Fortunata and Felix are dear to her, that seeing Lucius enjoy them makes her happy, and that neither she nor Lucius wishes to separate Eros from them. No manumission/status-change promise is made.
- Eros identifies **Menophilus**, an enslaved senior cellar/store servant, as abusing delegated authority through arbitrary striking and intimidation. He oversees several workers handling wine, oil, lamps, serving stores and related supplies and answers upward through the administrative chain to Philargyrus.
- Eros identifies **Chryseros**, an enslaved stores clerk/assistant already handling part of Menophilus's work, as a plausible replacement. Chryseros is exacting and not especially warm but is not known by Eros to strike subordinates from temper/humiliation. Philargyrus would need to assess his full competence.
- Eros identifies **Thais**, an enslaved senior woman involved in supervising laundry/linen work, as a lesser concern: no known beatings, but favoritism and punitive manipulation of work assignments, sleep and small comforts.
- Eros cautions against treating household problems as a simple list of villains: pressure travels downward, servants conceal errors, and rivalries can generate false/exaggerated accusations. Claims need corroboration.
- Aemilia jokes that she must become 'a senator of my own household' given the calculated moves at play. Eros cautiously agrees that household politics share features with elite politics while emphasizing enslaved people cannot opt out of household power.
- Aemilia then reveals something personal Eros did not necessarily know: Lucius refused to share Aemilia's bed as an adult husband until Aemilia was eighteen. Aemilia admits she had been jealous that Eros bore Lucius's children and could provide adult companionship/intimacy that Aemilia, while still a child, could not.
- Eros receives the confession with surprise and discomfort rather than triumph. She had known the marriage was unusual but did not know the full private boundary. She tells Aemilia that from below stairs the arrangement looked different: Aemilia was the legitimate wife whose future position and legitimate children were secure in ways Eros's never were, while Eros's access to Lucius was not a privilege she could freely accept or refuse.
- Eros does not mock Aemilia's childhood jealousy. She acknowledges that Aemilia could have felt excluded and jealous while still being protected by Lucius's refusal. She also makes clear that Eros herself did not possess the freedom Aemilia imagined when viewing her from the other side of the household hierarchy.
- The disclosure creates a sharper mutual recognition: both women had looked at the other's position and seen an apparent advantage while missing the constraint attached to it. Their positions were not equivalent—Aemilia remained freeborn wife and Eros enslaved—but the jealousy is now spoken rather than silently projected.
