# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, more than a week after Aemilia's pregnancy loss; exact day count remains loose. Aemilia is eighteen, at the Valerian domus in Rome. It is midday on the second day of Aemilia's return to household duties.
- Earlier events remain active canon: Eros's disclosure caused a severe marital argument; Lucius twice physically stopped Aemilia leaving and the second restraint broke his explicit promise not to stop her again. He later admitted **“I was wrong.”** The marital breach remains unresolved.
- Aemilia lost her approximately four-month pregnancy after hours of pain and bleeding in the shared bedchamber despite attendance by Demetrios and the trusted midwife. No medical cause was established. She then remained unconscious for about a day from severe weakness/blood loss before waking.
- Pia failed to return from her first genuine day of leave and remains absent. Her reason and whereabouts/fate remain unestablished. Any search and its results remain unestablished unless played.
- After grief-driven withdrawal, Cornelia's visit and a second withdrawal after sending her mother home, Aemilia returned to household duties. She now works in a changed manner: controlled and soft-voiced but without her former warmth, dimpling smiles or instinct to cushion commands. This reserve applies broadly to everyone, including Eros.
- Aemilia remains physically weakened after major blood loss, prolonged inactivity and limited eating. She is now on her second day back at household work. No fever, renewed hemorrhage or new medical complication is established.
- At midday Terentia, Lucius's stepmother and matron of the elder Messalla household, visits. Aemilia receives her in her plain working presentation, with much of her hair loose down her back.
- Aemilia tells Terentia **“I failed,”** explaining **“My duty to your family.”** She voices fear that the miscarriage may revive earlier pressure from both families to end the marriage. Terentia establishes that no one has presently threatened the marriage and Lucius has not said he wishes to put Aemilia aside.
- Aemilia eventually explains the marital breach: Eros repeated a private conversation to Lucius, including her judgment that Aemilia was sheltered/weak and likely to yield if Lucius disagreed; Lucius's response felt to Aemilia like defending Eros against his wife. During the argument Aemilia tried to leave. Lucius physically stopped her once, promised not to stop her again, then later shut the door and forced her shoulders/back against it when she tried to leave again. He recognized the broken promise and said **“I was wrong.”** Pain began during/after the argument; bleeding and miscarriage followed later that night. No medical cause was established, and neither Aemilia nor Terentia can establish that Lucius caused the loss.
- Aemilia tells Terentia that Lucius says he does not love Eros but cares about her because she is the mother of his children. Aemilia breaks on **“mother of his children”** because she has just lost her own first child and has no surviving child, then says **“She has a power over him.”** Terentia acknowledges Eros has influence through long familiarity and motherhood of Lucius's surviving children, but stresses that Lucius chooses what weight to give Eros's words and is responsible for his own judgment.
- Aemilia asks, **“What do I do? I am not a spiteful person. I do not want anyone punished. But it feels as if in turn I am punished.”** This makes explicit that Aemilia does not want retaliation against Eros or household punishment as a solution; she experiences her own restraint and unwillingness to punish as leaving her to absorb the consequences alone.
- Terentia answers as an experienced Roman matron rather than proposing modern equality. She tells Aemilia that refusing spite does not require refusing boundaries. Aemilia need not punish Eros merely for speaking, but she can cease treating Eros as a private confidante and require household matters to pass through appropriate channels. More importantly, Lucius must be addressed directly: the core marital injury is his decision to give Eros's private judgment of Aemilia weight inside their marriage and his breaking of his word when he physically stopped Aemilia again.
- Terentia advises Aemilia not to demand that Lucius cease caring about Eros's children or pretend Eros has no significance; such a demand would be unrealistic and would entangle innocent children in the quarrel. Instead Aemilia can require clarity about Eros's place relative to the marriage and insist that Lucius not use Eros's judgments as a measure of his wife. Terentia also says Aemilia is not obliged to restore marital warmth or intimacy merely to prove she is not vindictive; reconciliation requires Lucius to address the breach, not simply wait for Aemilia to resume normality.
- Terentia does not know the OOC-established later background approach by Eros to Lucius and therefore cannot reference it.
