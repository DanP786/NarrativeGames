# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, the following morning. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant, at the Valerian domus in Rome.
- Publius originally received 60,000 sesterces for workshop capital; 18,000 is presently due to the Valerian household as an overdue scheduled payment plus interest/arrears; earlier payments were made; workshop assets are pledged. Aemilia proposed investigating all creditors and potentially consolidating claims to preserve the productive workshop.
- Numerius Aufidius Crispus seized two adult enslaved metalworkers from Publius's workshop. One was struck while resisting removal but remains alive. Aufidius claims secured priority over Valerian interests, disputed/unverified. He prefers prompt certainty on his debt. Lucius secured a standstill: neither craftsman sold before the following day while documents exchanged/reviewed. That day is now.
- Aemilia learned the Valerian household punishes enslaved people and she had been shielded. Pia disclosed being physically struck when younger and structural sexual vulnerability, though no one sexually forced Pia. Aemilia ordered steward meeting today.
- Aemilia granted Pia one genuine weekly day free ordinary labor and starts it immediately today. Another enslaved household woman dresses Aemilia/does hair. Usual clothing, visibly different hair. Pia genuinely off ordinary duties today.
- Aemilia and Lucius review Priscus's compiled material. Aemilia's Adept Literacy lets her read confidently; Novice Accounts lets her follow main structure/obvious pressure points without resolving all accounting/legal ambiguity.
- Creditor schedule: Aufidius gross 14,400, less documented 1,600 bronze adjustment = 12,800 demanded. Second trade creditor 6,200 for charcoal/transport/supplies. Smaller private obligation 3,600 overdue. Valerian amount presently due 18,000. Pressing total ~40,600.
- Publius has ~21,000 customer receivables; only ~12,000 judged promptly collectible. Remaining ~9,000 is doubtful/delayed, not established loss. Some may arrive later, be reduced, or never recover. Finished/partly finished stock worth less in forced sale than completion; tools/furnaces/material are productive assets rather than easy cash.
- Workshop appears capable of earning out over time if kept operating but cannot satisfy pressing creditors now. Piecemeal seizure probably destroys value. Consolidation/controlled standstill has logic but transfers risk to Valerians.
- Aufidius's asserted priority is not cleanly proven; real debt/security, ambiguity whether reaches craftsmen ahead of earlier Valerian pledge. Resolution may exceed today's standstill. Bronze dispute real but limited; no proof it caused all broader losses.
- Aemilia proposes Valerians satisfy/secure outside creditors, keep workshop operating, consolidate debt under Valerian control and charge Publius more interest. Lucius accepts coherent in principle but wants stronger controls: no new borrowing without approval, direct account visibility, collected customer debts first against Valerian exposure, adequate security.
- Earlier repayment history on Valerian capital was substantially regular; present crisis is deterioration, not six-year chronic nonpayment. Aggressive new trade borrowing clusters after customer receipts slowed and around disputed bronze/rework period. No obvious large personal luxury diversion/hidden second business in current material.
- Aemilia now identifies the central mechanism herself: Publius kept borrowing to buy time while waiting for customers to pay. He continued commissions and bought inputs on credit without enough deposits/short customer terms, effectively financing customers with creditors' money. This turned a liquidity squeeze into a creditor spiral.
- Aemilia also observes that liquidation is unattractive because forced-sale value of workshop assets is unlikely to make the Valerian household whole, much less satisfy all creditors. Lucius agrees this is why immediate seizure is not automatically prudent. However, he distinguishes 'liquidation loses money' from 'rescue is profitable': sunk exposure can tempt a creditor to risk more merely to avoid recognizing an existing loss.
- Lucius treats Aemilia's diagnosis as substantially correct but pushes the next question: whether Publius learned from the spiral and whether the Valerians can change the business terms that caused it. A rescue cannot simply restore him to borrowing and extending generous customer credit as before. The likely workable structure would require deposits/shorter customer terms where possible, controlled purchasing/borrowing, direct oversight of receipts, and preserving skilled labor so the workshop can finish work. Exact terms not decided.
