# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, morning. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant, at the Valerian domus in Rome.
- Publius workshop crisis active. Figures: Aufidius 12,800; other pressing outside debts 6,200 and 3,600; Valerian due 18,000; pressing total ~40,600. Receivables ~21,000/~12,000 promptly collectible. Forced liquidation likely destroys value/may not cover Valerian claim. Lucius leans controlled rescue but has not executed it; Aufidius standstill on two seized enslaved craftsmen expires today.
- Pia's first genuine weekly day free ordinary labor is today. Another enslaved household woman dressed Aemilia/did hair; usual clothing, visibly different hair. Pia genuinely off ordinary duties.
- Marcus Aemilius Lepidus's unannounced visit has concluded. He withdrew prior pressure to end Aemilia's marriage, accepted her happiness and judgment, and agreed the marriage should continue. Aemilia and Marcus partially reconciled. Cornelia wants to be present during Aemilia's confinement/birth; Aemilia wants her there. Marcus has been told about the trusted unnamed midwife and Demetrios.
- After Marcus leaves, Aemilia and Lucius share an affectionate, impulsive adult marital interlude, non-explicitly narrated. Lucius then returns toward the urgent Publius/Aufidius matter before today's standstill expires.
- Aemilia is conducting a private household-discipline inquiry with senior steward Gaius Philargyrus. His legal status, age, origin and detailed personal history remain unestablished.
- Philargyrus has explained that minor mistakes occur constantly and are usually corrected without severe punishment; deliberate offenses include theft, falsified accounts/stores, repeated disobedience, deliberate damage, serious fighting, unauthorized absence, attempted flight/aiding flight, and serious deception.
- Across the urban household, a matter requiring Philargyrus's direct disciplinary attention arises roughly every few days; physical punishment occurs several times in an ordinary month by his practical estimate. Lesser delegated superiors sometimes strike/correct enslaved subordinates without reporting each incident, so neither Philargyrus nor Lucius/Aemilia automatically knows every blow delivered under the roof.
- Aemilia asks: 'You think beating is an effective deterrent? Or a reward for a job well done?'
- Philargyrus treats the second half as pointed irony rather than a literal suggestion. He says no competent steward regards beating as a reward. He does regard the credible possibility of physical punishment as a deterrent for some deliberate offenses, particularly repeated defiance, theft, violence and attempted flight, because fear can secure immediate compliance.
- He does not claim beating makes people loyal, honest or skilled. He acknowledges excessive or arbitrary violence can produce concealment, resentment, damaged workers, poorer work and flight—the opposite of good administration. His defense is pragmatic and Roman: punishment must be predictable, proportionate by household standards and tied to an understood offense, not inflicted merely because a superior is angry.
- Philargyrus challenges the easy alternative: if Aemilia wishes physical punishment reduced or forbidden, he needs to know what sanctions she expects administrators to use when reprimand, restitution, reassignment and loss of privileges fail, especially for theft, assault, repeated refusal or flight attempts. He does not refuse her authority; he asks what system is to replace the existing one.
- Aemilia's intended subjects still include who may order punishment; exact physical punishments used; recording/reporting; preventing abuse outside Lucius/Aemilia's notice; and formalizing Pia's weekly day free ordinary duties. Pia remains off duty.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia and Philargyrus remain seated. The discussion has moved from discovering that violence occurs to whether it works and what would replace it. Philargyrus awaits Aemilia's response. Lucius is occupied with Publius/Aufidius.
