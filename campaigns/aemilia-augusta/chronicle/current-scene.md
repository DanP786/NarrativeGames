# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC. Aemilia is eighteen and remains in physical recovery from severe blood loss after the pregnancy loss. Pregnancy loss remains medically unresolved and unrelated to Pia's disappearance.
- Pia has been recovered alive. Investigation establishes **Eros → Celer → Philargyrus → Naso → onward commercial chain**. Eros deliberately supplied Pia's identity, legitimate leave, timing/route and facilitated physical interception to isolate/weaken Aemilia. Eros intended a temporary/private removal rather than onward sale; Celer independently corroborates that original scope. Eros later learned from Celer that Pia had passed into commercial danger and remained silent to protect herself despite having ordinary means to warn the household.
- Eros also used small personal funds plus promised commercially useful Valerian information as consideration, and delivered limited ordinary purchasing/supplier information. No evidence establishes she caused Aemilia's pregnancy loss or ordered Pia's onward sale.
- After the interrogation Lucius and Aemilia repair the immediate rupture caused by Lucius contradicting Aemilia before Eros. Lucius acknowledges this undermined marital solidarity; commits to handling non-urgent disagreements privately rather than supplying arguments against Aemilia before someone exploiting division. Aemilia explicitly says **“you are forgiven”**, initiates an embrace, and Lucius accepts without treating it as erasure of older wounds. The older severe breach remains part of ongoing marital repair.
- Aemilia and Lucius jointly settle Eros's punishment. Aemilia rejects sale as too cruel despite retaliatory anger. Eros is permanently removed from privileged/domestic service in Rome and assigned to supervised ordinary agricultural labor on an unnamed Valerian agricultural property. The chosen placement is close enough that controlled child visits remain practicable but separated from Roman household information networks. A trusted estate supervisor is to ensure Eros works under ordinary discipline without special indulgence for bearing Lucius's children and without gratuitous brutality.
- Eros loses access to Valerian accounts, purchasing information, correspondence, messengers and household movements. She may not return to the Roman domus without express permission and has no ordinary access to Aemilia or Pia.
- Fortunata and Felix remain in the Valerian domus in Rome. They are not punished, demoted, or displaced because of Eros's acts. They are to have stable rooms within the main domestic household, remain together, retain familiar safe caregivers and routines, and receive ordinary affectionate care. Exact named rooms/caregivers are deliberately not fixed.
- Fortunata receives only an age-appropriate explanation that her mother has done something serious and must live/work elsewhere; Fortunata and Felix did nothing wrong. Felix, being approximately two, is given continuity of routine rather than adult explanations. Household members are not to treat the children as disgraced.
- Eros's contact with Fortunata/Felix begins infrequently, broadly around selected suitable feriae/holiday opportunities rather than ordinary daily contact. Visits are supervised/controlled and child-centered. No messages may be carried through the children. Greater frequency may be granted over years if Eros demonstrates sustained reliable conduct and does not use children to reach into the Roman household; increases are conditional/revocable rather than automatic and may eventually reach roughly weekly contact if circumstances warrant. Children's welfare controls.
- Household receives bounded truth sufficient for deterrence: Eros deliberately acted against Aemilia/the household in connection with Pia's disappearance, betrayed household trust, and was removed from domestic service as consequence. Private marital details, Eros's former sexual history with Lucius, and unsupported claims that she ordered onward sale are not disclosed.
- Aemilia and Lucius return together to the guarded room where Eros is confined and deliver the verdict themselves. Their return together visibly presents a united marital/household judgment without pretending formal authority is equal.
- Lucius formally pronounces the sentence: Eros will leave the Roman household and be assigned to agricultural labor on the selected Valerian property; she will not be sold; Fortunata and Felix remain in Rome, safe and cared for; Eros will retain limited controlled contact with them, with possibility of increased frequency only through sustained reliable conduct over years.
- Eros's composure breaks most visibly at the separation from Fortunata and Felix. She asks whether they are being sent away with her. Lucius answers no: the children remain in the Roman household and are not punished for her acts. Eros then asks whether she will see them. Lucius answers yes, but not as before; the initial contact will be infrequent and controlled, with any increase dependent on conduct and the children's welfare.
- Eros understands that the sentence strips away the sphere she fought to preserve: Roman domestic position, access, information, influence, and ordinary daily motherhood. She does not receive sale, mutilation, death, or a corporal spectacle.
- Lucius explicitly identifies the sentence as substantially the judgment he and Aemilia reached together. He does not undermine Aemilia before Eros or reopen the evidentiary dispute during sentencing.
