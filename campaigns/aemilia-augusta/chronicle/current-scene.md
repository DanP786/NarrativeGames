# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, toward dawn after a prolonged night medical crisis. Aemilia is eighteen, at the Valerian domus in Rome.
- Earlier that day Marcus Aemilius Lepidus withdrew pressure to end Aemilia's marriage and partially reconciled with her. Cornelia wants to attend Aemilia's confinement/birth; Aemilia had wanted her there. Pia's first genuine day free ordinary duties remained intact; formal weekly instruction remains pending.
- Aemilia's one-month urban-domus discipline trial remains approved conditionally by Lucius.
- Earlier at bedtime Eros's private disclosure to Lucius caused a severe marital argument. During it Aemilia twice tried to leave and Lucius physically stopped her twice; the second time he broke an explicit promise not to stop her again, drove the door shut and forced her back against it while avoiding direct pressure on her abdomen. He later admitted **“I was wrong.”** The marital breach remains unresolved.
- Aemilia developed abdominal pain before sleep. Later she woke with significant vaginal bleeding at approximately four months pregnant. Demetrios and the trusted midwife were summoned.
- The trusted midwife concluded from ongoing bleeding, recurrent labor-like/cramping pain and physical examination that Aemilia was losing the pregnancy and that the process could not realistically be stopped with available means. No causal link was established to emotion, activity, the argument, Lucius's restraint or any moral fault. Demetrios explicitly refused causal certainty.
- Over the following hours the pregnancy loss progresses and is completed sufficiently that the pregnancy is no longer ongoing. The physical details of the expelled pregnancy are kept non-graphic; exact sex/appearance and funerary handling have not yet been established.
- Aemilia experiences prolonged pain and blood loss through the hours. Afterward she becomes unresponsive/comatose rather than merely falling asleep. This is a serious maternal complication, not ordinary rest.
- Demetrios assesses her as alive but dangerously weakened. He monitors breathing, pulse by touch, skin warmth/color, responsiveness and ongoing bleeding with the limited methods available to ancient medicine. The midwife continues to monitor whether bleeding is slowing and whether the loss appears complete. No modern diagnosis, transfusion or resuscitation is available.
- Aemilia's current unconsciousness may plausibly reflect severe exhaustion and/or blood loss, but exact mechanism is not established. Demetrios does not claim certainty. Fever/infection is not yet established. Continued heavy hemorrhage would be immediately life-threatening; whether bleeding has adequately slowed remains to be established in play.
- Lucius remains present through the hours. He is devastated by the loss and alarmed by Aemilia's unresponsiveness. He does not treat unconsciousness as sleep and presses Demetrios for whether she is dying. Demetrios cannot promise survival; he focuses on observable signs and keeping her warm, still, clean, and monitored.
- No blame is assigned medically for the miscarriage. Lucius may privately fear/guilt the temporal proximity of his earlier conduct, but this is not proof of causation and must not be narrated as medical fact.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Toward dawn after hours of pregnancy loss. Aemilia lies motionless and unresponsive in bed after prolonged pain and bleeding. The pregnancy has been lost. She is alive but in serious danger from the aftermath; Demetrios and the trusted midwife remain at bedside monitoring bleeding and vital signs with ancient limitations. Maternal survival, recovery time and complications remain unresolved.
