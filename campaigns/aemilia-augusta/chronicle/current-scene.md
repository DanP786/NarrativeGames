# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC. Roughly a week has passed at the Valerian country villa since Aemilia and Lucius first resumed marital intimacy; the pregnancy loss is now more than two weeks in the past, though exact day count remains loose. Aemilia is eighteen and has returned with Lucius to the Roman domus.
- The severe marital breach remains canon: Lucius twice physically stopped Aemilia leaving an argument and the second restraint broke his explicit promise not to do so again. He later admitted **“I was wrong.”** The breach is actively being repaired but is not erased.
- During the week at the villa, Aemilia consistently welcomed Lucius's touch and took comfort in his devotion. She talked freely with him and initiated or returned affectionate touch. She still does not smile freely and does not laugh. Their adult marital intimacy has resumed voluntarily. No new pregnancy is established.
- Aemilia remains in physical recovery from severe blood loss and prolonged weakness. The quiet week improved her rest and endurance, but she is not fully restored to her former strength. No fever, renewed hemorrhage or new medical complication is established.
- Pia remains missing. The initial discreet information-only inquiry produced its first uncertain lead: a witness along the route associated with Pia's genuine leave recalls a woman broadly matching Pia's description with unfamiliar men and remembers that she did not appear to be moving with them freely. The witness cannot establish identity with certainty or say where the group ultimately went.
- On hearing this lead, Aemilia raises her voice for the first time anyone present has known her to do so: **“Then find her! I do not just want information if she is taken against her will. You know I was concerned about her.”** She then orders, **“I want her home. Immediately!”** before taking a breath, tears in her eyes, and adding **“Safely.”**
- The standing Pia instruction is explicit: if Pia is found held against her will, the Valerian household is to recover and bring her home as soon as this can be done safely. Speed does not override Pia's safety. A genuinely voluntary escape remains a distinct case.
- The steward accepts the order and begins treating the inquiry as an urgent recovery effort. Lucius supports Aemilia's instruction and places Valerian household resources and connections behind it. No exact captor, transaction or destination is yet known.
- After the steward leaves, Aemilia grips Lucius's toga and fears what Pia may have endured during the weeks missing. Lucius refuses to invent specific horrors from uncertainty and emphasizes finding Pia alive, making her safe, and learning the truth from Pia rather than fear.
- Aemilia then says, **“I hope she forgives me. I should have sent someone to find her the same night she didn’t return.”** Lucius does not promise Pia will forgive her or claim there was no failure at all. He distinguishes hindsight from what they knew that first night: Pia had genuine leave, and a late return did not itself prove abduction. He also acknowledges that, knowing what they know now, he wishes the household had begun searching sooner. He reminds Aemilia that responsibility for the initial response was not hers alone and that whoever took Pia, if the witness lead is true, bears responsibility for taking her. Whether Pia is angry, frightened, hurt or forgiving must be allowed to belong to Pia when she is found.

During genuine leave she was unlawfully seized and subsequently held/sold onward as enslaved property despite already belonging to the Valerian household. Aemilia, Lucius and the household do not know this hidden truth; it must be uncovered through plausible investigation.
