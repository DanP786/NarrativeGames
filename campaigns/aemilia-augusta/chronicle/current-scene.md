# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, night. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant, at the Valerian domus in Rome.
- Aemilia's unfamiliar hairstyle has been released. Pia's first genuine day free ordinary duties remained intact through the day; a formal standing weekly instruction remains pending.
- Marcus Aemilius Lepidus visited earlier today, withdrew prior pressure to end Aemilia's marriage, accepted her happiness and judgment, and partially reconciled with her. Cornelia wants to attend Aemilia's confinement/birth; Aemilia wants her there.
- Aemilia ordered a one-month urban-domus discipline trial after questioning Philargyrus: no punitive beating/striking; immediate protective force permitted; immediate modest rewards for competent work; simple tracking of serious offenses, responses and rewards. Lucius explicitly approved the month conditionally.
- Aemilia's private conversation with Eros established that Lucius's past sexual access was not something Eros always wanted and refusal was not meaningfully available; Eros prefers remaining with her children to sale/transfer but would choose freedom if she could remain near/protect them. Aemilia promised no retaliation and said she and Lucius do not wish to separate Eros from Fortunata/Felix; no manumission promise was made.
- Eros identified Menophilus as an abusive delegated supervisor, Chryseros as a plausible replacement, and Thais as a lesser concern for favoritism/punitive manipulation. These allegations remain unverified.
- During the afternoon/evening, Eros privately told Lucius the substance of her conversation with Aemilia. Lucius now knows about Eros's inability to meaningfully refuse his former sexual access, her conditional wish for freedom, the Menophilus/Chryseros/Thais allegations, Aemilia's former jealousy, and Aemilia's statement that the only person she has to fear is her husband. Eros also conveyed her judgment that Aemilia is kind and sincere but sheltered and unlikely to prevail if Lucius says no. Lucius did not retaliate against Eros for speaking.
- At midday Aemilia and Lucius discussed ignorance, household cruelty, his authority and his past access to enslaved women. Lucius emphasized that Aemilia's willingness matters because she can refuse him. Aemilia said she would never refuse him; Lucius insisted he does not require permanent sexual availability.
- Publius workshop crisis remains active. Earlier figures: Aufidius 12,800; other pressing outside debts 6,200 and 3,600; Valerian due 18,000; pressing total ~40,600; receivables ~21,000/~12,000 promptly collectible. The exact outcome of today's creditor deadline remains unestablished on-screen and must not be assumed.
- At bedtime Lucius enters their bedchamber and asks Aemilia, **“You are afraid of me?”** He reveals Eros came to him. Aemilia scowls, says **“Of course she did,”** stands with one hand on her pregnant abdomen and keeps several paces between them. Lucius explains Eros came because he holds final household authority and she does not trust Aemilia's goodwill alone to survive disagreement with him.
- Aemilia objects that nothing is presently being decided. Lucius agrees no immediate ruling is being made about Eros, her children, Menophilus, Chryseros or Thais, but explains that Eros has little control over when others decide her life and therefore wanted him informed beforehand.
- Aemilia feels personally betrayed and humiliated by Eros's disclosure. She had approached Eros hoping to understand enslaved lives, improve what she could, and lay down her longstanding jealousy toward the woman who had shared Lucius's bed and borne his children before Aemilia. She now feels that private vulnerability was carried directly to Lucius and that she was made a fool of. These are Aemilia's internal feelings and Lucius does not automatically know them.
- When Lucius again asks why Aemilia called him the only person she has to fear, Aemilia struggles to organize her thoughts and answers: **“I… I meant it in the context that you control part of my life, for her there are more.”** She is distinguishing the social/marital authority Lucius exercises over parts of her life from Eros's far broader vulnerability as an enslaved woman, not claiming equivalence between their positions.
- Lucius accepts the distinction as intelligible rather than accusing Aemilia of claiming she and Eros are equally unfree. He notes that he does in fact possess authority capable of materially affecting Aemilia's life, while Eros is vulnerable to him and to a wider chain of household authority in ways Aemilia is not. He remains troubled by the word **fear** and by Aemilia's physical distance, but does not force her closer.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia stands several paces from Lucius, hand on her abdomen, feeling betrayed by Eros though she has not yet articulated that betrayal aloud. She has explained that her remark about fearing Lucius referred to his real authority over part of her life, contrasted with the many people who hold power over Eros. Lucius is listening and has not closed the distance.
