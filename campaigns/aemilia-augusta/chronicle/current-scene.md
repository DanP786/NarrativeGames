# Current scene

- S01, approximately 12 BC. Aemilia is approximately three months pregnant. The pregnancy is progressing without an established complication.
- The birthday gathering at the residence of Marcus Valerius Messalla Minor and Servilia has ended. Later that evening Aemilia and Lucius are privately preparing for bed.
- A private message has been sent to Aemilia's parents, Marcus Aemilius Lepidus and Cornelia, telling them directly of the pregnancy so they will not learn through Valerian gossip.
- Lucius's father, Marcus Valerius Messalla, has learned directly that Aemilia is about three months pregnant. The timing convinces him that Lucius truly did deliberately postpone conception until Aemilia was an adult; Marcus grudgingly acknowledges that Lucius's judgment may have been right.
- Marcus recognizes Demetrios by reputation and calls him a fanatic for his persistent advocacy of stricter household cleanliness. After Aemilia quietly reports fewer illnesses since adopting Demetrios's practices, Marcus says he will hear Demetrios out and nothing more, adding that 'your mother' will decide whether the physician is worth disrupting the household. By 'your mother' Marcus means Terentia, Lucius's stepmother; the usage bristles Lucius, whose biological mother Claudia Pulchra died when he was about ten.
- At the gathering Terentia drew Aemilia aside. Their exchange was openly affectionate and teasing. Terentia told Aemilia she has always been her favourite, excluding Terentia's own biological children Decimus and Valeria Tertia.
- Terentia and Aemilia discussed potential husbands for Valeria Tertia. Aemilia suggested the unconventional prospect Gaius Vibius Rufus, about twenty-two, from a prosperous non-senatorial equestrian family and useful to Lucius's network. Aemilia describes him as kind, clever and perceptive, and reports that he has never attended a brothel and only occasionally attends public bathhouses. Aemilia further explained that Rufus's ambitions appear directed not toward political office but toward information and that he is not controlling but rarely concedes once persuaded. Terentia takes the proposal seriously despite the pedigree problem.
- Aemilia conspiratorially suggested that Lucius might happen to invite Rufus to their villa on the same day Aemilia happens to invite Terentia, allowing Terentia to observe Rufus without formally declaring him a marriage prospect. Terentia approved the discreet arrangement. The invitation has not yet been issued and Lucius has not yet agreed to participate.
- At the gathering Valeria Messalla enthusiastically said she had prayed for Aemilia to become pregnant. Valeria teasingly remarked that Lucius has always loved women and that their father spent heavily on silphium/laser as a contraceptive in Lucius's youth. Servilia added that it was evidently not used reliably with the enslaved women after Lucius married Aemilia, an allusion to his three surviving enslaved children and four children who died. The women do not know the full private history of Lucius deliberately delaying marital conception unless separately told.
- Asked whether she knew immediately that she was pregnant, Aemilia answered softly, 'I believe so.' Asked whether she told Lucius as soon as she suspected, Aemilia answered, 'I did.' Asked how he reacted, Aemilia said simply, 'He kissed me.' When Valeria observed that Lucius wanted the pregnancy very much, Aemilia smiled and answered, 'Yes.' Servilia then remarked that it must have been frustrating for Aemilia to see Lucius's enslaved women become pregnant while she did not. Aemilia answered without repudiating those children: placing a hand over her slight pregnant belly, she said, 'His children are dear to me. All of them.' The statement includes Hilarus, Fortunata and Felix as well as the unborn child.
- Later that evening, while privately preparing for bed with Lucius, Aemilia tells him that Valeria Major mentioned their father buying a great deal of laser for enslaved women when Lucius was younger. Lucius says Valeria exaggerated, though not entirely: Marcus was concerned about Lucius fathering inconvenient children before marriage and purchased contraceptive preparations, including laser, whose reputed reliability exceeded their actual reliability.
- Aemilia asks whether Eros and Daphne took the contraceptive preparations. Lucius explains that preparations were made available to them, but he cannot truthfully account for every dose or how consistently either woman used them. The women had their own reasons to use, avoid, conceal or discard such preparations while remaining unable to freely refuse Lucius's sexual access under slavery.
- Eros and Daphne were not the only adult enslaved women to whom Lucius exercised sexual access during the waiting years. Across those years there were six adult enslaved women in total, including Eros and Daphne. The number refers to women to whom he exercised sexual access, not freely consenting lovers. No additional women are named. Existing canon remains seven babies fathered in total, four of whom died and three of whom survive.
- Pia was not among them. Pia came into the Valerian household as Aemilia's personal attendant from her natal household, and Lucius never exercised sexual access to her.
- Realizing more fully that Lucius slept with more enslaved women than Eros and Daphne, Aemilia looks down with a hand resting on her pregnant belly and tells him, 'I didn't know your need for a woman's company until recently.' Lucius understands that she is connecting what she has learned of his frequent adult marital appetite with the scale of his conduct during the years he deliberately kept her marriage unconsummated. The conversation has become serious rather than teasing.
- Aemilia asks directly, 'How many?' Lucius answers, 'Six.' He makes clear that this is six adult enslaved women across the years between their marriage and Aemilia's adulthood, including Eros and Daphne, not six simultaneous relationships and not freely consenting lovers.
- Aemilia turns away, raises a hand to her throat and asks, 'From this household?' Lucius answers that some were from their own household and some were encountered while he was away with other parts of the wider Valerian establishment. Eros and Daphne remain in Aemilia's household, while the whereabouts or current status of the other four women are not yet established canon.
- Aemilia declines the offered names and says, 'No, our true marriage didn't start until I was eighteen. I should not act otherwise.' She crosses to her side of the bed in her sleeping dress. Her statement deliberately separates Lucius's conduct during the years when he maintained a childhood boundary from the fidelity she expects in their adult marriage, without pretending that learning the fuller history leaves her untouched.
- Lucius accepts the distinction rather than using it to excuse himself. He does not press Aemilia for reassurance or physical affection and reiterates that the promise he made when she became an adult governs the marriage now.
- Once in bed, Aemilia looks down at her pregnant belly and asks whether Lucius would still keep his fidelity promise if pregnancy, childbirth, illness, medical caution, or some other circumstance means they cannot share marital intimacy for a time. Lucius answers unequivocally that he would. His promise was fidelity, not fidelity only while intimacy remained available; abstinence may be difficult for him but does not release him from his word.
- Aemilia and Lucius remain committed to the adult marriage, though this conversation has exposed information Aemilia did not previously understand about his past conduct. Lucius remains faithful to his promise. The pregnancy is their hoped-for legitimate child, though its outcome cannot be guaranteed.
- Aemilia's gentler hairstyle preference remains established: soft, low-tension braids kept off her neck, with more elaborate arrangements reserved for occasions when needed.
- Demetrios's household health routines remain in force. The recurring mock gladiatorial games remain a household tradition.
