# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, several weeks after S01. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant. The pregnancy is more visibly apparent but no complication is established.
- Morning at Lucius and Aemilia's Valerian domus in Rome. Lucius is away from the domus. The discreet Rufus-Valeria gathering is now several weeks past. Terentia remains cautiously favorable; Valeria and Rufus remain unaware of the matchmaking purpose; Marcus Valerius Messalla has not been told. Rufus's Atalanta-poem search remains unresolved.
- Aemilia receives Tullia in the tablinum with Pia attending. Tullia is an adult married Roman woman in her thirties from a respectable senatorial-connected family, known to Aemilia by sight from elite social and religious occasions but not as an intimate. Tullia's own attendant remains near the doorway.
- Tullia explains that one of her husband's freedmen, Publius, has been pressed for payment on an obligation by a Valerian household agent. The debt itself is not denied. The agent has threatened to seize pledged workshop equipment and stock if payment is not produced promptly. Publius and his wife say this would prevent the workshop from continuing to earn.
- Aemilia promises to take the matter to Lucius when he returns, saying the household would not want a man's livelihood stripped without consideration. She does not forgive the debt, declare the agent wrong, order collection stopped herself, or accept Publius's account as proven.
- Aemilia then asks for more detail: how much is owed and why Publius cannot pay.
- Tullia says Publius told her the payment presently demanded is 18,000 sesterces. The obligation arose from money advanced for workshop stock and operating expenses, secured in part against equipment and stock. Tullia does not claim to have inspected the written accounts herself.
- According to Publius's account, the workshop is a metalworking business producing household fittings, lamps, hinges, locks, and similar goods. He suffered a combination of delayed payments from customers and a costly batch of purchased metal that proved poorer in quality than represented, forcing some work to be remade and tying up cash in unusable or discounted stock. He says the business is still operating and has orders due to pay within the coming months, but lacks enough liquid money to meet the Valerian demand now without surrendering productive assets.
- Tullia is careful to distinguish what she knows from what Publius has told her. She says she can vouch that the workshop exists and that Publius has long served her husband's household reliably, but she cannot personally certify his books, the quality dispute over the metal, or the exact timing of his customers' promised payments.
- The Valerian agent's identity, the full original amount and exact contractual terms, whether 18,000 sesterces is principal/interest/a due installment, whether seizure is contractually justified, and whether Publius's business account is accurate remain unverified. These can be checked against Lucius's household records.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Tullia has supplied Aemilia with a concrete amount and Publius's stated explanation for the shortfall, while openly acknowledging the limits of her knowledge. Aemilia has enough information to ask sharper questions of Lucius and his accounts when he returns.
