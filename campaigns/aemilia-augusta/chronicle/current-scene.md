# Current scene

- S01, approximately 12 BC, several weeks after Aemilia and Lucius' late-night conversation. Aemilia remains seventeen and is now somewhat closer to her eighteenth birthday; Lucius is approximately thirty.
- Morning at the Valerian domus in Rome. Aemilia's father has escalated his concern about the long-childless marriage. Rather than openly accuse Lucius or demand a divorce, he sends respectable household delegates to sound out Lucius' intentions and investigate whether the marriage is in danger.
- The delegates' questions are indirect but unmistakable: does Lucius remain satisfied with Aemilia as wife; is divorce being contemplated; has he sought physicians regarding the absence of pregnancy; has he prayed, vowed offerings or made sacrifices for fertility and legitimate issue; and does he regard the failure to produce an heir as a problem requiring action.
- The inquiry puts Lucius in a delicate position. Elite society believes the marriage has long been consummated, while in fact Lucius deliberately kept it unconsummated while Aemilia was a child. He therefore cannot truthfully explain the childlessness without exposing their secret, but he also has no intention of allowing Aemilia's natal family to treat divorce as imminent.
- Lucius receives the delegates formally and makes his position on the marriage unmistakable: he is satisfied with Aemilia, has no present intention of divorcing her, and considers speculation about ending the marriage unwarranted. On physicians and fertility rites he answers carefully, protecting the private reason no pregnancy has occurred without inviting outsiders into Aemilia's body or the intimate affairs of his household.
- The previously contemplated physician access is superseded by this broader paternal inquiry: no intimate examination of Aemilia occurs. Aemilia remains seventeen; no sexual or erotic content occurs.
