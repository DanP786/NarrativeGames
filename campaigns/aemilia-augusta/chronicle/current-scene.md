# Current scene

- S02, approximately 12 BC, morning. Aemilia is eighteen and approximately four months pregnant, at the Valerian domus in Rome.
- Publius workshop crisis active. Figures: Aufidius 12,800; other pressing outside debts 6,200 and 3,600; Valerian due 18,000; pressing total ~40,600. Receivables ~21,000/~12,000 promptly collectible. Forced liquidation likely destroys value/may not cover Valerian claim. Lucius leans controlled rescue but has not executed it; Aufidius standstill on two seized enslaved craftsmen expires today.
- Pia's first genuine weekly day free ordinary labor is today. Another enslaved household woman dressed Aemilia/did hair; usual clothing, visibly different hair. Pia genuinely off ordinary duties. Steward meeting remains due later.
- Aemilia's father Marcus Aemilius Lepidus, 49, senator/former praetor, arrived uninvited/without prior message. Conservative regarding daughters' education/female public visibility. Previously pressured Aemilia to end marriage and privately explored dowry/remarriage contingencies during long childless period.
- Marcus came privately before Cornelia/family. He explicitly reverses prior course: pregnancy removes central childlessness premise; marriage should continue; he will not pursue/encourage dissolution or another match on old grounds. He admits discounting Aemilia's judgment and says hers proved better on this matter. Aemilia says she is happy in Valerian household; Marcus accepts this as real and says he should have weighted it more.
- Lucius arrives after urgent business and catches Marcus saying he has no wish to take Aemilia's present life from her. Lucius greets him properly but corrects implied authority: Aemilia's place as his wife is not Marcus's simply to grant or withdraw. He states marriage continues because Lucius intends it and Aemilia has chosen it, and he would resist natal-family attempts to remove/reassign her. This is personal/political/household assertion, not claim of legal indissolubility.
- Aemilia remains deliberately silent during the exchange between husband and father. She understands that interrupting Lucius while he asserts marital/household authority would weaken the very position he is defending. Her silence is strategic social judgment, not fear or absence of opinion. She has already made her own wish clear to both men: she is happy and chooses the marriage.
- Marcus notices that Aemilia does not contradict Lucius. He answers Lucius without trying to enlist Aemilia against him. Marcus says he did possess duties/interests as her father and will not apologize for having considered her future, but concedes he pressed beyond what the facts justified and that Aemilia herself rejected his proposed course.
- Marcus accepts the present practical point: he is not here to reclaim Aemilia, negotiate a new husband, or reopen dissolution. He says, in effect, that Lucius need not defend the marriage against him today because Marcus has already abandoned that effort. He does not concede that Lucius alone possesses every conceivable legal/familial right, preserving Roman status realism and his own dignity.
- Lucius does not apologize for drawing the line. The two men now understand each other's present positions: Marcus withdraws active interference; Lucius makes clear renewed interference would be opposed; Aemilia's expressed choice aligns with Lucius.
- CURRENT MOMENT: Aemilia remains seated and silent while Marcus and Lucius face one another after this exchange. The immediate question is whether the tension settles into civility or Marcus has more to say about his visit/pregnancy. Publius deadline active; steward meeting later; Pia off duty.
