# Current scene

- S01, approximately 12 BC, around five and a half years after the wedding-night opening.
- Aemilia is seventeen, a few months from her eighteenth birthday. Lucius is approximately thirty.
- Their marriage remains privately unconsummated. Publicly Lucius behaves as an established husband and protects the secret without hesitation.
- It is publicly known that during the marriage Lucius has fathered children by adult enslaved women in his household. This makes male infertility an implausible explanation for Aemilia's lack of pregnancies, while those children do not replace the legitimate heirs expected from his marriage.
- At an elite private gathering in Rome, Gaius Fabius Crispus probes Aemilia about the marriage's lack of children and suggests Lucius' extraordinary kindness may be part of the explanation.
- Aemilia answers, 'A man must be unkind to have children?' Her retort exposes the ugliness of Crispus' insinuation.
- Crispus counters that everyone knows Lucius is capable of fathering children because he has children by enslaved women. He carefully distinguishes biological fertility from legitimate succession and uses that fact to sharpen the question of why Aemilia has never been pregnant.
- Lucius reaches them during the exchange and hears enough to understand Crispus is probing the private marriage. He remains outwardly controlled and gives Aemilia the opportunity to answer before he intervenes.
