- S02, approximately 12 BC. The next day has begun in Rome after Aemilia and Lucius's private conversation in the report room. The pregnancy loss is more than two weeks in the past, exact day count loose. Aemilia is eighteen.
- The severe marital breach remains canon: Lucius twice physically stopped Aemilia leaving an argument and the second restraint broke his explicit promise not to do so again. Their voluntary adult intimacy and affectionate closeness resumed at the country villa and continued through the return to Rome.

- Pia remains missing. Evidence shows a woman strongly matching Pia was forcibly controlled, taken to a holding yard, transferred through Titus Fabius Naso, and then into the commercial network of **Quintus Numisius Faustus**, a Campanian dealer operating through Capua. Exact present location remains unknown. Aemilia's standing order remains immediate but **safe** recovery, with Lucius authorizing payment if fastest.
- OOC GM canon remains: Pia did not flee; Eros deliberately facilitated the initial interception to isolate Aemilia but did not intend/order onward sale and did not design the full chain. Exact mechanics and how much Eros now knows remain unestablished. Naso is not automatically connected directly to Eros. Pregnancy loss remains medically unresolved and unrelated to Eros.
- Naso is held in a functional/service area of the Valerian domus. He is visibly bruised/beaten; who struck him, when, and whether Lucius ordered it remain unestablished.
- Valerian men are pursuing Faustus's Roman contacts and southward route. Recovery remains first priority.
- Aemilia has asked whether selling property one does not own is a crime. Lucius explained that an unauthorized sale cannot extinguish the Valerian ownership claim, while Naso's personal culpability depends on what he knew and did.
- Aemilia follows in her soft voice: **“How would one ordinarily sell a person through a broker? Would proof of ownership be required?”**
- Lucius explains without false bureaucratic precision that Roman slave sales do not require a single universal ownership certificate checked by an official before every transaction. In ordinary commerce a broker may rely on the seller's identity, reputation, possession/control of the enslaved person, witnesses, written sale terms or mandates, and representations/warranties that the seller has authority to sell. More careful documentation is expected where an agent sells for an absent owner or where circumstances are irregular.
- Lucius emphasizes that this does not make mere possession equivalent to lawful title. A broker dealing with unknown men who produce a controlled woman, claim authority for an unnamed or unverified owner, and seek rapid onward disposal has reason to establish who authorizes the sale. Failure to do so may not by itself prove knowing theft, but it weakens Naso's claim that he behaved as a wholly ordinary innocent intermediary.
- Pressed by the distinction, Naso admits that he did **not** receive a formal written mandate naming the supposed owner of Pia. He relied on the holding-yard channel, the two men's assertion of authority, and the commercial fact that the woman was physically under their control. This is now established.
- Naso continues to insist he did not know Pia belonged to the Valerian household. That remains a claim, not proven truth.
- This creates a new investigative question: who vouched for or introduced the two men strongly enough that Naso and the yard accepted their asserted authority without a named-owner mandate? That link may eventually lead backward toward the original interception, but no connection to Eros is yet known or established in evidence.
- Eros remains in Rome with former privileged access ended. Aemilia's one-month trial prohibiting punitive beatings continues; full-month assessment not yet formally presented.
- Exact sex/appearance and handling of the lost child remain unestablished. Publius/Aufidius creditor deadline outcome remains unestablished.
