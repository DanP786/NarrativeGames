# Current scene

- S01, approximately 12 BC. It is later in the morning after Aemilia's eighteenth birthday at the Valerian domus in Rome.
- Earlier Aemilia encountered Eros and learned that Eros' two-year-old son Felix had been feverish during the night, though somewhat improved by morning. Aemilia relieved Eros of her duties for the day so she could remain with her son. Felix's outcome remains unresolved.
- Aemilia has joined Lucius and the Greek physician Demetrios in a private consultation. Lucius explains that Demetrios has been urging senatorial elites to adopt cleaner and healthier domestic habits intended to reduce sickness, but his advice has largely been ignored. Lucius intends to listen and has included Aemilia because implementation would intersect strongly with her household authority.
- Demetrios' approach is empirical but historically bounded: he has no germ theory or modern microbiology. He reasons through contemporary ideas of regimen, environment, corrupted air or water and bodily balance, while observing that cleaner water and vessels, waste removal, washing, clean linens and dressings, food storage, ventilation and reduced crowding around some illnesses can sometimes improve health.
- Aemilia asks whether these measures would also affect the health of children. Demetrios answers that children are among those he is most concerned about: in his observation they are less able than healthy adults to withstand severe fluxes, fevers and other illnesses, so reducing avoidable exposure to foul water, spoiled food, waste and the immediate surroundings of sickness may preserve some children who would otherwise become ill. He carefully does not claim that cleanliness can prevent every childhood illness or death.
- Demetrios still does not know, unless told, about Felix's current fever or the private reproductive history of Aemilia and Lucius.
- This is the first day of Aemilia and Lucius' adult marital phase. Lucius' fidelity promise remains active, and they intend to try for legitimate children together, but no pregnancy or reproductive outcome is assumed.
