# Titus Fabius Naso

## Description
Roman freedman operating in Rome as a commercial intermediary in the sale, transport and placement of enslaved laborers. His exact age, former owner and family circumstances are not yet established.

## Status
Alive and held in a functional/service area of the Valerian domus rather than the family's private rooms. Valerian searchers found him through one of his regular business contacts and secured a small bundle of business tablets and memoranda.

Naso is visibly injured when Aemilia first sees him: facial bruising/swelling and other non-graphic signs show he has been beaten or roughly handled. It remains unestablished who struck him, precisely when, whether Lucius ordered it, or whether the violence occurred during capture/transport or after arrival.

## Role in Pia's disappearance
The holding yard identifies Naso as the intermediary who arranged the onward transfer of a woman strongly matching Pia. Naso now confirms arranging such a transaction but says he received her through the yard as an apparently ordinary lawful transfer and did not know she belonged to the Valerian household.

He says two men connected with the delivery represented that there was lawful authority to dispose of the woman. He did not personally know the owner they purported to represent. Their identities remain unestablished.

Naso says he passed the woman onward to the agent of a Campanian dealer who was assembling enslaved laborers for movement south from Rome. The receiving agent's personal identity and exact destination remain unestablished, but Naso says the relevant commercial mark/reference is in his seized records.

Aemilia finds a terse tablet entry consistent with this account: an unnamed female household slave received through the relevant holding-yard channel and included in a grouped onward transaction. An abbreviated mark/reference identifies the receiving agent commercially but has not yet been decoded.

## Voice and manner
Bruised, wary and commercially precise. Naso attempts to protect himself by distinguishing narrowly between what he personally arranged and acts committed by others. He is not casually defiant before Lucius, but neither does he volunteer guilt beyond what records or questioning force him to acknowledge.

## Knowledge
Naso knows the commercial step he arranged and can identify records and business contacts connected with the receiving agent. He does not automatically know the full chain behind Pia's interception. He is not presently established as knowing Pia's Valerian ownership when he handled the transfer.

## Relationship to Aemilia
No prior personal relationship. He now knows she is the domina connected to the missing woman whose transfer he brokered. Aemilia immediately turns to his records while Lucius questions him.

## GM constraints
- Do not retroactively make Naso the architect of the original interception without evidence.
- Do not establish a direct Naso–Eros link merely for convenience.
- Use his business records and testimony to move the investigation one plausible link at a time.
- Pia's exact current location remains unknown.
- Do not assume Lucius inflicted or ordered Naso's beating until play establishes it.
- Next investigation step: identify/decode the receiving agent's commercial mark, determine whether the southbound group has left Rome, and establish its destination.
