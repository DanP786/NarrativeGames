# Gaius Vibius Rufus

## Description
Gaius Vibius Rufus, approximately twenty-two, is a wealthy young Roman equestrian from a prosperous but non-senatorial family. His family possesses money and standing without the ancestral magistracies or senatorial pedigree of the Valerii Messallae. No specific physical appearance has yet been established in canon; incidental details from generated group images are not binding unless established in play.

## Position and resources
Rufus is rapidly increasing his wealth through a combination of Italian landed property, contracts, credit relationships and commercial connections handled within the respectable conventions available to an equestrian household. He has become a useful practical contact within Lucius Valerius Messalla's wider business and patronage network.

## Ambition and information network
In Aemilia's judgment, Rufus is not primarily ambitious for political office. His deeper ambition is information. She believes a meaningful portion of his growing wealth is spent obtaining access, reports, correspondence, introductions and useful knowledge from commercial, estate and elite networks. This helps explain his usefulness to Lucius.

## Voice and mannerisms
Direct play establishes dry confidence and restrained humor. Rufus admits ignorance rather than bluffing, asks targeted questions, and thinks in terms of sources, incentives, independent reports and what people are willing to risk or spend. He can become competitively interested in a difficult problem without belittling the person who posed it.

## Traits
- Financially ambitious
- Information-seeking
- Practically competent
- Socially rising
- Useful to senatorial contacts
- Kind and perceptive in Aemilia's judgment
- Not controlling, but reluctant to concede once persuaded of a position

## Relationship to player
Aemilia has known Rufus for approximately four years through Lucius's business and patronage affairs, first meeting him when she was about fourteen and he about eighteen. Lucius gently encouraged conversation between them when Aemilia was shy. There is no established romantic history. Aemilia knows Rufus well enough socially to suggest him privately to Terentia as a possible husband for Valeria Tertia and gives a favorable personal assessment of his kindness, intelligence and attentiveness.

## Relationship to Lucius
A useful contact rather than a social equal in pedigree. Lucius values Rufus's growing resources, practical network and appetite for information. Lucius has agreed to the exploratory possibility of Rufus as a match for Valeria Tertia, provided Valeria does not dislike him, Terentia does not find him unsuitable, and Rufus does not treat Valeria merely as a route into the Valerii.

## Reported private reputation
Aemilia tells Terentia she has heard that Rufus has never attended a brothel and only occasionally attends public bathhouses. This remains secondhand reputation reported by Aemilia, not independently established fact, and is not treated as proof of virtue.

## Marriage significance
A match with Valeria Tertia would be unconventional for the Valerian Messalla family because Rufus comes from the equestrian rather than senatorial order. His growing fortune and information network could make the connection materially and strategically attractive, while Aemilia believes his personal character could benefit Valeria. His firmness could also make him difficult when disagreements arise. Marcus Valerius Messalla would have strong status and dynastic reasons to scrutinize or resist the match.

## S01 meeting with Valeria Tertia
Rufus attended Lucius and Aemilia's domus believing himself present for legitimate business, unaware that Aemilia and Terentia were also assessing him as a possible husband for Valeria. He did not immediately single Valeria out. When Valeria challenged him to locate an unidentified Greek poem about Atalanta remembered from her tutor, he admitted he did not know it, asked for fragments and context, and treated her as a useful source rather than a child to indulge. He said that if he found a purchasable copy he would tell her the cost before buying it because the choice of its worth should be hers. The poem remains unresolved. At supper he remained socially attentive without conspicuously pursuing Valeria.
