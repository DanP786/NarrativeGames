# Demetrios

## Description
Adult Greek physician received privately by Lucius Valerius Messalla at the Valerian domus in Rome. His exact age, origin within the Greek-speaking world, status and professional history remain to emerge in play.

## Role
Physician consulted by Lucius on the first morning of the adult phase of Lucius and Aemilia's marriage. Demetrios has been trying to persuade men connected with the Senate and elite Roman households to adopt cleaner, healthier domestic habits intended to reduce sickness. His advice has largely been ignored or dismissed by senators, prompting Lucius to invite him privately to explain it for possible use in the Valerian household.

Lucius invited Aemilia to attend because many of the practical household measures Demetrios proposes fall within or intersect with her authority over domestic management. Aemilia subsequently spends the remainder of the day accompanying him through a detailed inspection of the domus.

## Voice and manner
Educated and professionally self-possessed. Speaks Greek fluently and can conduct himself appropriately in an elite Roman household. He distinguishes observation and practical experience from certainty: he does not possess germ theory or modern microbiology, but has formed empirical views about foul or stagnant water, spoiled food, waste, dirty dressings and linens, ventilation, washing and the separation of some sick persons from crowded healthy quarters.

## Current medical ideas
Demetrios argues from the medical concepts available to his era, including regimen, environment, corrupted air or water and bodily balance. He believes illness can sometimes be reduced by practical household measures even where its ultimate cause is uncertain. His recommendations include cleaner water vessels, attention to water sources and stagnation, prompt disposal of waste, washing hands and implements after tending wounds or the sick, laundering or changing soiled linens, keeping kitchens and food stores clean, avoiding spoiled food, ventilation where practicable, and reducing unnecessary crowding around some illnesses. He also advocates particular cleanliness around childbirth while making no claim that it can eliminate pregnancy or childbirth risks. These are empirical practices, not modern scientific knowledge, and their effectiveness varies by disease.

## Knowledge
Lucius has heard enough of Demetrios' unsuccessful efforts among senators to consider the physician worth hearing privately. Over the course of his first day's inspection Demetrios gains practical knowledge of the Valerian domus' water handling, kitchens and food storage, waste arrangements, laundry and bedding, servants' quarters and ordinary practices around illness. He has not been told the private reproductive history of Lucius and Aemilia. Whether he learns of Felix's current fever remains unresolved unless it arises directly in play.

## Relationship to Aemilia
New acquaintance, now marked by a full day of serious practical collaboration. Aemilia listens closely and personally accompanies his inspection rather than delegating it. By evening she insists that Demetrios accept the household's hospitality for food and a bed for the night so their work need not end abruptly. He recognizes that she is taking his recommendations seriously and that implementation rests substantially with her.
