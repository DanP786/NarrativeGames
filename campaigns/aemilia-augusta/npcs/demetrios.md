# Demetrios

## Description
Adult Greek physician received privately by Lucius Valerius Messalla at the Valerian domus in Rome. His exact age, origin within the Greek-speaking world, status and professional history remain to emerge in play.

## Role
Physician consulted by Lucius on the first morning of the adult phase of Lucius and Aemilia's marriage. Demetrios has been trying to persuade men connected with the Senate and elite Roman households to adopt cleaner, healthier domestic habits intended to reduce sickness. His advice has largely been ignored or dismissed by senators, prompting Lucius to invite him privately to explain it for possible use in the Valerian household.

Lucius has invited Aemilia to attend because many of the practical household measures Demetrios proposes would fall within or intersect with her authority over domestic management.

## Voice and manner
Educated and professionally self-possessed. Speaks Greek fluently and can conduct himself appropriately in an elite Roman household. He distinguishes observation and practical experience from certainty: he does not possess germ theory or modern microbiology, but has formed empirical views about foul or stagnant water, spoiled food, waste, dirty dressings and linens, ventilation, washing and the separation of some sick persons from crowded healthy quarters.

## Current medical ideas
Demetrios argues from the medical concepts available to his era, including regimen, environment, bodily balance and corrupted air or water. He believes illness can sometimes be reduced by practical household measures even where its ultimate cause is uncertain. His recommendations may include cleaner water vessels, attention to water sources and stagnation, prompt disposal of waste, washing hands and implements after tending wounds or the sick, laundering or changing soiled linens, keeping kitchens and food stores clean, avoiding spoiled food, ventilation where practicable, and reducing unnecessary crowding around some illnesses. These are empirical practices, not modern scientific knowledge, and their effectiveness varies by disease.

## Knowledge
Lucius has heard enough of Demetrios' unsuccessful efforts among senators to consider the physician worth hearing privately. Demetrios has not yet established what, if anything, he knows about Felix's current fever or the private reproductive history of Lucius and Aemilia.

## Relationship to Aemilia
New acquaintance. No established personal relationship. Lucius has introduced her explicitly as his wife and invited her to hear Demetrios' household advice directly.
