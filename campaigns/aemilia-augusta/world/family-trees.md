# Family trees and offices

Canonical family structure as of approximately 12 BC. Ages are approximate where noted; these are fictional branches of historical Roman gentes rather than identifications with specific attested historical individuals.

## Aemilia's natal family — Aemilii

### Parents
- **Marcus Aemilius Lepidus**, 49 — Aemilia's father. Senator of an old patrician Aemilian branch. Married once and still married to Aemilia's mother. Former quaestor and praetor; deliberately never reached the consulship. He now concentrates on Senate business, patronage, litigation affecting family property, and management of substantial Italian estates through freedmen and agents. Conservative about daughters' education and female public visibility.
- **Cornelia**, 43 — Aemilia's mother, born to a wealthy Cornelian senatorial house. Alive. First and only wife of Marcus Aemilius Lepidus; the marriage remains intact. Experienced elite matron responsible for the domestic and social management of her husband's household, kinship obligations, marriages and religious observances. Aemilia is her biological daughter.

### Children of Marcus Aemilius Lepidus and Cornelia
1. **Marcus Aemilius Lepidus Minor**, 27 — eldest child and principal male heir. Full brother of Aemilia. Senator; has completed the quaestorship and is building toward the praetorship. Married to **Claudia Tertia**, 22. Their children are **Marcus**, 4, and **Aemilia Lepida**, 2.
2. **Aemilia Prima**, 24 — Aemilia's elder full sister. Married to **Gaius Junius Silanus**, 34, senator. Their children are **Junia**, 8, **Gaius Junius**, 6, and **Marcus Junius**, 3.
3. **Publius Aemilius Lepidus**, 21 — Aemilia's younger full brother. At the beginning of a senatorial career and unmarried.
4. **Aemilia**, 18 — youngest surviving child. Wife of Lucius Valerius Messalla; approximately three months pregnant with their first legitimate child.

There are no living half-siblings in Aemilia's immediate natal household. Marcus and Cornelia have remained married throughout their children's lives. Two additional children died very young and are not counted among the living sibling group.

## Lucius' natal family — Valerii Messallae

### Parents
- **Marcus Valerius Messalla**, 58 — Lucius' father, senior Valerian aristocrat and paterfamilias of this branch. Married twice. Senator and former consul; subsequently held a provincial proconsular command and remains influential. Bluntly dynastic and socially conservative.
- **Claudia Pulchra**, died aged 32 — Marcus Valerius Messalla's first wife and Lucius' biological mother. She died when Lucius was about ten, after bearing three surviving children and suffering additional unsuccessful pregnancies.
- **Terentia**, 45 — Marcus Valerius Messalla's second and current wife, married to him for roughly seventeen years. Alive. From a wealthy senatorial Terentian family. She is Lucius' stepmother, not his biological mother. She has two surviving children by Marcus and presides over the elder Messalla household as its current matron.

### Children of Marcus Valerius Messalla and Claudia Pulchra — full siblings
1. **Marcus Valerius Messalla Minor**, 35 — Lucius' elder full brother. Senator and former praetor. Married to **Servilia**, 30. Their children are **Marcus**, 10, **Valeria**, 8, and **Lucius**, 5.
2. **Valeria Messalla**, 33 — Lucius' elder full sister. Married to **Quintus Fabius Maximus**, 42. Their children are **Fabia**, 14, **Quintus Fabius**, 11, and **Lucius Fabius**, 7.
3. **Lucius Valerius Messalla**, approximately 30 — Aemilia's husband. Senator and former quaestor, now occupied with Senate business, legal advocacy, patronage, estate interests and positioning for the praetorship. Married to Aemilia, 18. Aemilia is approximately three months pregnant with their first legitimate child; no living legitimate child has yet been born.

### Children of Marcus Valerius Messalla and Terentia — Lucius' half-siblings
4. **Decimus Valerius Messalla**, 16 — Lucius' younger paternal half-brother. Still under his father's authority and education. Unmarried.
5. **Valeria Tertia**, 13 — Lucius' younger paternal half-sister. Educated conventionally for an elite girl and approaching marriage age. Unmarried and has no children. Her parents are considering prospects; Aemilia and Terentia are discreetly assessing Gaius Vibius Rufus without Valeria being told a match is under consideration.

## Lucius' children outside his marriage
During the years in which Lucius refused to attempt conception with Aemilia while she was a child, he fathered seven babies by adult enslaved women; four died in infancy or early childhood. Three survive and were born enslaved. They are biologically Lucius' children but are not legitimate members of the dynastic line of his marriage to Aemilia:
- **Hilarus**, approximately 6 — son of Daphne.
- **Fortunata**, approximately 4 — daughter of Eros.
- **Felix**, approximately 2 — son of Eros.

## Political and economic character of the two houses
The **Aemilian branch** is old, wealthy and senatorial but currently more substantial than dazzling: land, inherited name, Senate membership, legal advocacy and carefully maintained alliances matter more than spectacular office. Marcus Aemilius Lepidus has not reached the consulship; his eldest son is expected to improve the branch's immediate political standing.

The **Valerian Messalla branch** is the more immediately powerful political house. Marcus Valerius Messalla is a former consul and provincial governor, his eldest son Marcus has reached the praetorship, and Lucius is progressing through the senatorial cursus. This disparity helps explain why Aemilia's very substantial dowry and Aemilian blood were valuable to the match while the Valerian marriage offered her natal family strong political connection.

## Naming note
Roman elite naming is repetitive by design. Women are commonly identified by feminine forms of their gens names and distinguishing cognomina or ordinal/familial usage where necessary. The repeated Marcus, Lucius, Aemilia and Valeria names in this tree are intentional rather than accidental.
