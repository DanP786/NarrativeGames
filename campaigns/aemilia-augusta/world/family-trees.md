# Family trees and offices

Canonical family structure as of approximately 12 BC. Ages are approximate where noted; these are fictional branches of historical Roman gentes rather than identifications with specific attested historical individuals.

## Aemilia's natal family — Aemilii

### Parents
- **Marcus Aemilius Lepidus**, 49 — Aemilia's father. Senator of an old patrician Aemilian branch. Married once and still married to Aemilia's mother. Former quaestor and praetor; deliberately never reached the consulship. He now concentrates on Senate business, patronage, litigation affecting family property, and management of substantial Italian estates through freedmen and agents. Conservative about daughters' education and female public visibility, though not personally impoverished or politically marginal.
- **Cornelia**, 43 — Aemilia's mother, born to a wealthy Cornelian senatorial house. Alive. First and only wife of Marcus Aemilius Lepidus; the marriage remains intact. Experienced elite matron responsible for the domestic and social management of her husband's household, kinship obligations, marriages and religious observances. Aemilia is her biological daughter.

### Children of Marcus Aemilius Lepidus and Cornelia
1. **Marcus Aemilius Lepidus Minor**, 27 — eldest child and principal male heir. Full brother of Aemilia. Senator; has completed the quaestorship and is building toward the praetorship through Senate attendance, patronage and legal advocacy. Married to **Claudia Tertia**, 22, daughter of a Claudian senatorial family. Their children are **Marcus**, 4, and **Aemilia Lepida**, 2. Both are Aemilia's nephew and niece.
2. **Aemilia Prima**, 24 — Aemilia's elder full sister. Married at fifteen to **Gaius Junius Silanus**, 34, senator from an old noble family. Gaius has served as quaestor and aedile and is presently pursuing the offices and alliances expected of a man aiming at the praetorship. Their children are **Junia**, 8, **Gaius Junius**, 6, and **Marcus Junius**, 3. All three are Aemilia's niece/nephews.
3. **Publius Aemilius Lepidus**, 21 — Aemilia's younger full brother. At the beginning of a senatorial career. Has completed military service as a junior officer/tribune attached to a legionary command and is now in Rome preparing for the vigintivirate/quaestorian stages of public life, studying law and cultivating patrons. Unmarried; his parents are actively considering a politically useful marriage.
4. **Aemilia**, 18 — youngest surviving child. Wife of Lucius Valerius Messalla.

There are no living half-siblings in Aemilia's immediate natal household. Marcus and Cornelia have remained married throughout their children's lives. Two additional children died very young and are not counted among the living sibling group.

## Lucius' natal family — Valerii Messallae

### Parents
- **Marcus Valerius Messalla**, 58 — Lucius' father, senior Valerian aristocrat and paterfamilias of this branch. Married twice. Senator and former consul; subsequently held a provincial proconsular command and remains influential through Senate seniority, clients, old military relationships, landholding and family alliances. He no longer needs a formal annual magistracy to exercise substantial political weight. Bluntly dynastic and socially conservative.
- **Claudia Pulchra**, died aged 32 — Marcus Valerius Messalla's first wife and Lucius' biological mother, from a patrician Claudian family. She died when Lucius was about ten, after bearing three surviving children and suffering additional unsuccessful pregnancies.
- **Terentia**, 45 — Marcus Valerius Messalla's second and current wife, married to him for roughly seventeen years. Alive. From a wealthy senatorial Terentian family. She is Lucius' stepmother, not his mother. The marriage remains intact. She has two surviving children by Marcus and presides over the elder Messalla household as its current matron.

### Children of Marcus Valerius Messalla and Claudia Pulchra — full siblings
1. **Marcus Valerius Messalla Minor**, 35 — Lucius' elder full brother. Senator and former praetor, currently one of the family's most active public political figures; handles Senate alliances, judicial business and patronage and expects eventually to compete for the consulship. Married to **Servilia**, 30, of a senatorial Servilian family. Their children are **Marcus**, 10, **Valeria**, 8, and **Lucius**, 5. They are Lucius' nephews and niece.
2. **Valeria Messalla**, 33 — Lucius' elder full sister. Married to **Quintus Fabius Maximus**, 42, a senator who has held the praetorship and has served in provincial administration. Their children are **Fabia**, 14, **Quintus Fabius**, 11, and **Lucius Fabius**, 7. They are Lucius' niece and nephews.
3. **Lucius Valerius Messalla**, approximately 30 — Aemilia's husband. Senator. Earlier completed the junior military service expected of an elite Roman man and has served as quaestor; he is now at the stage of his career where Senate business, legal advocacy, patronage, estate interests and positioning for the praetorship occupy him. He is not a salaried professional in the modern sense: his public career is the cursus honorum of a wealthy senator, supported by landed wealth, investments, credit relationships, clients and household administration. Married to Aemilia, 18. They currently have no established legitimate living child; Aemilia has just missed a menstrual cycle and believes she may be pregnant, but this is not yet confirmed.

### Children of Marcus Valerius Messalla and Terentia — Lucius' half-siblings
4. **Decimus Valerius Messalla**, 16 — Lucius' younger paternal half-brother. Still under his father's authority and education. Studying rhetoric, Greek, law and public affairs in preparation for military service and an eventual senatorial career. Unmarried.
5. **Valeria Tertia**, 13 — Lucius' younger paternal half-sister. Educated conventionally for an elite girl and approaching the age at which her parents are negotiating a marriage. Unmarried and has no children.

## Lucius' children outside his marriage
During the years in which Lucius refused to attempt conception with Aemilia while she was a child, he fathered seven babies by adult enslaved women in his household; four died in infancy or early childhood. Three survive and were born enslaved. They are biologically Lucius' children but are not legitimate members of the dynastic line of his marriage to Aemilia:
- **Hilarus**, approximately 6 — son of Daphne.
- **Fortunata**, approximately 4 — daughter of Eros.
- **Felix**, approximately 2 — son of Eros.

## Political and economic character of the two houses
The **Aemilian branch** is old, wealthy and senatorial but currently more substantial than dazzling: land, inherited name, Senate membership, legal advocacy and carefully maintained alliances matter more than spectacular office. Marcus Aemilius Lepidus has not reached the consulship; his eldest son is expected to improve the branch's immediate political standing.

The **Valerian Messalla branch** is the more immediately powerful political house. Marcus Valerius Messalla is a former consul and provincial governor, his eldest son Marcus has reached the praetorship, and Lucius is progressing through the senatorial cursus. This disparity helps explain why Aemilia's very substantial dowry and Aemilian blood were valuable to the match while the Valerian marriage offered her natal family strong political connection.

## Naming note
Roman elite naming is repetitive by design. Women are commonly identified by feminine forms of their gens names and distinguishing cognomina or ordinal/familial usage where necessary. The repeated Marcus, Lucius, Aemilia and Valeria names in this tree are therefore intentional rather than accidental.
