# Narrative

Aemilia enters the household of the senatorial Lucius Valerius Messalla at twelve in 18 BC. Lucius preserves the formal hierarchy of a Roman elite marriage but refuses to consummate it while she is a child and spends nearly six years educating her far beyond what her father intended. Through letters, accounts, practical law, patronage, politics and household management, Aemilia grows into an observant young domina whose private judgment Lucius increasingly seeks. She also turns the Greek physician Demetrios's practical recommendations on water, food, waste, washing and illness into durable household routines.

By eighteen Aemilia loves Lucius and regards the Valerian domus as her home. Before their marriage enters its adult phase, she asks him for fidelity; Lucius promises that from her eighteenth birthday onward he will seek no sexual access to Eros or any other woman. Their adult married life becomes frequent and affectionate, and within several months Aemilia becomes pregnant. Aemilia also learns that during the years Lucius refused to involve her sexually as a child, he used his ownership power to summon adult enslaved women including Eros and Daphne. The history is not reframed as freely consensual. Aemilia distinguishes those years from the fidelity she expects in their adult marriage, and Lucius's promise remains binding.

Aemilia's influence extends beyond her own household. She proposes the wealthy young equestrian Gaius Vibius Rufus as a possible husband for Lucius's thirteen-year-old half-sister Valeria Tertia and arranges, with Lucius and Terentia's understanding, a deniable family-and-business encounter at the domus. Valeria and Rufus are not told of the matchmaking purpose. Terentia's first assessment is favorable but cautious, while Rufus's non-senatorial status remains a serious dynastic obstacle. By the end of Session 02 Marcus is investigating Rufus's family standing, property, debts, reputation and prospects while Terentia scrutinizes whether his interest would be in Valeria herself or in marrying into the Valerii. No engagement exists and Valeria remains unaware.

Session 02 breaks the earlier domestic confidence. After Eros reports a private conversation with Aemilia to Lucius and characterizes Aemilia as sheltered and likely to yield to him, Aemilia and Lucius quarrel severely. Aemilia tries to leave; Lucius physically stops her twice, the second time after explicitly promising he would not do so again. He later admits, **“I was wrong.”** That night Aemilia develops severe pain and bleeding and loses the approximately four-month pregnancy despite attendance by Demetrios and a trusted midwife. She suffers major blood loss and remains unconscious for roughly a day. No cause for the miscarriage is established, and the argument or restraint is not declared causal.

The loss leaves Aemilia grieving, physically weak and markedly changed in her household manner. Cornelia stays with her for several days, but Aemilia eventually sends her mother home and withdraws again. When she returns to work she remains soft-spoken but no longer cushions commands with her former warmth. Terentia later learns the substance of the marital breach and advises Aemilia that mercy toward Eros and the children need not mean restored confidence, and that Lucius remains responsible for his own choices. The marriage is not reconciled: Lucius has not yet reopened the core dispute, while Aemilia has stopped trying to explain it repeatedly.

At the same time Pia, Aemilia's longtime enslaved personal attendant, fails to return from a genuine day of leave and remains missing for more than a week without confirmed word. On Aemilia's second day back at work she personally asks the steward about Pia, then overexerts herself and becomes unable to climb a flight of steps. She calls for Lucius. He asks before lifting her; Aemilia rests her head against his chest while he carries her, and later grabs his hand when he begins to withdraw from the couch where he has settled her. The gesture marks renewed voluntary closeness without resolving the breach. Unknown to Aemilia, Eros has also privately sought Lucius's company in a manner recalling their former sexual relationship during the weeks of Aemilia's withdrawal; Lucius was tempted but refused sexual access and remained faithful to his promise.
