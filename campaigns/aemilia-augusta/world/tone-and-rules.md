# Tone and rules

- Voice: Third person, present tense.
- Prose style: Gritty historical realism — direct, physical, socially observant, modern-readable but not modern-minded. Sensory detail serves material reality rather than spectacle. Little romanticisation.
- Historical posture: Aim for historically grounded Augustan Roman law, custom, household structures, status and religion. Where evidence or practice is uncertain, do not manufacture false precision.
- Tone: Serious, intimate and socially unforgiving.
- Content posture: Nothing categorically excluded when historically and fictionally appropriate. Because Aemilia begins at age twelve, sexual activity involving her as a child is not depicted. Lucius' refusal to consummate the marriage while she is a child is established canon.
- Agency posture: Gritty realism. Failure and unintended consequences are real.
- Death rule: Hard death enabled.
- Mind-reading rule: None. Thoughts are private. Omens, dreams and divination do not create automatic telepathy.
- Scope: Personal household and marriage story capable of widening into dynastic, legal, social and imperial politics.
- Character-development rule: Aemilia has no predetermined secret adult personality. Her traits and outlook develop from her choices, experiences, education and relationships during play.
