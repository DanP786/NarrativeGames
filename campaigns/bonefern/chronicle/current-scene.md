# Current scene — Korren's lodge, Day 10 midday

- Mira wakes from her rest and stretches beneath the light fur; flexing her right foot wakes the pain beneath Senna's dressing again. Korren wakes with her and reminds her not to put weight on it yet.
- Korren raises the private thing he marked at council. Mira explains that Thaal asking how Korren had "made" her forced her to feel the difference between what the camp sees now and how little choice she had at the beginning. She tells Korren she chose the cave and chooses him now, but cannot make the beginning into a choice after the fact. Korren acknowledges the first taking was his decision, not hers.
- Mira says, "I don't want anything to change." Korren understands this as a present choice and takes her back into his arms deliberately. Their quiet intimacy follows; afterward Korren protects her injured foot.
- Senna comes to the lodge for the promised private conversation about Mira's neck. Mira grants it with a nod; Korren protects her privacy while she dresses, then remains outside.
- Mira explains that she infers the hidden implant translates language but does not know for sure; implanted technology itself is plausible where she comes from, but she did not knowingly choose this implant.
- Mira gives Senna her fullest account yet of the human mission: six women were workers aboard a sky transport seeking a habitable world; mission rules forbade settling a planet with intelligent life; trained first-landing personnel, not workers like Mira, should have landed; the women are frightened and unprepared for Vethaal.
- Mira explains the human contract system and realizes aloud that buried or misleading terms may have technically authorized procedures/reassignment/research without the women understanding what they agreed to. Senna rejects hidden meaning as meaningful choice in her own moral frame.
- Asked whether the contract can be recovered, Mira shakes her head: "No. We came with nothing." Senna recognizes that the absence is itself structurally important: the women arrived without tools, records, clothing, or any means to verify what was done to them. She does not treat that as proof of motive, but it removes the easiest way to test Mira's contract theory.
- Senna turns from the inaccessible contract to evidence physically present on Vethaal: the six women themselves, their memories, and the hidden implants. She proposes speaking to each woman separately about the last thing she remembers aboard the ship and examining the remaining women's necks, without telling them one another's answers first. She asks Mira whether all six women should be told what Senna has found about the implants now.
