# Session 01 — Arrival at Caer Ardyn

Lady Cerys of House Briarwyn, twenty-two and raised more as a huntress than a court ornament, arrived at Caer Ardyn astride her bay mare Bramble in practical travel clothes. She dismounted before a stool could be brought and met Lord Chancellor Oswin and King Aldren with confident courtesy. Aldren personally showed her part of the palace. They discovered a shared preference for woods and open country over overly ordered gardens, and Cerys was introduced warmly to Lady Merien, Mistress of the Household.

Cerys bathed and dressed for her first royal supper with the help of her close friends and ladies-in-waiting, Anwen and Eira. At supper she and Aldren developed an easy rapport, joking about raspberry versus blackberry jam. Cerys accidentally touched Aldren's arm while laughing and blushed; he protected her from embarrassment rather than teasing her. The court noticed how comfortable they seemed together.

The following morning Anwen and Eira shared court gossip: Lady Vivienne of Ravenscar had been an expected candidate to marry Aldren, the Duchess of Ravenscar disapproved of Cerys's unconventional presentation, and Aldren's cousin Lord Garrick Vayne was ambitious enough to merit watching. Cerys explored Caer Ardyn, watched Aldren spar in the training yard, and briefly spoke with Garrick when he arrived late. She later wandered through the falcon mews, kitchens, and orchards, becoming temporarily difficult for the household to locate. Aldren correctly guessed she would have sought somewhere green and open.

At a private lunch, Cerys and Aldren spoke candidly about their marriage. Aldren explained that seven potential brides had originally been considered, then three, and that although Lady Vivienne offered strong royal blood and political advantages, he personally asked negotiations to begin with House Briarwyn. He valued Briarwyn's honorable conduct during the Sundering and reports that suggested Cerys was practical, curious, compassionate, and not shaped solely to marry a king. Cerys admitted that she had initially accepted the marriage as duty, trusting her father not to sacrifice her purely for politics, but that in the last day she had learned more about Aldren the man than she had from years of stories about Aldren the king.

Aldren asked, despite their existing betrothal, whether Cerys would permit him to court her properly so they might build something personal within their political obligations. Cerys answered, "I would really like that." They agreed to ride together in the royal woods the following morning. Later that afternoon, Cerys returned toward her chambers to prepare for the evening, intending to tell Anwen and Eira what had happened.
