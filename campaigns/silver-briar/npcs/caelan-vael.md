# Sir Caelan Vael

## Role
Potential Arthurian-style rival love interest: a Lancelot-shaped pressure on Cerys and Aldren's developing marriage, but never a predetermined affair. Cerys retains complete agency over whether she feels friendship, attraction, irritation, loyalty, temptation, love, or nothing romantic at all.

## Description & voice
Age 27. A renowned knight from the lake-country marches of western Valedorn, currently away from Caer Ardyn on royal service. Tall, lean-powerful rather than bulky, black hair worn a little too long for court fashion, storm-grey eyes, and a pale old scar crossing one side of his ribs rather than his face. Striking rather than conventionally polished. He carries himself with the unconscious physical confidence of someone exceptionally good at dangerous things.

Caelan's voice is direct, low-key, and often irreverent. He is far less socially smooth than Garrick. When he wants something, subtlety is rarely his first instinct.

## Reputation
Among soldiers, Caelan is already becoming legendary: an extraordinary horseman and swordsman, personally brave to the point of recklessness, and notorious for going back for wounded comrades when sensible men would withdraw. He fought for Aldren late in the Sundering after initially serving an independent western lord. Aldren spared that lord after surrender; Caelan subsequently swore himself personally to Aldren and has become one of the strongest candidates for eventual leadership among the Companions of the Silver Hart.

At court his reputation is more mixed. He is admired, desired, and considered dangerous company. He has little patience for status games and has previously had discreet affairs with unmarried or widowed women of the nobility. He does not pretend chastity he does not possess.

## Traits
- Intensely loyal once loyalty is earned.
- Courageous, competitive, physically confident.
- Emotionally direct and persistent.
- Impatient with hypocrisy.
- Capable of tenderness but dislikes appearing vulnerable.
- Possessive streak when deeply attached.
- Poor at accepting emotional defeat gracefully.
- Believes wanting something strongly is a reason to pursue it, not a reason to retreat.

## Short-term goals
- Complete his current royal mission and return to Caer Ardyn.
- Earn a permanent place among Aldren's closest Companions.
- Prove western knights can be loyal to the reunited Crown without becoming tame courtiers.

## Long-term goals
- Become the finest knight in Valedorn and have that achievement mean something beyond tournaments.
- Protect Aldren, whose mercy fundamentally changed the course of Caelan's life.
- Find something personally worth belonging to after years in which war defined him.

## Romance posture toward Cerys
Caelan has not met Cerys and therefore has no present romantic feelings for her. Attraction must arise from actual interaction, not destiny or authorial decree.

If genuine attraction develops, however, **Caelan will be the pursuer**. Cerys does not need to initiate or seek an affair for the triangle to become active. He is capable of pursuing her hard: seeking private conversations, engineering reasons to ride or train near her, complimenting her directly, challenging emotional distance, admitting desire rather than hiding behind courtly euphemism, and continuing to make his interest clear even when doing so is personally inconvenient.

Persistence is not permission. A clear boundary from Cerys is canonically meaningful. Caelan may struggle emotionally with rejection, may remain attracted, may argue once when he believes she is denying feelings she has actually expressed, and may need distance afterward; he does not override an explicit refusal, force physical intimacy, threaten her for affection, or treat betrothal/marriage as evidence that her own wishes do not matter. 'Pushes hard' means active romantic pressure and temptation, not coercion.

He is also not required to respect Aldren's claim simply because Aldren is king. If Caelan comes to believe Cerys genuinely loves him, his loyalty to Aldren and desire for Cerys can enter direct conflict. That conflict may make him reckless, jealous, self-sacrificing, or politically dangerous.

## What may draw Caelan toward Cerys
- Courage without theatricality.
- Her comfort on horseback and in wild country.
- Refusing to be ornamental merely because court expects it.
- Dry humor and willingness to answer him back.
- Compassion that is practical rather than performative.
- Seeing vulnerability in him without treating him as weak.

## What may repel or sour him
- Deliberate cruelty toward subordinates.
- Cowardice disguised as prudence.
- Manipulating his attraction merely for influence.
- Treating him as socially inferior while privately seeking his attention.
- Betraying someone and then refusing responsibility for the choice.

## Methods & lines
Will: disobey a cautious order to rescue someone; fight duels; challenge rank when conscience demands it; pursue a desired relationship openly once he believes the feeling is real; risk reputation for someone he loves.
Won't: deliberately harm Cerys to control her; knowingly use physical force to obtain romance or sex; casually betray Aldren for political gain; court Cerys solely because she is the future queen.
Last resort: if torn between Aldren and a Cerys who genuinely reciprocates his love, Caelan may choose personal loyalty over political duty and accept the consequences rather than quietly surrender what he believes is mutual.

## Relationship with Aldren
Public tag: Loyal. Caelan owes Aldren his life indirectly and his future directly. He admires Aldren's restraint because Caelan himself does not naturally possess it. Their eventual friendship should be genuine; this is essential to making any later romantic conflict painful rather than merely convenient.

## Relationship with Cerys
Public tag: Unmet. No disposition exists yet beyond whatever preconceptions he acquires before meeting her.

## Knowledge
Currently knows that Aldren is betrothed to Lady Cerys of House Briarwyn and the broad political significance of the match. He has not met her and does not know about their private courtship, their lunch conversation, or Cerys's feelings.

## GM trajectory note
Do not rush his arrival merely to create romance. Allow Cerys and Aldren to establish real attachment first. When Caelan enters, give him independent plot relevance and a genuine bond with Aldren before any triangle becomes dominant. Attraction, if it occurs, should emerge from scenes. If Cerys shows no reciprocal interest, Caelan remains a consequential knight and character rather than becoming a mandatory romantic antagonist.
