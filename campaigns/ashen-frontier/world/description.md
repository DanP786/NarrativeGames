# World — The Ashen Frontier
312 years ago the Ashfall destroyed the old realms. Surviving human polities shelter behind enormous Bastions powered by ward-furnaces, defended by the Wardens. Blackthorn Bastion guards the northern marches; its surrounding dependent farms, quarries and road stations live under military law during alerts. Outside lies the Frontier: ruined cities, forests with ash-choked roots, derelict forts, contaminated waterways and the Blight's altered creatures. Warden companies include trained professionals, conscripts, aristocrats, pardoned offenders and Ember-bearers; class and rank remain sources of friction. Blackthorn's command can requisition rare Ember talents for high-risk missions.

## Warden families, pregnancy and healing — established OOC canon
The Wardens are a mixed military order of volunteers, disgraced nobles, criminals and pardoned offenders, alongside conscripts and career soldiers. Pregnancy does NOT expel a Warden from the order or automatically strip earned rank. A pregnant Warden's role changes according to health, stage of pregnancy, capabilities and garrison needs: typically reduced exposure to frontier patrols and combat, then reassignment to watch administration, stores, instruction, intelligence, records, repairs or other garrison work as appropriate. A healer or midwife attends pregnancy and childbirth; afterward, return to more strenuous duties depends on recovery and practical arrangements. There is no modern standardized maternity-leave system. Command discretion, resources, class, prejudice and the dangers of the frontier can make treatment uneven. Marriage is not a universal prerequisite for continued service or parenthood. Families and children may live within or around Bastion settlements subject to local circumstances; do not assume a specific household allocation without establishing it in play.

Herbalism is genuine and respected medicine in this medieval-fantasy setting, not automatically ineffective folk practice. Duskthorn-root tea is an established herbal contraceptive used by Isolde for years, including during her past relationship with Cassian and her relationship with Garran; do not retroactively declare it useless or invent a prior pregnancy. Its precise efficacy and individual effects remain uncertain rather than modern-statistical. Harris's concern about Isolde continuing duskthorn during neurological rehabilitation is specific to possible effects that complicate monitoring of dizziness, weakness or other symptoms, NOT a general rejection of herbs. The internal protective sheaths he supplied are an alternative requiring preparation and correct use, not automatically more effective; the choice is Isolde's, with advice and possible consequences. Isolde wants children with Garran someday (player-established private character intention); she has not necessarily told Garran and does not necessarily want pregnancy immediately. Pregnancy, if it arises, must not be predetermined or treated as automatic punishment for impulsiveness. Effects of pregnancy on Ember or Severance are not established; do not invent universal certainty.

## Magic and fixed truths (GM canon; reveal in play)
The Ember is an uncommon inborn affinity awakened under stress or maturation; each bearer manifests a constrained individual expression. Ember workings are not free: physiological debt accrues with intensity, and manifestations beyond a Bastion's ward line attract Blight-sensitive predators. Ward-furnaces burn treated emberstone to maintain a field that inhibits large-scale Blight cohesion, but their reserves are finite. Emberstone is mined from pre-Ashfall strata and can become unstable if contaminated. Severance perceives structural bindings of Blight-grown organisms as luminous seams; an actual blade strike along a seam can unmake that binding. It does not grant superhuman speed, armour or immunity. Fewer than twelve Severance wielders are documented; three are believed alive.

## Deep history — GM truth, not automatically NPC knowledge
The Ashfall was a failed attempt by the ancient Meridian Conclave to connect ward-furnaces across the continent into a single living lattice, drawing energy from a buried network of prehuman resonant roots called the Underweave. The catastrophic feedback made the Underweave imprint on organisms as Blight, and left pockets of coordinated awareness. The Underweave is not one omniscient mind: separate knots have different purposes and imperfect communication. Recent coordinated attacks are caused by a waking knot beneath ruined Greywatch Fortress responding to renewed deep emberstone mining near Blackthorn. It is assembling a path toward the Bastion's ward-furnace to quiet the damaging extraction; its methods are lethal and alien, not straightforward evil. The military does not know this. Senior quartermaster officials have quietly increased mining quotas under orders from the inland capital. Severance can disrupt Blight constructs but cannot alone solve the underlying system. Do not change these answers merely to suit later player speculation.

## Greywatch disaster — fixed past
Six weeks before opening, 32 Wardens entered a reconnaissance corridor near Greywatch Fortress. An unusually intelligent Blight ambush split them into two trapped groups. Isolde could reach and sever the bindings around only one group before a collapsing passage sealed the other. Her action directly saved nine. Seventeen of the original 32 returned in total; fifteen died. A formal inquiry ruled her choice tactically defensible. Among the dead was Sergeant Oren Kest, older brother of Warden Tamsin Kest, who survived in another part of the mission and blames Isolde. No evidence currently proves Isolde could have saved both groups. The dead and the surviving knowable witnesses are consistent facts, not a mystery to rewrite.
